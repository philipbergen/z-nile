### F1 [high] AC2's "the saucer fires at the nearer ship" passes with a raw distance that ignores the wrap, and with an aim taken from the higher score
Where: `acceptance/scores.spec.luau` :: "fires the saucer at the nearer ship on the field, aimed from that player's score"; Design, TwoPlayer step 5 and prep 5 ("the nearer ship on the field (the short way across the wrap) and aims from that player's score"); Rules SC8, SC9.
Evidence: mutant R2, in `TwoPlayer`'s `target`, `local d = math.sqrt((s.x - p.ship.x) ^ 2 + (s.y - p.ship.y) ^ 2)` instead of `Field.distance`: all five files `44 passed, 0 failed`. The homes are antipodal in x (256 and 768, 512 apart), so the raw and the wrapped distance pick the same ship in every case the spec sets up. My probe kills it: ships at (100, 384) and (700, 384), a small saucer still at (1000, 384), both scores 40,000. The short way picks player 1 (124 against 300), and the raw distance player 2: `expected 0, got 128`. Its mirror (ships at 324 and 924, saucer at 24: player 2 by the short way) fails it too: `expected 128, got 0`. Mutant R5, which aims from `math.max(pilots[1].score, pilots[2].score)` in place of the target's score: scores and match `24 passed` (the spec only fails when the target's own score is the high one). My probe kills it: target score 0, the other player's 40,000, saucer 150 below the target, 20 seeds, and at least one shot must miss the perfect heading.
Critique: saucers enter and leave at the left and right edges, so the wrap decides the target exactly where saucers spend their time. A wrong "nearer" is a wrong result in normal play, and check.sh passes it. The score source matters the same way: the race's leader would draw the small saucer's sharp aim onto the trailing player. The tempting fix is one case that moves one ship across the edge. That case alone still passes a target that is "always the lead" when the nearer ship by the short way is player 1 on tick 1.
Consequences: none beyond the cases: the rule is already the Design's, and the prototype passes both.
Recommendation: in that scores case (or one beside it, same AC2), add three sub-cases. (a) Ships at (100, 384) and (700, 384), saucer at (1000, 384): the shot's heading is `Field.heading(Field.delta(F, 1000, 384, 100, 384))`. (b) The mirror: ships at (324, 384) and (924, 384), saucer at (24, 384): the heading is toward player 2 by `Field.delta`. (c) Target score 0, other score 40,000, saucer at the target's x, 150 below it, seeds 1 to 20: at least one heading differs from `Field.heading(0, -150)`. Add rows SC21 "the nearer the short way across the wrap | mutant: raw distance" and SC22 "aimed from the target's own score | mutant: the higher of the two scores", and add both to `mutants.py`.

### F2 [medium] "The lead goes first at every per-player stage" is tested only on lead-1 ticks, or at no stage at all, for the clocks, the controls, the collisions and the same-point bounce
Where: Design, prep 6 ("The lead goes first at every per-player stage and wins every tie"), prep 8 ("Two ships on one point part along +x, player 1 to the left") and step 3 ("pilots 1 and 2 (in slot order, so the same-point rule is fixed)"); Rules SC13 to SC15, SC19, CO21; `contact.spec.luau` :: "separates ships that touch while moving apart" (its same-point sub-case runs only the first tick).
Evidence: each mutant below passes all five files (`44 passed, 0 failed`), and a probe case kills each one:
- R1: `bounce(m, a, b, events)` (the `order[1], order[2]` the prototype computes on the line above) in place of `bounce(m, m.pilots[1], m.pilots[2], events)`. Probe: both ships on (500, 500) on the second tick (lead 2): `expected 488 ± 1e-09, got 512` (player 2 went left).
- R3: collisions `for _, p in m.pilots` in place of `order`. Probe: on a lead-2 tick, ships at (500, 500) and (530, 500) with one small rock at (515, 500) touching both: `expected 2, got 1` (player 1 took the rock and the lost life).
- R6: controls in slot order. Probe: on a lead-2 tick both players jump; a twin match where only player 2 jumps gives player 2 the same spot under the Design: `expected 816, got 94`.
- R7: clocks in slot order. Probe: on a lead-2 tick both ships return from hyperspace; over seeds 1 to 60 player 2's roll matches a twin where only player 2 returns: `expected "alive", got "dead"`.
Critique: on tick 1 the lead and slot order are the same, so a case that runs only tick 1 can't tell them apart, and no case exercises the clocks, the controls or the collisions at all. The Design promises an alternating, fair lead at every stage and lists only three ties under Edge cases. The tempting fix is to run the existing cases on both leads. That still leaves the three stages with no case, and leaves "two ships ram one rock" as a tie the Design never names.
Consequences: the collision tie needs words. Going first in a ram means the lead takes the rock's points and loses a life, and the other ship then meets only what is left (a large rock's pieces may still reach it). That is the Design's rule as it stands, so no new rule, but it should be said under Edge cases so f0222's Observed criteria don't call it a bug.
Recommendation: (1) Run the same-point bounce sub-case for lead 1 and lead 2 (step once before placing the ships for lead 2), with player 1 at 488 both times; add row CO22 "the same-point rule on either lead | mutant: bounce in lead order". (2) Add to scores' lead case, each for lead 1 and lead 2: the shared small rock both ships overlap (the lead's `RockHit` and `ShipDestroyed`, the other untouched); a double jump checked against a one-jumper twin match (the lead's spot is the first draw); a double return from hyperspace over 60 seeds checked against a one-returner twin (the lead's roll is the first draw). Add rows SC23 to SC25 (collisions, controls, clocks in the lead's order | mutant: slot order), and add R1, R3, R6 and R7 to `mutants.py`. (3) Under Edge cases add "two ships ramming one rock on one tick: the lead's collision comes first (its points, its lost life); the other meets only what is left" and "two jumps or two returns from hyperspace on one tick: the lead draws first".

### F3 [medium] With friendly fire on, "the nearest target along the move wins" passes when a rock or the saucer anywhere on the move beats a nearer foe

### F4 [medium] A revive that restarts the wave passes: every revive case runs on wave 1, so `t.expect(m.wave).toBe(1)` can't fail
Where: Design, revive bullet ("Score, `nextExtra`, the wave and the other player stay") and prep 10; `survivor.spec.luau` :: "revives an out player with 3 new lives, keeping their score and next extra life" (`t.expect(m.wave).toBe(1)` on a match that never left wave 1) and "restarts a finished match with a revive".

Take `copy(m.pilots[2])` before `revive(m, 1)` and expect it unchanged after. ### F5 [low] A run or a match that ends through `leave` emits no event, unlike every other end, and the events table doesn't say so
Where: Design, the `leave` bullet, prep 11, prep 12 and the events table (`MatchOver` only from "the settle, both out").
Evidence: a probe on the prototype. Player 2 rams a rock on their last life (`RockHit,ShipDestroyed,GameOver`, match `playing`), then `leave(m, 1)` returns `true`, and the match is `gameover` and player 1 `gameover`. The next `step` returns `0` events. So player 1's run ends with no `GameOver`, and the match with no `MatchOver`. `survivor.spec.luau`'s leave case already pins zero events after the last leave, so this is the intended behaviour, but nothing in the Design says it.
Critique: f0222, which isn't designed yet, will read this events table to end a reserved server and to drive the HUD. A place that waits for `MatchOver` never sees one when the last playing player leaves while the other is out (for instance, waiting on f0223's revive offer).
Consequences: none if it is written down. Emitting an event from `leave` would change its signature and the pinned zero-events case, so don't.
Recommendation: add one sentence to the events section and the `leave` bullet: "`leave` and `revive` emit no event; the caller reads `m.phase` and the pilot's `phase` and `left` after the call (a match `leave` ends has no `MatchOver`)". Name it in the f0222 sentence under "The split and server authority".
