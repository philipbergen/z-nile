## Problem

I also want a new project for a landing portal where you can scroll between the games and see the highscores for each, the same cost model remains but it shoudl show 5 robux for each game witha slash through showing it is currently on sale for 1 robux. highscores shoudl show how many revives were used. The walls can be vertical and horizontal but do not connect. for asteroids we could have a two player version where each player is attacking the aliens from opposing sides. You can shoot each other's barriers. the aliens would then come in from the side and and split into two groups that move toward either player". Then: "Add a third game first person asteroids." Decided with the user (2026-10-31): the 2-player aliens game is pure Space Invaders (`invaders`); Asteroids is its own game (`asteroids`); one Roblox experience with the portal as start place, replacing pickman's lobby; every board shows revives used; one free game per hour per game.

The approved design is `asteroids/.tracker/game-design.md`, section **Game design: Asteroids (`asteroids/`)** (c0183). This issue owns these parts of it, as they are said there: **The field and view** ("The rules are 2-D") and **Rules (the classic)** (not the revive offer). The rest of it belongs to other issues (c0190's ownership table), listed under Non-goals.

What should happen:
- Solo Asteroids in pure Logic: the wrap-around field, the ship's turn, thrust and drag, up to four shots, rocks that split large → medium → small, both saucers, hyperspace with its risk, lives with an extra every 10,000, and the waves.

## Non-goals

## Solution
Six pure Logic features in `asteroids/src/shared/Logic/`: `Field` (the wrap-around field, its headings and a moving point's sweep against a circle), `Ship` (one ship's flight), `Rocks` (the three sizes and the split), `Saucers` (the two saucers, their waits, crossing and aim), `Pilot` (one player's ship and its life: on the field, in hyperspace or lost, protection, the controls and the shots it fires, lives, score and extra lives) and `Classic`, one player's run as plain data that is itself that player's pilot and composes the rest: the rocks, the saucer, the waves and the order of a tick. A run is stepped once per tick at 60 ticks a second with the player's input (`{ turn, thrust, fire, hyperspace }`) and returns the events of that tick; the field is 2-D in field units (`x` across, `y` up, 1,024 × 768). Every number is a frozen data table, and randomness is the run's own seeded generator. f0222's server adapter steps it and replicates it, f0223 makes the revive offer and adds the revive, and f0221 builds the two-player game from the same leaves, stepping each of its two pilots through `Pilot`'s functions.

## Design

### Product decisions this relies on
Quoted from `asteroids/.tracker/game-design.md`, **Game design: Asteroids (`asteroids/`)**:
- Pillar (2): "Deterministic Logic."
- The field and view: "The rules are 2-D: a flat, wrap-around field (leave one edge and you come back on the opposite edge), as in the arcade. Rocks tumble in 3-D for looks only." (The tumble is f0222's; Logic gives each rock an `id` the view can key it by.)
- Rules: "**The ship** turns at a fixed rate and thrusts with inertia and slight drag, up to a top speed."
- Rules: "**Shots:** up to 4 on the field at a time, each lasting about 1 s, and shots wrap round the field too."
- Rules: "**Hyperspace** puts the ship at a random spot, with a 1 in 6 chance of exploding on re-entry (a tuning constant)."
- Rules: "**Rocks:** large splits into 2 medium, medium into 2 small, and small is destroyed. They score 20, 50 and 100. Ramming one costs a life (the rock still splits)."
- Rules: "**Waves:** wave 1 has 4 large rocks, and each later wave adds 2, up to 11. Clearing every rock starts the next wave after 2 s."
- Rules: "**Saucers:** the large saucer (200) fires at random. The small saucer (1,000) aims at you, gets more accurate as the score rises, and appears more often in later waves. They cross the field edge to edge."
- Rules: "**Lives:** 3, with one extra every 10,000 points. After a death the ship respawns at the centre once the centre is clear, protected for 2 s (shown by a flicker)." (The flicker is f0222's: it shows `protect > 0`.)
- Rules: "**Game over:** the revive offer (1 R$ for 3 new lives, keeping score and wave)." This is f0223's. This issue ends the run at the last life lost and keeps everything the revive keeps (score, wave, the field) frozen in the state; f0223 adds `Classic.revive`.
- Every game supports keyboard, gamepad and touch (f0203, pickman's rule f0146): Logic takes one `Input` value whatever the device; the key map is f0222's.

Prep choices (each marked "(prep)" where it matters below; each is a tuning or a reading the capture leaves open, none changes a decision above, and each costs one data-table edit or one rule to change):
- **The field:** 1,024 × 768 units (the arcade's screen); 256 whole headings, counter-clockwise from +x, 64 up; 60 ticks a second.
- **The ship:** radius 12; turns 3 headings a tick (a full turn in 86 ticks, about 1.4 s); thrust 0.1 a tick along its heading; drag 1% of the velocity every tick, thrusting or not ("slight drag": from top speed it coasts to half in about 1.1 s); top speed 6 a tick (the width in 2.8 s), reached after 93 ticks of thrust; it starts, and respawns, at the centre at rest pointing up. No protection at the run's start (the rocks start 160 or more away).
- **Shots:** from the ship's nose at 8 a tick plus the ship's velocity (the arcade's); 60 ticks ("about 1 s"); a fire is the tick a press arrived (f0222 passes `fire = true` once per press, as Invaders' f0218 does), and Logic fires whenever `fire` is exactly true and fewer than 4 are out. A fire with 4 out is dropped, not remembered.
- **Rocks:** radii 40, 20 and 10; every new rock (a wave's and a split's pieces) gets a drawn heading and a speed drawn from 8 steps of its size's range (large 0.75 to 1.5, medium 1 to 2.25, small 1.25 to 3 a tick), so pieces fly faster than their parent on average; pieces start at the hit rock's centre. Ramming a rock or a saucer scores it (the arcade's).
- **Waves:** each rock starts on the field's edge (the bottom or the left: the field wraps, so these are all four edges), moved half that edge along when it would start within 160 of the ship.
- **Hyperspace:** the ship is away 30 ticks (0.5 s) at a spot drawn when it jumps, at rest, heading kept; the 1-in-6 roll is made at re-entry; it can come back onto a rock (which then hits it, as in the arcade).
- **Saucers:** the next one comes once the field has had none for 900 ticks (15 s) in wave 1, 120 fewer each wave, never under 360 (6 s), counting every tick of play whether the ship is on the field or not; it is small with a chance of 1/8 in wave 1, one eighth more each wave, 7/8 from wave 7 ("appears more often in later waves"); the large moves 2 a tick, the small 3; it changes course every 60 ticks to 45° up, level or 45° down; the large fires every 60 ticks in a drawn direction, the small every 40 ticks at the ship (no lead), with an aim error drawn within a spread of 16 headings (22.5°), 8 from 10,000 points, 4 from 20,000 and none from 40,000 ("more accurate as the score rises"); its shots fly 6 a tick for 60 ticks; it fires only while the ship is on the field, and a shot due while the ship is away waits for it. **Saucers and their shots pass through rocks** (the arcade's collide with them; this keeps the rocks the player's points, and costs one collision rule to change).
- **A loss:** the ship waits 120 ticks (2 s), unseen, then comes back at the centre on the first tick no rock or saucer comes within 80 of the centre (80 beyond the body's own radius); its protection lasts 120 ticks ("2 s") and covers rocks, the saucer and its shots (a protected ship passes through them and they stay whole); a protected ship flies, fires and can jump. **The protection runs down every tick the ship is not lost, on the field or away in hyperspace**, so a jump neither ends nor lengthens it (it stays "2 s" from the respawn), and a protected ship that comes back onto a rock passes through it; **the re-entry roll ignores protection** (a jump is always the gamble). The saucer fires at a protected ship (it is on the field). Its shots in flight fly on and still score. At most one loss a tick. The loss event says where the ship was lost (for a hyperspace explosion, the spot it came back to), so f0222 can show the explosion there.
- **Game over** freezes the run: `step` changes nothing (no drift) until f0223's revive.

### The field (`src/shared/Logic/Field/init.luau`), a leaf
```lua
export type Field = { width: number, height: number }
Field.CLASSIC: Field -- { width = 1024, height = 768 }
Field.HEADINGS: number -- 256
function Field.wrap(f: Field, x: number, y: number): (number, number) -- x % width, y % height: [0, width) x [0, height)
function Field.delta(f: Field, ax: number, ay: number, bx: number, by: number): (number, number) -- shortest a -> b, each part in [-size/2, size/2)
function Field.distance(f: Field, ax: number, ay: number, bx: number, by: number): number -- the length of delta
function Field.unit(heading: number): (number, number) -- (cos, sin) of heading * 2 pi / 256
function Field.heading(dx: number, dy: number): number -- the nearest whole heading (0..255) to the direction; 0 for (0, 0)
function Field.sweep(f: Field, x: number, y: number, vx: number, vy: number, cx: number, cy: number, r: number): number?
```
- `sweep` is how far along its move (0 to 1) a point moving from `(x, y)` by `(vx, vy)` first comes **closer than** `r` to `(cx, cy)`, across the wrap (the shortest `delta` to the centre, then the segment); 0 when it starts inside; nil when its closest approach is `r` or more (touching is not reaching) or it does not move and is outside. Every shot hit uses it, so no shot jumps a rock between two ticks' positions, however fast.
- Exactly half the field away counts as the negative half (`delta(0, 0, 512, 384) = (-512, -384)`), so the shortest way is defined everywhere.

### `Ship` (`src/shared/Logic/Ship/init.luau`), a leaf
```lua
export type Config = { radius: number, turnRate: number, thrust: number, drag: number, maxSpeed: number, startHeading: number }
export type Ship = { x: number, y: number, vx: number, vy: number, heading: number }
Ship.CLASSIC: Config -- radius 12, turnRate 3, thrust 0.1, drag 0.01, maxSpeed 6, startHeading 64
function Ship.new(c: Config, x: number, y: number): Ship -- at rest, heading startHeading
function Ship.fly(s: Ship, c: Config, f: Field.Field, turn: number, thrust: boolean) -- one tick
function Ship.nose(s: Ship, c: Config, f: Field.Field): (number, number) -- radius ahead along the heading, wrapped
```
- **One tick of flight, in this order** (each step pinned): turn (`heading = (heading + turn * turnRate) % 256`; a turn of 1 is to the left, counter-clockwise seen from above with `y` up, -1 to the right); thrust (`v += unit(heading) * thrust`, along the heading it has after this tick's turn); drag (`v *= 1 - drag`, every tick); the top speed (a speed above `maxSpeed` is scaled down to it, so the limit is on the speed, not on each axis); move and wrap (`x, y = wrap(x + vx, y + vy)`).

### `Rocks` (`src/shared/Logic/Rocks/init.luau`), a leaf
```lua
export type SizeName = "large" | "medium" | "small"
export type Size = { radius: number, points: number, speedMin: number, speedMax: number, splitsInto: SizeName?, pieces: number }
export type Rock = { id: number, size: SizeName, x: number, y: number, vx: number, vy: number }
export type Draw = (n: number) -> number -- a whole number in 1..n
Rocks.SIZES: { large: Size, medium: Size, small: Size }
	-- large { radius 40, points 20, speed 0.75..1.5, splitsInto "medium", pieces 2 }
	-- medium { radius 20, points 50, speed 1..2.25, splitsInto "small", pieces 2 }
	-- small { radius 10, points 100, speed 1.25..3, splitsInto nil, pieces 0 }
Rocks.SPEED_STEPS: number -- 8
function Rocks.speed(size: SizeName, step: number): number -- speedMin + (speedMax - speedMin) * (step - 1) / 7
function Rocks.launch(id: number, size: SizeName, x: number, y: number, draw: Draw): Rock -- draws the heading (draw(256) - 1), then the step (draw(8))
function Rocks.split(r: Rock, firstId: number, draw: Draw): { Rock } -- its pieces at its centre, ids firstId, firstId + 1, ...
function Rocks.move(r: Rock, f: Field.Field) -- one tick in a straight line, wrapped; no drag
```

### `Saucers` (`src/shared/Logic/Saucers/init.luau`), a leaf
```lua
export type Kind = "large" | "small"
export type KindConfig = { radius: number, points: number, speed: number, fireTicks: number, aimed: boolean }
export type Config = { large: KindConfig, small: KindConfig, firstWait: number, waitStep: number, minWait: number,
	smallEighths: { number }, turnTicks: number, shotSpeed: number, shotLife: number, spread: { { score: number, spread: number } } }
export type Saucer = { kind: Kind, x: number, y: number, vx: number, vy: number, travelled: number, fireElapsed: number, turnElapsed: number }
export type Draw = (n: number) -> number
Saucers.CLASSIC: Config -- large { radius 20, points 200, speed 2, fireTicks 60, aimed false },
	-- small { radius 10, points 1000, speed 3, fireTicks 40, aimed true }, firstWait 900, waitStep 120, minWait 360,
	-- smallEighths { 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 }, turnTicks 60, shotSpeed 6, shotLife 60,
	-- spread { { 0, 16 }, { 10000, 8 }, { 20000, 4 }, { 40000, 0 } }
function Saucers.wait(c: Config, wave: number): number -- max(minWait, firstWait - waitStep * (wave - 1))
function Saucers.smallShare(c: Config, wave: number): number -- smallEighths[min(wave, #smallEighths)]
function Saucers.spread(c: Config, score: number): number -- the last spread row whose score the score has reached
function Saucers.appear(c: Config, f: Field.Field, wave: number, draw: Draw): Saucer
function Saucers.move(s: Saucer, c: Config, f: Field.Field, draw: Draw): boolean -- true once it has crossed
function Saucers.aim(s: Saucer, c: Config, f: Field.Field, score: number, tx: number, ty: number, draw: Draw): number
```
- **`appear`** draws, in this order: the kind (small when `draw(8) <= smallShare(wave)`), the side (`draw(2)`: 1 from the left edge heading right, 2 from the right heading left; both start on `x = 0`, which is also the right edge), the height (`draw(height) - 1`). It starts level (`vy = 0`) at its kind's speed, with every count at 0.
- **`move`**: `turnElapsed += 1`; at `turnTicks` it goes back to 0 and `vy = (draw(3) - 2) * speed` (down, level or up at 45°); then `x, y = wrap(x + vx, y + vy)` and `travelled += |vx|`; it returns true once `travelled >= width` (the large after 512 moves, the small after 342), and the saucer leaves. It wraps top to bottom while it crosses; it never comes back round the side.
- **`aim`**: the large draws its direction (`draw(256) - 1`); the small points at `(tx, ty)` the short way across the wrap (`Field.heading(Field.delta(...))`), plus an error `draw(2 * spread + 1) - 1 - spread`, wrapped into 0..255. With no spread the draw is `draw(1)` and the aim is exact.

### `Pilot` (`src/shared/Logic/Pilot/init.luau`), a leaf over `Field` and `Ship`
```lua
export type State = "alive" | "hyperspace" | "dead"
export type Cause = "rock" | "saucer" | "saucershot" | "hyperspace"
export type Shot = { x: number, y: number, vx: number, vy: number, life: number }
export type Controls = { turn: number, thrust: boolean, fire: boolean, hyperspace: boolean }
export type Config = { field: Field.Field, ship: Ship.Config, lives: number, extraLifeEvery: number, shotLimit: number,
	shotSpeed: number, shotLife: number, hyperspaceTicks: number, hyperspaceOdds: number, respawnTicks: number, protectTicks: number }
export type Pilot = { ship: Ship.Ship, shipState: State, timer: number, protect: number, shots: { Shot },
	lives: number, score: number, nextExtra: number }
export type Event = { kind: string, lives: number?, cause: Cause?, x: number?, y: number? }
export type Draw = (n: number) -> number
function Pilot.new(c: Config, x: number, y: number): Pilot -- on the field at rest at (x, y), c.lives, no score
function Pilot.controls(input: any): Controls -- a turn only of exactly 1 or -1, the rest only when exactly true
function Pilot.exposed(p: Pilot): boolean -- on the field and protect == 0
function Pilot.award(p: Pilot, c: Config, points: number, emit: (Event) -> ()) -- the score, then each extra life it earns
function Pilot.lose(p: Pilot, c: Config, cause: Cause, emit: (Event) -> ()): boolean -- the loss; true on the last life
function Pilot.clock(p: Pilot, c: Config, draw: Draw, clear: () -> boolean, emit: (Event) -> ()): boolean -- step 1
function Pilot.control(p: Pilot, c: Config, controls: Controls, draw: Draw, emit: (Event) -> ()) -- step 2
```
- A pilot is **any table with these fields**: `Classic`'s `Game` has them all at its top level and passes itself (`Pilot.clock(g, g.config, …)`), and `Classic.Config` has every field of `Pilot.Config`. Luau accepts a wider table where a narrower one is expected, so no copy is made and every promoted case's `g.ship`, `g.lives` and `g.score` is the pilot's own. A run with two ships (f0221) keeps two pilot tables and steps each through the same functions; the functions keep no state of their own (CV5).
- `Pilot` emits through a callback with its own small `Event` type; `Classic` copies each into its own event list (`{ kind, lives, cause, x, y }`), so neither module needs the other's type and no `any` crosses between them (pickman-arch rule 8).
- `Pilot.lose` emits `ShipDestroyed` with the place the ship was lost (`x`, `y`: where it stood, or for a hyperspace explosion the spot it came back to), then puts the ship at the field's centre, lost (`timer = respawnTicks`, `protect = 0`), and returns whether that was its last life. **What the last life ends is the run's**: `Classic` ends the run (`GameOver`); f0221's run ends when both are out ("a lone survivor plays on").
- `Pilot.clock` and `Pilot.control` are steps 1 and 2 below. The respawn's clear centre is the run's to judge (it reads the rocks and the saucer), so `clock` asks `clear()` only on a tick the lost ship's wait is over.

### `Classic` (`src/shared/Logic/Classic/init.luau`, `Classic/Rng.luau`)
```lua
export type Phase = "playing" | "gameover"
export type ShipState = Pilot.State
export type Cause = Pilot.Cause
export type By = "shot" | "ship"
export type Shot = Pilot.Shot
export type Input = { turn: number?, thrust: boolean?, fire: boolean?, hyperspace: boolean? }
export type Event = { kind: string, points: number?, id: number?, size: Rocks.SizeName?, saucer: Saucers.Kind?, by: By?,
	cause: Cause?, lives: number?, wave: number?, x: number?, y: number? }
export type Config = { tickRate: number, field: Field.Field, ship: Ship.Config, saucers: Saucers.Config, lives: number,
	extraLifeEvery: number, shotLimit: number, shotSpeed: number, shotLife: number, hyperspaceTicks: number,
	hyperspaceOdds: number, respawnTicks: number, clearRadius: number, protectTicks: number, firstRocks: number,
	rocksStep: number, maxRocks: number, waveTicks: number, spawnClear: number }
export type Game = { config: Config, phase: Phase, wave: number, score: number, lives: number, nextExtra: number,
	rng: number, nextId: number, ship: Ship.Ship, shipState: ShipState, timer: number, protect: number,
	shots: { Shot }, rocks: { Rocks.Rock }, saucer: Saucers.Saucer?, saucerShots: { Shot }, saucerElapsed: number,
	waveTimer: number }
Classic.TICK_RATE = 60
Classic.CONFIG: Config -- tickRate 60, field Field.CLASSIC, ship Ship.CLASSIC, saucers Saucers.CLASSIC, lives 3,
	-- extraLifeEvery 10000, shotLimit 4, shotSpeed 8, shotLife 60, hyperspaceTicks 30, hyperspaceOdds 6,
	-- respawnTicks 120, clearRadius 80, protectTicks 120, firstRocks 4, rocksStep 2, maxRocks 11, waveTicks 120,
	-- spawnClear 160
function Classic.new(seed: number, config: Config?): Game -- wave 1's rocks, 3 lives, the ship at the centre, playing
function Classic.step(g: Game, input: Input?): { Event } -- one tick; the events in the order they happened
function Classic.rockCount(c: Config, wave: number): number -- min(maxRocks, firstRocks + rocksStep * (wave - 1))
```
- `Rng.luau` is a Park-Miller generator (as pickman's `Ghosts/Rng.luau` and Invaders' f0216), its state one number in `Game.rng`, seeded from `seed` floored; a seed that is NaN or infinite counts as 0. Every draw a leaf makes comes through a `Draw` over that state, so the run stays plain data and replays. The arcade library's Rng (f0203) lets "a game's Logic take a `Draw` ... or a seed"; this takes a seed, and its leaves take a `Draw` with the same type, so f0222 may later pass an arcade seed without Logic requiring arcade Logic (asteroids-arch, change 1).
- `config` (prep) is optional and defaults to `Classic.CONFIG`; every rule reads `g.config`, never `Classic.CONFIG`, so f0221 can pass its own (1.5 × the rocks) and the specs can set one number (the hyperspace odds) apart.
- **Input** is sanitized in Logic (`Pilot.controls`), so a client can't lie through the adapter: `turn` counts only when it is exactly `1` or `-1` (anything else is 0); `thrust`, `fire` and `hyperspace` only when exactly `true`. A missing or non-table input is none of them.
- **The ship's life.** `shipState` is `"alive"` (on the field), `"hyperspace"` (away; `timer` counts down) or `"dead"` (lost; `timer` counts down, then it waits for a clear centre). `protect` counts the protected ticks left. A lost ship's `ship` is `Ship.new` at the centre (unseen: f0222 draws no ship unless `shipState == "alive"`), so the respawn needs no other position.
- **One tick while playing, in this order** (the order is a rule; the cases named in the Rules table pin each part):
  1. **The ship's clocks** (`Pilot.clock`). While the ship is not lost (on the field or in hyperspace), `protect` drops by one if above 0. In hyperspace: `timer` drops by one; at 0 it re-enters: `draw(hyperspaceOdds) == 1` loses the ship there (cause `"hyperspace"`), protected or not, else it is on the field again (event `HyperspaceEnd`) and acts this tick. Lost: `timer` drops by one if above 0; once it is 0, on the first tick the centre is clear (every rock's centre at least its radius + `clearRadius` from the field's centre, and the saucer's likewise) the ship comes back: `Ship.new` at the centre, on the field, `protect = protectTicks`, event `Respawn`; it acts this tick. (A jump on tick J re-enters on J + 30; a loss on tick D respawns on D + 120 at the earliest; protection covers the 120 ticks from the respawn's, jumps included.)
  2. **The input** (`Pilot.control`), only while the ship is on the field: a hyperspace press jumps (the spot drawn, `x` then `y`, as `draw(width) - 1` and `draw(height) - 1`; velocity 0; heading kept; `timer = hyperspaceTicks`; event `Hyperspace` with the spot) and nothing else happens from the input this tick; otherwise `Ship.fly` with the turn and the thrust, then a fire with fewer than `shotLimit` shots out starts one at `Ship.nose`, with velocity `unit(heading) * shotSpeed + the ship's velocity`, `life = shotLife` (event `Fire`).
  3. **The rocks move.**
  4. **The saucer.** With none, `saucerElapsed += 1`, and at `Saucers.wait(wave)` (read from the current wave each tick) one appears (`Saucers.appear`; `saucerElapsed = 0`; event `SaucerAppear`). With one, `Saucers.move`; crossed, it leaves (`saucerElapsed = 0`; event `SaucerLeave`); else `fireElapsed += 1` and, once it is at least its kind's `fireTicks` **and** the ship is on the field, it fires (`fireElapsed = 0`; from its centre at `unit(Saucers.aim(..., g.score, ship.x, ship.y)) * shotSpeed`, `life = shotLife`; event `SaucerFire`). A saucer that appears does not move or fire that tick.
  5. **The ship's shots**, oldest first. Each finds the earliest target along its move (`Field.sweep` against every rock's radius and the saucer's; the smallest `t`; a tie between rocks goes to the earlier listed, a tie between a rock and the saucer to the rock). A rock hit is removed (event `RockHit`, `by = "shot"`), scores, and its pieces (`Rocks.split`, ids from `nextId`) join the end of the rock list at once, so a later shot this tick can hit them. A saucer hit is removed (`saucerElapsed = 0`; event `SaucerHit`, `by = "shot"`) and scores. Either way the shot is gone. A shot with no hit moves and wraps, `life -= 1`, and is gone at 0: a shot moves on the tick it is fired and 59 more.
  6. **The saucer's shots**: each one whose move reaches the ship (radius 12) while the ship is **exposed** (on the field and `protect == 0`) loses the ship (cause `"saucershot"`) and is gone; the others move, wrap and age as the ship's do. They pass through rocks.
  7. **The ship's collisions**, if exposed: the first rock in the list whose centre is closer than the two radii added up is rammed (removed, scores and splits, event `RockHit` with `by = "ship"`), then the ship is lost (cause `"rock"`); else a saucer closer than the two radii is rammed (event `SaucerHit`, `by = "ship"`; scores) and the ship is lost (cause `"saucer"`). The points (and an extra life they earn) come before the loss.
  8. **The waves.** With `waveTimer > 0` it drops by one, and at 0 the next wave starts: `wave += 1`, then the wave's rocks (below), event `WaveStart`. Else, with no rock left, `waveTimer = waveTicks` (event `WaveClear` with the cleared wave): the next wave starts 120 ticks after the tick its last rock went.
  - **A loss** (any cause, `Pilot.lose`): `lives -= 1` (event `ShipDestroyed` with the cause, the lives left and where it happened), the ship lost at the centre (`timer = respawnTicks`, `protect = 0`). The last life lost is game over (`phase = "gameover"`, event `GameOver`, set by `Classic`), and the rest of the tick is skipped. A ship lost in step 6 is no longer exposed in step 7, so one tick costs at most one life. The ship's shots in flight fly on.
- **The wave's rocks** (`Classic.new` and each next wave): `rockCount(wave)` large rocks, each placed by `at = draw(width + height) - 1` (`(at, 0)` along the bottom edge for `at < width`, else `(0, at - width)` up the left edge); when that point is closer than `spawnClear` to the ship's point, it moves half that edge along (`x + width/2` on the bottom, `y + height/2` on the left, wrapped), which puts it at least 224 from the ship; then `Rocks.launch` with id `nextId`. A new wave changes nothing else: score, lives, shots, the saucer, its clock and the ship stay.
- **Score and extra lives** (`Pilot.award`). Points are added as they are won; each time the score reaches `nextExtra` (10,000, then 20,000, ...) the run gains a life (event `ExtraLife` with the lives after), after the hit's event. No cap.
- **Game over** is final until a revive (f0223): `step` returns no events and changes nothing.
- **The events** (the one channel f0222 renders and sounds from; each carries at least these fields, and a later issue may add fields):

  | kind | when | fields |
  |---|---|---|
  | `Fire` | step 2, a shot starts | none |
  | `Hyperspace` | step 2, the jump | `x`, `y` (the spot) |
  | `HyperspaceEnd` | step 1, back on the field | none |
  | `RockHit` | step 5 or 7 | `id`, `size`, `points`, `by` |
  | `SaucerHit` | step 5 or 7 | `saucer`, `points`, `by` |
  | `ExtraLife` | after the hit whose points reached it | `lives` (after the gain) |
  | `SaucerAppear` | step 4 | `saucer` |
  | `SaucerLeave` | step 4, crossed | `saucer` |
  | `SaucerFire` | step 4 | `saucer` |
  | `ShipDestroyed` | step 1, 6 or 7 | `cause`, `lives` (left), `x`, `y` (where it was lost) |
  | `GameOver` | after the last `ShipDestroyed` | none |
  | `Respawn` | step 1 | `lives` |
  | `WaveClear` | step 8, no rock left | `wave` (the cleared one) |
  | `WaveStart` | step 8, 120 ticks later | `wave` (the new one) |

### The split and server authority
- Everything above is pure Logic (pickman-arch rule 2): no engine words (the meta spec bans the word `game` even in comments, so comments say "run"; `gameover` and `Game` are allowed), time is the tick count, randomness is the seeded state, input is the `Input` value.
- f0222's server adapter owns a `Game` per player, steps it at `TICK_RATE` from an accumulator (fixed ticks, so the rules don't depend on the frame rate), feeds it the player's held turn and thrust and a fire or hyperspace on the tick its press arrived, and replicates it. The client only sends intents and renders (the 3-D tumble, the flicker, the tiled wrap). Score, lives, every hit and every draw are the server's (rule 7).
- f0223 adds the revive and reads `score`, `wave` for the boards; f0221 builds two ships on one field from the same leaves.

### Edge cases
- Simultaneous events in one tick follow the order above: the clocks before the input (a ship back from hyperspace or a respawn acts at once); the input before anything moves (a jump takes the ship away before any hit, and wins over a fire); the ship's shots before the saucer's shots and the collisions (a shot that breaks the rock you are about to ram scores it, though its pieces may still catch you); a saucer shot and a rock on one tick cost one life; the points of a ram and its extra life before the ram's loss; the last life lost ends the tick, so no `WaveClear` comes with it (a revive then finds no rock, and the next tick's step 8 clears the wave: no softlock).
- Across the wrap: every hit (a ram, a saucer's ram, the saucer's shots and the ship's) measures the short way, through `Field`, so a rock 10 units across an edge rams the ship, and the saucer's shots wrap like every other body.
- Protection and hyperspace: the protection runs down while the ship is away, so a jump keeps it but does not lengthen it; a protected ship that comes back onto a rock passes through it; the re-entry roll is made whether or not the ship is protected.
- Bounds: every position is wrapped into the field; `delta` is defined at exactly half the field; the saucer leaves after a full width; a shot with `life` 0 is gone.
- Empty input: nil, a non-table, or junk values are no turn, thrust, fire or jump.
- No rocks: only between a clear and the next wave (`waveTimer` runs). No saucer: its clock runs.
- A player who leaves: f0222 drops their `Game`. A loss is the `dead` state; a jump is the `hyperspace` state; both ignore input.
- Frame-rate independence: Logic counts ticks only; the adapter's accumulator turns seconds into ticks.
- Determinism: the same seed and inputs give the same run tick for tick in one runtime. The state and events are JSON-safe (dense arrays, string keys, finite numbers) so f0222 can replicate them and a failure can be replayed in Lune. Floating-point results (`math.cos`, `math.atan2`, `math.sqrt`) are not promised bit for bit across runtimes: only the server steps a run (E4).

### Scalability
- Per tick: the ship O(1); rocks O(R) to move (R ≤ 11 large → at most 11 + 22 + 44, in practice under 20: the bench's busiest moment had 15); the ship's shots O(4 × (R + 1)) sweeps; the saucer's shots ≤ 2, O(1) each; the collisions O(R + 1). Prep's bench (E3): 2.3 µs a step on average (7.2 µs on a busier run), under 0.05% of one core per run at 60 Hz, so a server full of solo runs, or two ships on one field (f0221), stays far inside the budget.
- The state is a dozen numbers, ≤ 4 + 2 shots, the rocks (6 numbers each) and one saucer: f0222 decides what to replicate (the rock list on change by id, positions per tick, or each rock's start and velocity, since a rock drifts in a straight line).

### Maintainability
- Every number is a data table: `Field.CLASSIC`, `Ship.CLASSIC`, `Rocks.SIZES`, `Saucers.CLASSIC` (with its share and spread tables), `Classic.CONFIG`; a new rock size is a `SIZES` row whose parent names it in `splitsInto`, a new saucer kind a `KindConfig`, a tuning a table edit.
- **f0221's path (design review 001 F5):** the per-ship rules (the ship's life, protection, the respawn, the jump and its roll, the controls, the shot limit, the award and extra lives, the loss) are `Pilot`'s, not `Classic`'s, so f0221's two-player run keeps two pilot tables on one field and steps each through `Pilot.clock`, `Pilot.control`, `Pilot.award` and `Pilot.lose`; it writes its own composer only for what is shared (the rocks ×1.5 through its config's `firstRocks`, `rocksStep` and `maxRocks`, the saucer, the waves, the bounce, which pilot a shot's points go to, and the run's end). What stays run-wide is `Classic`'s: the saucer's clock, aim and fire hold, the wave clock and the clear-centre test (f0221 decides whether the saucer aims at the nearer ship). The correct variants of E2 keep every promoted spec green under f0221's kinds of change: a player-count field in the state (CV2), an owner on the ship (CV3), the rock count from the caller's config (CV4), an extra event field (CV1), and a second pilot table stepped by the same clock and controls every tick (CV5).
- The events are the one channel f0222 renders and sounds from, so a new effect needs no new state; a rock's `id` lets the view keep one tumbling model per rock.

### Usability
- For the player: the arcade's rules and pace, readable at a glance from the cockpit (f0222's view): one field, four shots at most, a saucer's shots only while you are there to see them, a respawn that waits for room, two seconds' protection, and a hyperspace that is a real gamble. Nothing in Logic hides a rule the view can't show (`protect`, `shipState`, the saucer's kind).
- For the developer: six small modules, typed, each with one table of numbers; `Classic.step(g, input)` is the whole loop and returns what happened; the state can be set directly in a spec; the leaves take a `Draw`, so a spec can script every random choice. Invalid input can't move or fire the ship.

## Acceptance criteria

### Checked
- AC1: a 1,024 x 768 wrap-around field: every body wraps, distances run the short way across the wrap, and a move is checked along its whole length.
- AC2: the ship turns 3 headings a tick, thrusts 0.1 along its new heading with inertia, loses 1% a tick to drag and never passes 6 a tick.
- AC3: up to 4 shots from the nose at 8 a tick plus the ship's velocity, each flying 60 ticks and wrapping, hitting the nearest target along its move.
- AC4: rocks split large into 2 medium into 2 small, scoring 20, 50 and 100; ramming one costs a life and the rock still splits and scores.
- AC5: the large saucer (200) fires at random, the small (1,000) aims at the ship, closer as the score rises, and comes more often in later waves; both cross edge to edge.
- AC6: hyperspace puts the ship at a random spot for 30 ticks, and it explodes on re-entry 1 time in 6.
- AC7: 3 lives and one more every 10,000; a lost ship comes back at the centre once it is clear, protected 120 ticks; the last life lost is game over.
- AC8: wave 1 has 4 large rocks and each later wave 2 more, up to 11, on the edges clear of the ship; the next wave starts 120 ticks after the last rock.
- AC9: a seeded, deterministic, JSON-safe run at 60 ticks a second, whose events carry their kinds' fields.
- AC10: every Logic feature is engine-free, requires only declared edges and has unit specs. (The meta spec checks the features that exist; AC1 to AC9's `@logic/<Feature>` requires are what prove the six exist, `Pilot` through `Classic`'s require of it.)

### Judged
None: every rule is a number or an order a spec checks.

### Observed
None in this issue: it has no view. How the rules feel from the cockpit (the turn rate, the drag, the saucers' pace) is f0222's Observed criteria, and each is one number in a table.
