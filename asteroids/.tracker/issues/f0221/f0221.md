## Problem
The user, 2026-10-31: "I want to branch out into multiple games, they will each have their own directory in the project: first person snake, first person asteroids. I also want a new project for a landing portal where you can scroll between the games and see the highscores for each, the same cost model remains but it shoudl show 5 robux for each game witha slash through showing it is currently on sale for 1 robux. highscores shoudl show how many revives were used. You can shoot each other's barriers. the aliens would then come in from the side and and split into two groups that move toward either player". Then: "Add a third game first person asteroids." Decided with the user (2026-10-31): the 2-player aliens game is pure Space Invaders (`invaders`); Asteroids is its own game (`asteroids`); one Roblox experience with the portal as start place, replacing pickman's lobby; every board shows revives used; one free game per hour per game.

The approved design is `asteroids/.tracker/game-design.md`, section **Game design: Asteroids (`asteroids/`)** (c0183). This issue owns these parts of it, as they are said there: **2 players**, as rules (not "Pairing"). The rest of it belongs to other issues (c0190's ownership table), listed under Non-goals.

What should happen:
- Two ships share one field from opposite sides with 1.5 × the rocks; own scores, lives and revives; no friendly fire by default, ships bounce; a lone survivor plays on.
- Pure Logic: the place that runs it, and the party's arrival, are f0222's.

## Non-goals

- The place and the party's arrival: f0222.

## Solution
A new pure Logic feature, `asteroids/src/shared/Logic/TwoPlayer/`, runs the two-player match on f0220's solo rules. The match is one table that is a `Classic` run on the field's side (one set of rocks, one saucer, one wave clock and one seeded generator) and holds two players, each a `Pilot` (f0220's per-ship leaf) with a slot, a home, a run phase, revives and a left flag. Each tick steps every player through the same per-ship functions the solo run uses (`Pilot.clock`, `Pilot.control`, `Pilot.award`, `Pilot.lose`) and the field through `Classic`'s run-wide steps, which this issue makes public over "a run and a player" (in solo both are the run itself, so nothing the solo run does changes: f0220's specs stay green, AC6). `TwoPlayer` owns only what joins the two: the ships' homes on opposite sides, the 1.5 × rock count (three numbers in its config), the bounce, friendly fire's switch, which player the saucer aims at, the tick's lead (who goes first and wins a tie, alternating every tick), the lone survivor, the match's end, and each player's revive and leaving. Every number is a data table; the user's two switches are `friendlyFire` (false) and `shipsBounce` (true).

## Design

### Product decisions this relies on
Quoted from `asteroids/.tracker/game-design.md`, **Game design: Asteroids (`asteroids/`)**, **2 players**:
- "Both ships share one field, starting on opposite sides, with the same waves (the rock count is ×1.5)."
- "Each has their own score, lives and revives, so it is a race for the most points on shared rocks."
- "Default: shots pass through the other ship (no friendly fire), but ships bounce off each other. The user may change either."
- "If one player is out with no revive, the other plays on alone."
- Pillar (2): "Deterministic Logic." And the solo rules this builds on, as f0220 designs them (its Design, prepared on its prep branch `ac257be4`): the field, the ship, shots, rocks, saucers, hyperspace, lives, protection, the respawn and the waves, with `Pilot` holding every per-ship rule (f0220 design review 001 F5: "f0221's two-player run keeps two pilot tables on one field and steps each through `Pilot.clock`, `Pilot.control`, `Pilot.award` and `Pilot.lose`").
- Not this issue's: "**Pairing** works as for Invaders" (f0208, f0222); "Game over: the revive offer" (the offer and its purchase are f0223's: this issue gives each player the revive the offer buys, as "own … revives" asks).

Prep choices (marked "(prep)" below; each is a reading or a tuning the capture leaves open, none changes a decision above, and each costs one data-table edit or one rule to change):
1. **Homes** (prep): player 1 starts at (256, 384) and player 2 at (768, 384), a quarter and three quarters of the way across at mid-height ("opposite sides": 512 apart, the farthest two points on a wrapping row), both at rest pointing up (the solo start heading). Each ship is lost to and respawns at its own home, not the centre.
2. **×1.5, rounded down** (prep): 6, 9, 12, 15, then 16 large rocks (the solo 4, 6, 8, 10, 11 × 1.5, 16.5 rounded down). These are the config's `firstRocks = 6`, `rocksStep = 3`, `maxRocks = 16`, so no new rule: every other solo number stays.
3. **A home is clear of rocks and the saucer only** (prep), as the solo centre is: the other ship is not counted, so a player cannot camp a rival's home to hold their respawn; the bounce parts two ships that meet there.
4. **A new wave's rocks keep clear of both ships** (prep): a rock that would start within 160 of a ship still playing moves half its edge along (the solo rule), unless that puts it nearer a ship than it was. With one ship this is exactly f0220's rule (the moved point is always 224 or more from the ship).
5. **The saucer** (prep): one saucer and one clock for the match; it fires at the nearer ship on the field (the short way across the wrap) and aims from that player's score (the small saucer's accuracy rises with the score it is aiming from); it holds its fire while neither ship is on the field. Its shot hits the first exposed ship it reaches along its move.
6. **The tick's lead** (prep): player 1 leads the first tick, player 2 the next, and so on. The lead goes first at every per-player stage and wins every tie (two shots reaching one rock on one tick, the saucer's aim between two ships equally near, a saucer shot reaching both at one point). Alternating keeps the race fair; a fixed order would give player 1 every tie.
7. **Friendly fire, when switched on** (prep): a shot can hit the other player's exposed ship (on the field, not protected), never its own; the nearest target along the move wins and a tie goes to a rock, then the saucer; the hit costs the other player a life (cause `"shot"`), scores nothing, and names the shooter.
8. **The bounce** (prep): two ships on the field whose centres are closer than their two radii (24) move half the overlap apart each along the line of centres and, if they are closing, swap their speeds along that line (an elastic bounce of equal masses: momentum kept, the sideways speeds kept); a speed above the top speed is scaled down to it. Protected ships bounce too (protection is from harm); a ship away in hyperspace or lost does not. Touching (exactly 24) is not overlapping. Touching never costs a life. Two ships on one point part along +x, player 1 to the left. A `Bounce` event lets f0222 play a sound.
9. **Out** (prep): a player's last life lost ends their run (`phase = "gameover"`, their own `GameOver` event) and they are out of play at once: no clock, input, shots or collisions; their ship stays gone at their home; their shots leave the field on that tick; their score, the wave and their revives stay for f0223's boards. The other plays on alone in the same match: the waves stay at 1.5 × (the user said "plays on alone", not "plays solo"; one rule to change), and are kept clear of the survivor only.
10. **Revive** (prep): `TwoPlayer.revive(m, slot)` gives an out player 3 new lives (the solo revive's "3 new lives, keeping score and wave"), keeping their score and their next extra life, counted in their `revives`; their ship comes back at its home on the first tick it is clear, protected 120 ticks (`Respawn`). It works while the other plays and restarts a finished match. When it may be called (the offer, the purchase, its timeout) is f0223's.
11. **Leave** (prep): `TwoPlayer.leave(m, slot)` takes a player out for good at once (no revive), ship and shots gone; the other plays on; the last to leave ends the match. f0222's place calls it when a player drops.
12. **The match's end** (prep): both out is `phase = "gameover"` and a `MatchOver` event; `step` then changes nothing until a revive.

### `Pilot` (f0220's leaf, a change): a home
```lua
export type Home = { x: number, y: number }
export type Cause = "rock" | "saucer" | "saucershot" | "hyperspace" | "shot" -- shot: the other player's (friendly fire)
export type Pilot = { ..., home: Home? } -- every field f0220 gives it, and the home; nil is the field's centre
function Pilot.home(p: Pilot, c: Config): (number, number) -- p.home, or (width / 2, height / 2)
```
- `Pilot.lose` puts the lost ship at `Pilot.home` (f0220: the centre), and `Pilot.clock` brings it back there (f0220: the centre) once `clear()` says so. A solo run's pilot has no `home`, so it is the centre and nothing changes.

### `Classic` (f0220's, a change): its run-wide steps made public
`Classic.step` keeps its order and behaviour and calls these, which `TwoPlayer` calls for the field and for each player. In a solo run the run is itself the player (`g` passed twice); a `Run` is the field's side of a run and a `Player` one player on it. Luau accepts the wider table where a narrower one is expected (f0220's own note on `Pilot`), so no copy is made.
```lua
export type Home = Pilot.Home
export type Rock = Rocks.Rock
export type Saucer = Saucers.Saucer
export type Player = { ship, shipState, timer, protect, shots, lives, score, nextExtra, home: Home?, -- a Pilot
	phase: Phase, slot: number? } -- whether their run is over; their slot in a match (nil solo)
export type Run = { config, wave, rng, nextId, rocks, saucer, saucerShots, saucerElapsed, waveTimer }
export type Event = { ...f0220's fields..., player: number?, shooter: number? }
function Classic.draw(g: Run): Rocks.Draw -- every draw a rule makes, from g.rng (f0220's private drawer)
function Classic.seed(seed: number): number -- the generator's first state (Rng.seed)
function Classic.spawnWave(g: Run, ships: { Ship.Ship }) -- the wave's rocks, clear of every listed ship (prep 4)
function Classic.clearAt(g: Run, x: number, y: number): boolean -- f0220's centreClear, at any point
function Classic.clock(g: Run, p: Player, events: { Event }): boolean -- step 1: Pilot.clock, its home clear by clearAt; true: their run is over
function Classic.control(g: Run, p: Player, input: Input?, events: { Event }) -- step 2: Pilot.control(Pilot.controls(input))
function Classic.moveRocks(g: Run) -- step 3
function Classic.saucerStep(g: Run, target: Player?, events: { Event }) -- step 4: fires at target, aimed from target.score; nil holds its fire
function Classic.moveShots(g: Run, p: Player, foe: Player?, events: { Event }) -- step 5: p's shots; foe: friendly fire's target
function Classic.moveSaucerShots(g: Run, players: { Player }, events: { Event }) -- step 6: the first exposed ship along each move; a tie to the earlier listed
function Classic.collide(g: Run, p: Player, events: { Event }) -- step 7
function Classic.waveClock(g: Run, ships: { Ship.Ship }, events: { Event }) -- step 8
```
- `Classic.step` becomes: `clock(g, g)` (return when it ends the run), `control(g, g, input)`, `moveRocks(g)`, `saucerStep(g, if g.shipState == "alive" then g else nil)`, `moveShots(g, g, nil)`, `moveSaucerShots(g, { g })`, the over check, `collide(g, g)`, the over check, `waveClock(g, { g.ship })`. Each is f0220's step with the player passed in.
- **A player's events carry their slot**: f0220's emitter (which copies each `Pilot.Event` into a `Classic.Event`) and every `RockHit`, `SaucerHit` and `GameOver` set `player = p.slot`, so a solo run's events gain no field. A loss by a friendly shot sets `shooter` too (the shooter's slot); no other event has one. The field's events (`SaucerAppear`, `SaucerLeave`, `SaucerFire`, `WaveClear`, `WaveStart`) have no `player`.
- **The last life lost** ends that player's run: `p.phase = "gameover"` and `GameOver` (in solo, `p` is the run: f0220's `endIfOut`). What that ends besides is the caller's: `Classic.step` returns; `TwoPlayer` ends the match only when both are out.
- `moveShots` with a `foe`: the shot's earliest target along its move among the rocks, the saucer and the foe's ship (radius 12, only while `Pilot.exposed(foe)`); a tie goes to a rock, then the saucer. The foe hit loses a life (`Pilot.lose`, cause `"shot"`, `shooter = p.slot`) and the shot is gone; the shooter scores nothing.
- Nothing else in `Classic` changes; `TwoPlayer` copies no solo rule (no second respawn, protection, jump, shot limit, extra life, split, saucer clock or wave clock).

### `TwoPlayer` (`src/shared/Logic/TwoPlayer/init.luau`)
```lua
export type Phase = "playing" | "gameover"
export type Config = { classic: Classic.Config, homes: { Classic.Home }, friendlyFire: boolean, shipsBounce: boolean }
export type Pilot = { ...every field of Classic.Player..., revives: number, left: boolean }
export type Match = { ...every field of Classic.Run..., pair: Config, phase: Phase, lead: number, pilots: { Pilot } }
TwoPlayer.CONFIG: Config -- classic = Classic.CONFIG with firstRocks 6, rocksStep 3, maxRocks 16 (frozen copy);
	-- homes { { x = 256, y = 384 }, { x = 768, y = 384 } }; friendlyFire false; shipsBounce true
function TwoPlayer.new(seed: number, config: Config?): Match -- wave 1 (clear of both ships), both playing, lead 1
function TwoPlayer.step(m: Match, inputs: { Classic.Input? }?): { Classic.Event } -- one tick; inputs[1], inputs[2]
function TwoPlayer.revive(m: Match, slot: number): boolean -- false (nothing changes) unless that player is out and has not left
function TwoPlayer.leave(m: Match, slot: number): boolean -- false if they have already left (or there is no such slot)
```
- `m.config` is `config.classic` (every `Classic` rule reads `g.config`), `m.pair` the whole `Config`, `m.rng` is `Classic.seed(seed)` (so a fraction is floored and a seed that is not a finite number counts as 0, as solo), `m.pilots[i]` player i: `slot = i`, `home` a copy of `homes[i]`, a ship at the home at rest pointing up (`Ship.new`), on the field, unprotected, no shots, `lives`, `nextExtra` from the config, `phase = "playing"`, `revives = 0`, `left = false`. `Classic.spawnWave(m, { both ships })` makes wave 1.
- **Input**: player i's is `inputs[i]`; a missing or non-table `inputs` is no input for either, and each entry is sanitized by `Pilot.controls` (exactly 1 or -1, exactly true).
- **One tick** (while `m.phase == "playing"`; the order is a rule). `order` is `{ pilots[lead], pilots[3 - lead] }`, then `lead` flips. "Each player" below means each player in `order` whose `phase` is `"playing"`.
  1. Each player's clocks: `Classic.clock`. Then the settle (below).
  2. Each player's controls: `Classic.control(m, p, inputs[p.slot])`.
  3. The bounce (prep 8), if `pair.shipsBounce` and both players are playing with ships `"alive"`: pilots 1 and 2 (in slot order, so the same-point rule is fixed), `bounce`.
  4. `Classic.moveRocks(m)`.
  5. `Classic.saucerStep(m, target)`: `target` is the playing player whose ship is `"alive"` and nearest the saucer (`Field.distance`), the lead on a tie; nil when there is no saucer or no such ship.
  6. Each player's shots: `Classic.moveShots(m, p, foe)`, `foe` the other player when `pair.friendlyFire` and they are playing, else nil. Then the settle.
  7. `Classic.moveSaucerShots(m, the playing players in order)`. Then the settle.
  8. Each player's collisions: `Classic.collide`. Then the settle.
  9. `Classic.waveClock(m, the ships of the players still playing)`.
  - **The settle**: every player not playing has no shots (`shots = {}`); when neither is playing, `m.phase = "gameover"`, event `MatchOver`, and the rest of the tick is skipped.
- **`gameover`**: `step` returns no events and changes nothing.
- **`revive(m, slot)`**: only for a player whose `phase == "gameover"` and who has not left. `phase = "playing"`, `lives = config.lives`, `revives += 1`, ship lost at its home with `timer = 0` (so `Pilot.clock` brings it back, protected, on the first tick the home is clear), `protect = 0`, no shots; `m.phase = "playing"`. Score, `nextExtra`, the wave and the other player stay.
- **`leave(m, slot)`**: `left = true`, `phase = "gameover"`, ship lost at its home (`shipState = "dead"`, `timer = 0`, `protect = 0`), no shots; when no player is still playing, `m.phase = "gameover"`. Score and revives stay.
- **`revive` and `leave` emit no event** (they are calls between ticks, not steps): the caller reads `m.phase` and the player's `phase` and `left` after the call, so a match that `leave` ends has no `MatchOver`. (Design review 001 F5.)
- **The events** (`Classic.Event`; every field not listed is nil):

  | kind | from | `player` | other fields |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `Fire`, `Hyperspace`, `HyperspaceEnd`, `RockHit`, `SaucerHit`, `ExtraLife`, `Respawn`, `GameOver` | the player's own steps | their slot | f0220's |
  | `ShipDestroyed` | a loss | the slot of the ship lost | f0220's; `shooter` (the other slot) only when `cause = "shot"` |
  | `SaucerAppear`, `SaucerLeave`, `SaucerFire`, `WaveClear`, `WaveStart` | the field | nil | f0220's |
  | `Bounce` | step 3 | nil | none |
  | `MatchOver` | the settle, both out | nil | none |

### The split and server authority
- Everything above is pure Logic (pickman-arch rule 2): no engine words (no lowercase `game` even in comments: comments say "run" or "match"), time is the tick count, randomness is the match's one seeded generator (one stream for both players, so a match replays from its seed and both inputs).
- f0222's server adapter owns a `Match` per reserved server, steps it at `Classic.TICK_RATE` from an accumulator with both players' inputs (held turn and thrust, a fire or jump on the tick its press arrived, as f0220's adapter), replicates it to both, and calls `leave` when a player drops (and, since `leave` emits no event, reads `m.phase` after it to end the reserved server). f0223 makes the revive offer and calls `revive`, and reads each player's `score`, `revives` and the match's `wave` for the boards. Clients send intents and render (rule 7).

### Edge cases
- Simultaneous events on one tick follow the order above and the lead: two shots on one rock (the lead's scores), the saucer aiming between two ships equally near (at the lead), a saucer shot reaching two ships at once (the one it reaches first along its move; the lead on an exact tie), both players losing their last lives (both `GameOver`s, then one `MatchOver`).
- A player out on the tick's first stage (a hyperspace explosion): the settle clears their shots before any shot moves.
- Two ships ramming one rock on one tick: the lead's collision comes first (its points, its lost life); the other meets only what is left (a large rock's pieces may still reach it). Two jumps, or two returns from hyperspace, on one tick: the lead draws first (its spot, its roll). (Design review 001 F2.)
- Bounds: every position wraps (the bounce uses the short way across the wrap and wraps the parted ships); touching is not overlapping; a bounce never leaves a ship above the top speed.
- Empty input: nil, a non-table, a missing entry or junk values are no input for that player.
- A player who leaves: `leave`; one who respawns: at their home, once it is clear of rocks and the saucer, protected.
- Frame-rate independence: ticks only (the adapter's accumulator turns seconds into ticks).
- Determinism and JSON safety: as f0220's run (dense arrays, string keys, finite numbers), within one runtime (f0220's E4).
- Execution window: this prep's `asteroids/.tracker/architecture.md` is f0220's prepared file (its prep branch `ac257be4`) plus the `TwoPlayer` row and three edges. Whichever of the two preps lands second resolves the conflict by taking this file (it holds both). f0221 `depends_on` f0220, so its raft starts on f0220's code; Implementation step 1 re-cuts the prototype if f0220's code differs from its prototype.

### Scalability
- Per tick: two ships O(1) each; rocks O(R) to move (R ≤ 16 large → at most 16 + 32 + 64, in the bench's busiest moment 25); each player's ≤ 4 shots O(R + 2) sweeps; the saucer's ≤ 2 shots against two ships; the collisions O(R + 1) per ship; the bounce O(1). Prep's bench (E3): 5.8 µs a step, 0.035 % of a core per match at 60 Hz.
- The state is f0220's run fields plus two pilots (a dozen numbers and ≤ 4 shots each): f0222 replicates it the way it replicates a solo run.

### Maintainability
- Every per-ship rule lives once, in `Pilot`, and every run-wide rule once, in `Classic`; `TwoPlayer` holds only what joins two players. A tuning is one table edit (`TwoPlayer.CONFIG.classic` takes any solo number; the two switches and the homes are its own).
- f0222's and f0223's kinds of change keep every spec green (E2's correct variants): an event with extra fields (CV1, CV6), a pilot or a match with a new field (CV2, CV3), a solo `Classic.revive` (CV4) and a solo run counting its revives (CV5).
- A third player would be a third pilot and a lead order over three; the steps already take a player and a list of players.

### Usability
- For the players: the solo rules exactly, side by side; a fair race (the lead alternates, every tie names a rule); no griefing (no friendly fire by default, no camping a home, bounces never hurt); a dropped or finished partner never ends your run.
- For the developer: `TwoPlayer.step(m, { input1, input2 })` is the whole loop and returns what happened, each event naming its player; `revive` and `leave` are the two calls the place and the boards make; the state can be set directly in a spec, and every rule reads `m.config` and `m.pair`, so a spec or the user can flip a switch.
