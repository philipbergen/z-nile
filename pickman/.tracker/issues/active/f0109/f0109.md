# f0109: White ghost and green walls

## Problem
One ordinary ghost is white, and the player wants that ghost a different color. That body is Inky, painted `(0, 255, 255)`. Blinky stays red, Pinky stays pink, and Clyde stays orange. The maze walls stay blue the whole time, including while the ghosts can be eaten. During that edible time the walls should be green, and blue again as soon as the ghosts can kill.

Scope decision (user, 2026-10-25): "Yes, reversal stands" — the capture (25e64e3c, "Frightened colors") asked for a new colour for the frightened ghosts' white blink and kept Inky cyan; the user's edit of 2026-10-19 (fa35872b) reversed it: Inky's normal colour changes, and the blink stays white.

Scope decision (user, 2026-10-25): the danger radar's walls follow the maze walls' colour rule ("Yes, and the danger radar needs to be more visible…"); the radar's redesign itself (circle segments with a ring of translucent indicators, about 60% of the smallest screen dimension) is a separate new issue, not f0109.

The user, 2026-10-25, again: "Having a white ghost doesn't work … it is just not very visible." The reason for the change is visibility: the near-white ghost is hard to see in the maze, so its new color must stand out from the walls, the floor, the pellets and the other ghosts.

## Non-goals
- The frightened blink stays white, and the blue frightened body stays blue.
- Eyes stay white and pupils stay blue. The ghost-house door stays pink, the floor stays black, and the level-clear wall flash stays white.
- Eating, the score chain, and the levels with no frightened time stay as they are. On those levels the walls stay blue, because the ghosts cannot be eaten.
- Inky's new colour: decided by G1 (lemon, 230, 255, 0).

## Solution
- Give Inky's ordinary body a color other than `(0, 255, 255)`, (chosen: G1). The frightened look does not use it.
- Paint the walls green while the local snapshot says the ghosts are edible, including the blink, and blue again when that ends. A level-clear flash still lights the walls while it is on.

## Design
Decisions relied on (game-design.md): the classic ghost colours (Blinky red, Pinky pink, Clyde orange; frightened blue, blinking white, eyes white) stay; the level-clear flash lights the walls white (R0015); the power period is the frightened timer, 0 on levels 17, 19, 20 and from 21 (R0009). Authority: colours are presentation, decided on the client from the replicated frame; no server change.

**Inky's colour (G1, decided with the user 2026-10-25).** Lemon **(230, 255, 0)**, in `View.GHOST_COLORS.inky` (`pickman/src/shared/Logic/View/init.luau`): GhostView's body and DangerHud's edge glow already read it through `View.ghostColor`, so one value changes both. Chosen by measurement (E1): WCAG contrast 8.0 against the blue walls, 18.7 against the black floor, 3.40 against the edible-green walls; CIE76 distance ≥ 30 from every other drawn colour: the other ghosts, the frightened blue, white, the pellets (255,184,174), the blaster's cyan (0,255,255), which Inky stops sharing, and f0126's gold marked walls, now (255, 215, 0) (the user, 2026-10-26, "Brighter gold", in f0126's session): lemon is 31.4 from it, its nearest (45.4 from the first gold, 255, 191, 0), and every ghost colour stays at least 30 (Clyde 33.3). The radar's own "you" dot is yellow (255,255,0), but the radar draws ghosts by look (all hunting ghosts red), never by name, so they never meet there.

**The walls.** Pure, in `View`:
- `View.WALL_COLOR = (33, 33, 222)` (today's MazeView constant, moved), `View.WALL_EDIBLE_COLOR = (14, 64, 14)` (G2: the user's middle green, E2c), and `View.wallColor(lit: boolean, edible: boolean): Rgb` — the level-clear flash (white, `View.WHITE`) wins, then green while edible, else blue; a fresh table each call, like `ghostColor`.
- `View.edible(play: Session.PlayFrame): boolean` — `play.frightened > 0`: the power timer runs. `Game` sets it to 0 on a death and at level clear, and it stays 0 on levels with no frightened time, so the walls stay blue there (Non-goals). It includes the blink (the timer runs through it) and, as the arcade does, the time after an eaten ghost revives dangerous (G3).

**The radar's walls (scope decision).** `pickman/src/client/DangerHud.luau` draws the radar's wall dots in `View.wallColor(lit, edible)` (today a copy of the blue, `Color3.fromRGB(33, 33, 222)`), from a state Main hands it each frame, `DangerHud.wallState(overlay.flash, View.edible(frame.play))`, and sets each visible wall dot's colour every render step, computed once per step (design review 002 F4). One rule for every wall drawn.

**The adapter.** `pickman/src/client/MazeView.luau` keeps no colours of its own: it keeps `Lit` and `Edible` as attributes on its model, gives every wall part it makes the model's current colour through one local `wallColorOf(model)` (`View.wallColor` of those attributes), so a wall built or rebuilt while the ghosts are edible comes out green (f0126's `MazeView.rewall`, which adds or removes walls during play, uses the same helper), and repaints when either attribute changes: `MazeView.flash(model, lit)` (kept, as f0031 pins it) and a new `MazeView.edible(model, edible: boolean)`. `Main.client.luau` requires `View` and, after its flash call, calls `MazeView.edible(model, View.edible(frame.play))` and `DangerHud.wallState(overlay.flash, View.edible(frame.play))` each frame (no-ops unless something changed).

**The test fake (design reviews 001 F2, 002 F1, F6).** `pickman/tests/lib/fakeengine.luau` gains what MazeView's and DangerHud's real sources need: `:SetAttribute` (refusing what Roblox refuses: a name that is not 1–100 letters, digits or underscores; a table, function, thread or Instance value), `:IsA` (only the classes the specs use: a class is itself, a Part is a BasePart), View, Awareness and Hud among the Logic it serves, and `fake.module(source, name, screen?)`, which runs one client view module's real source with the real Logic, a `workspace` with a CurrentCamera, and the client services a view asks for: GuiService (`GetInsetArea`, no insets), Players (a LocalPlayer with a PlayerGui), RunService (`RenderStepped`, a signal the spec fires), UserInputService (`TouchEnabled`); a ScreenGui's `AbsoluteSize` is the screen. The views' extra constructors (`UDim`, `UDim2.fromOffset`, `Vector3`, `NumberSequence`) live only in `fake.module`'s environment: in the shared one, `fromOffset` let f0061's off-screen-button mutant pass (E8). Its contract (stay exact in both directions) is checked by re-running f0061's affected mutants (E8).

### Scalability
One comparison per frame; repainting walks the maze's wall parts (about 50 merged runs) only when lit or edible changes: twice per power pellet and per flash toggle.

### Maintainability
Every colour a wall or ghost is drawn in lives in `View`, one pure table read by every view: MazeView and the radar lose their own wall constants (the HUD's own blue frame, `HudView.luau:18`, is not a wall and stays). The rule "flash wins, then edible" is one function.

### Usability
Inky is a bright, saturated lemon yellow, distinct from every other colour on screen; the maze turns green the moment the ghosts can be eaten and back to blue when the power ends, a whole-screen cue in first person where the ghosts may be out of sight.
