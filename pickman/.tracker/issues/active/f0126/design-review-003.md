### F1 [high] The fresh-maze reset is still unpinned on three cells: on level 2 the ghosts still walk through last level's holes, after a death they can't enter the holes at all, and a new run keeps the old marks. All 103 cases pass with each (repeat of design-review-002 F2 and F3; the third round to find holes in this mechanism)
Where: Behaviour 8's table and its claim "Every column has a case on every row that changes it"; Rules R2b, R2c, R2d, F3 to F3e; `closed.spec` (new run only); `ghosts.spec`'s `drive` ("Ends at the first death"); pick.spec "a new run starts from a fresh maze: no holes and new marks"
Evidence: three mutants of the prototype's `Session`, each run against all 10 sequestered files:
- X17 (level × the ghosts' maze): move `Ghosts.useMaze(member.ghosts, s.maze)` out of `freshMaze` and into `Session.playAgain` only. Result: `103 passed, 0 failed`. On level 2 the ghosts walk the holed maze of level 1, through walls the player sees closed. This is the level half of round 2 F2's exact scenario ("on level 2 and in a new run"). Only the new-run half got a case (`closed.spec` plays three deaths and `playAgain`).
- X1 (death × the ghosts' maze, a "kept" cell): in the Ready handler, `elseif member.ghosts then Ghosts.useMaze(member.ghosts, s.maze)` for `respawn`. Result: `103 passed, 0 failed`. After any death the ghosts treat every hole as a wall while the player can still walk into it, so a one-tile nook is a place no ghost can ever reach: a safe spot for the rest of the level, picks included.
- X16 (new run × the marks): `Session.playAgain` keeps `member.picks.marked` across `freshMaze`. Result: `103 passed, 0 failed`. `Picks.reset` builds a new table and `Game.playAgain` returns `Ready "again"` itself, so no later reset repairs it: the new run keeps run 1's last marks, which were rolled on run 1's holed maze and can be walls behind a hole that is now closed. Such a wall can't be reached, so it stays gold and can't be picked for the whole run. The new-run case asserts only `#walls.marked == 3` and `Maze.kind(CORRIDOR, tile) == "wall"`, though its title says "and new marks". Round 2 F3's set-differs and pickable-in-the-plain-layout checks went into the level case only.

A probe I ran shows the death row is cheap to pin: the restless picker on classic until 2 holes are open, then `Session.kill`, then plain steps, requiring a ghost to stand on an opened tile. It finds seed 16 in 1.6 s on the prototype and fails under X1 (`expected true, got false`).
Critique: the table now has the right columns, but each "kept" cell and each trigger is still pinned only where the author wrote a case, and the claim under the table is false three times. The tempting fix is three more cases, one per mutant. That repeats rounds 1 and 2, where each fix pinned the cells it named and the next reviewer found the next cell. The root cause is the mechanism: the ghosts hold a second copy of the member's maze (`set.maze` via `Ghosts.useMaze`), and every trigger has to re-point it by hand.
Consequences: removing the copy changes `Ghosts`' API: the maze comes in `Ghosts.Input` each step, or `useMaze` is called once per step from `member.maze`. That makes the `Ghosts.useMaze` refusal (another id or size) a per-step check, and it changes rows P6 and G1. Pinning the level row with ghosts needs a classic level clear. That means a pellet-seeking autopilot (BFS over `View.frameMaze` to the nearest pellet, turn intents) with `play.lives` raised in the spec's config so that deaths don't end the run. It costs a few thousand steps, about what `closed.spec` already plays.
Recommendation: question the mechanism. (1) Give the per-member maze one source of truth: `member.opened`. Derive `member.maze` from it, and hand the ghosts `member.maze` on every step rather than keeping a copy that each trigger must reset. Then the ghosts' column of the table disappears, and X1 and X17 can't be written. If the copy stays, say why in the Design. (2) Either way, make the claim under the table true: one mutant per cell (6 columns × 3 rows, "kept" cells included), each a Rules row that has been run. (3) Add the cases: the death-row ghost probe above (a ghost stands on a hole after a death); a level-row case on classic (autopilot clear with lives raised), where no ghost stands on a wall of `View.frameMaze` over the next steps and the search must find a seed where a ghost would have entered a closed hole; and in the new-run case the level case's two checks (every mark is pickable in the plain layout, and over a seed search the set differs from the marks before the reset).
Lesson: zz-test/per-member-copy-pinned-at-every-reader

### F2 [medium] Two more stated rules have no case: a pending request "does not count toward the per-tick intent limit", and the pick's length uses the ghosts' "normal" speed even while they are blue
Where: Behaviour 2 ("A request does not count toward the per-tick intent limit (it holds no queue slot, and repeats only reset the counter)"); Behaviour 4 (`Levels.ghostSpeed(base, level, "normal")`); game-design (prep) "a hunting ghost walking 10 tiles"; Rules table (no row for either)
Evidence: two mutants, each `103 passed, 0 failed` on all 10 files:

- X6: `startPick` uses `if member.play.frightened > 0 then "frightened" else "normal"`. A pick started while the ghosts are blue, after a pill boon or a power pellet, lasts longer.

The prototype also refuses a `Pick` submitted after four movement intents in the same tick (the rate-limit check comes before the pick branch). The Design doesn't say whether that is meant: "does not count toward" covers only the other direction.
Critique: X6 is the natural reading of the user's "the time it takes for a ghost to move 10 paces" when the ghosts are blue, so an executor may well write it. The (prep) choice of "hunting" is the only thing that rules it out, and nothing pins it. X4 is the obvious way to fold a new intent into `pending`. Lesson z-design/spec-design-correspondence, as in round 2 F4.
Consequences: the limit rule needs a decision on the other direction. Either a `Pick` is always accepted, or it is refused when four intents are already queued. Whichever is chosen is one sentence in Behaviour 2 and one assertion.
Recommendation: (a) a pick.spec case that submits `Pick` and then `maxIntentsPerTick` movement intents in one tick and expects every one accepted (this kills X4). In the same case, state and assert what a `Pick` after four movement intents returns. (b) A boons.spec case: after a `pill` boon (`withBoon("pill")`), start the next pick while `frame.play.frightened > 0` and expect `PickStarted.ticks == PICK_TICKS` (this kills X6). Add a row for each.
Lesson: z-design/spec-design-correspondence

### F3 [medium] The pick bar and the boon banner are placed by hard-coded screen fractions in HudView, outside Hud's pure layout, so "clear of the score, the lives and the radar" is Observed when it could be Checked
Where: Behaviour 9 ("The HUD shows a gold bar … and the boon's name"); Implementation step 7 ("bar and banner laid out with the HUD's safe inset, under the radar and the overlays"); OC2; prototype `HudView.pick` (`UDim2.fromScale(0.5, 0.62)` and `(0.3, 0.02)` for the bar, `(0.5, 0.3)` and `(0.6, 0.08)` for the banner)
Evidence: every other HUD element is placed by a pure Hud function that has specs: `Hud.layout(width, height, inset)` (score, lives), `Hud.shotIcons(l, lives, shots)` (f0100's shot bars, with `tests/place/hud-shots`), `Hud.radarBox(...)`, `Hud.jumpButtons(...)`. The Design gives the pick bar and the banner no layout function and no box. Step 7 asks for the safe inset, but the prototype's `HudView.pick` ignores it, and no check would notice. f0110 (touch buttons) and d0149 (touch circles, with a pick button), both in flight, add elements to the same screen.
Critique: pickman-arch rule 3 ("Any branching an adapter does that could be wrong belongs in Logic") and zz-test ("If something could be expressed as a measurable proxy, it's not Judged or Observed"). Where the bar sits relative to the radar, the score and the touch buttons is box geometry, which the HUD already checks for its other elements. The tempting fix is to name fractions in the Design. That pins numbers, not the rule the user cares about: clear of everything else.
Consequences: a `Hud.pickBox(l)` and a `Hud.bannerBox(l)` (inside the safe inset) need defined coordinate spaces (zz-arch lesson name-each-coordinate-space). They also become one more thing for d0149's layout to keep clear of, which is the point.
Recommendation: add `Hud.pickBox(layout)` and `Hud.boonBox(layout)` to Interfaces, in the same space as `Hud.layout`'s boxes. Add a parts.spec case: on a phone-sized and a desktop viewport, with and without a safe inset, neither box overlaps the score, the lives, `Hud.shotIcons` at three shots, or `Hud.radarBox` (touch and desktop), and both lie inside the inset. Add an adapters pin that `HudView.pick` places its frames from those boxes. OC2 then only judges readability.
Lesson: zz-arch/name-each-coordinate-space
