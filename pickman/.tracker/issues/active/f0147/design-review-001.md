### F1 [high] The capture asks for every level to be strictly faster; the prep dropped that for the tunnel and frightened ghosts without asking the user
Where: f0147.md Behaviour 2, E2; game-design.md "Levels by picked walls" (prep) bullet; ramp.spec "each level up to 34 is faster than the one before" (`>=` for the tunnel and frightened ghosts)
Evidence: The capture's Problem says "every level is 5% faster than the one before, for the player and the ghosts alike", and its Solution leaves open "how the rounding keeps each level strictly faster than the one before". The design answers that it doesn't, for two ghost kinds, and marks it only **(prep)**. With round-half-up from 30 × factor × 0.7 × 1.05^(n−1), measured with a python3 one-liner: shipped (tuning 1/1.1), the tunnel ghost (10 on level 1) is no faster on levels 2, 4, 6, 8 and 12, and the frightened ghost (12) on levels 3, 6 and 11. Untuned: tunnel on 2, 4, 6 and 10, frightened on 3 and 8. The user was asked four questions on 2026-10-31 (top, eyes, counter, bonanza), but not this one.
Critique: A (prep) marker covers the prep's own choices. It does not cover a captured outcome that came out smaller. The executor will build what the spec pins (`>=`), and the user finds out only by reading the (prep) line. The obvious fix is to keep the (prep) marker and call it a detail.
Consequences: Asking the user either confirms the ties or brings in a sub-unit mechanism (a per-ghost fractional remainder carried across steps). That mechanism touches `Ghosts.speedOf`'s callers, makes the step-length measure in ramp.spec ("move the ramp's units a step") alternate between two values, and needs determinism cases.
Recommendation: escalate to the user: (a) plain rounding, where the slow tunnel and frightened ghosts sometimes keep a speed for two levels (today's design, no cost); (b) forced +1 a level for those kinds, which runs about 20% over the 5% curve by level 21 (E2); (c) fractional speeds carried across steps (Bresenham-style), so each kind is exactly 5% faster on average, at the cost of a new remainder per ghost and a spec that measures speed over a window, not per step. Record the answer as a `Decided with the user` line. For (b) or (c), change ramp.spec's `>=` to `>` (or to a windowed average) and add a mutant row.

### F2 [medium] The bonanza case passes only because of seed 1's roll order: a correct implementation fails it when a faster pick is banked before the tenth pick
Where: levels.spec "the tenth pick grants the bonanza, not a rolled boon: a banked faster pick and a shot at once" (`t.expect(s.members.p.picks.fast).toBe(fast + 1)`)
Evidence: `fast` is read after nine picks. If one of those rolls banked a faster pick, the tenth pick is a fast one and consumes it (`if pick.fast then member.picks.fast -= 1`, f0126's rule). The tenth then adds the bonanza's one, so the count ends at `fast`, not `fast + 1`. I ran the case's own steps on the prototype (unchanged src) for seeds 1 to 24, as a scratch spec under `.cache/` that I then removed: seeds 6, 13 and 20 end at `1->1`, so the correct prototype fails the assertion. Seed 1 passes only because of f0126's prototype's draw order of marks and boons. f0126 is not landed yet (R0053), and its executor may draw in a different order.
Critique: The spec is sequestered, so an executor whose f0126 draws differently gets a red AC2 for correct code and has to stop and escalate. The author's tempting fix is to change the expectation to "fast + 1, or fast when the tenth was fast". That weakens the case: it would also accept a missing bonanza fast pick whenever the tenth pick was a fast one.
Consequences: Pinning the state before the tenth pick, as the case already does for `longHops`, also makes the "no rolled boon" checks independent of the seed.
Recommendation: before the tenth pick, set `s.members.p.picks.fast = 0` (so the tenth pick is never fast) and expect exactly 1 after it. Add a second sub-case that starts with `fast = 1` and expects 1 (one consumed, one granted), so M19 still dies. Check the other bonanza and level cases for the same dependence on the first nine rolls (for example, shots held when the tenth finishes, already pinned in "wasted when three are held") and pin whatever they read.

### F5 [medium] "A cleared level beats a kill" for the tenth pick is a Design rule with no spec case and no row
Where: f0147.md Edge cases, first bullet; Rules and mutants (no row)
Evidence: The design relies on the tenth pick finishing at the start of the step, so `Game.cleared` runs before `stepGhosts` and `Game.died` then refuses. Neither f0147's specs nor f0126's test this. In f0126's acceptance, the only catch case is "a ghost that reaches a picking player catches them, and the pick is wasted" (ghosts.spec:68), which is not the finishing step. A plausible wrong implementation ends the level after the step's moves, or calls `Game.cleared` at the end of the step. In that version a ghost arriving in the finishing step kills, `Game.cleared` is refused, `levelPicks` stays at 10 with `bonanza` pending, and the level ends one pick later. A reorder inside `finishPick` wouldn't show up in any current case.
Critique: This rule decides whether a player who has just earned the bonanza loses a life to it.
Consequences: Once pinned, the order also needs a case that the pending bonanza is not left set after a refused clear.
Recommendation: add a levels.spec case: with a ramp of `picks = 1` (as the long-pill case does), put an active hunting Blinky one step from the picking player, timed to arrive in the finishing step. Expect `LevelCleared`, no `Died`, lives unchanged, then `Ready reason = "level"`. Add row M46 (the clear moved after `stepGhosts`) and its mutants.py entry.
Lesson: z-design/spec-design-correspondence
