# f0234: Wait for the start at a jump's top arc, free to look

## Problem
While the game waits to start, and at the start of each new life, the player stands on the floor and sees little. They should instead be elevated at the top of a jump arc (as if at the apex of a jump), with the view held there, and be able to look around freely, so the maze is surveyed before play begins. When play starts (or the life begins) the player comes down into the normal game.

## Non-goals
- Showing where power pills are while jumping: f0235.
- The glow that shows where regular pills are: f0236.
- Changing normal jump physics or height during play.

## Solution
- Hold the player at the apex of a jump arc during the pre-start wait and the new-life wait; look controls stay live.
- Ends with a smooth drop (or the existing start) into play.

## Design

### Product decisions relied on (pickman/.tracker/game-design.md)
- *Run pauses* (R0009): "'Ready!' 2 s before play and after each death and level; death 1.5 s; level clear 2 s. Keys pressed in a pause are ignored." The wait this issue lifts is that READY pause: `Game` phase `"ready"`, `readyTicks = 60` steps (4 s of real time at `Session.STEP_RATE` 15).
- *Spy hop* (f0101): "the eye rises 14 studs and lands in 12 steps (800 ms of real time) ... The server decides the hop (the `Hop` intent) and the camera shows it." The held eye is the top of that hop (`Hop.RISE`, 14 studs, over the 9-stud walls) and its landing is that hop's own second half.
- *Movement*: "**Look:** the camera's yaw and pitch are free and never change the heading"; *Game speed*: the spy hop "is counted in steps, and its real-time numbers follow `GAME_SPEED`". The hold and the landing are counted in steps too, so they end exactly when the server's play starts.
- *Authority*: "The **server** simulates every maze. Clients send intents and render the replicated state."

### The behaviour
1. **Held at the top.** While a READY pause runs, the camera's eye is `View.SCALE.eyeHeight + Hop.RISE` (18 studs, 9 over the wall tops): the apex of a hop, held still. That is the pause at the start of a run (the capture's "while the game waits to start") and the pause of each new life after a death (the capture's "at the start of each new life"). **(prep)**, the user may veto: also the READY of a new level and of a new run after game over, because each is the same `"ready"` phase showing the same READY! banner, and a fresh level (new marked walls) is where a survey helps most; telling them apart would add state for a worse result.
2. **Free to look.** Every look input stays live while held: the mouse (the cursor stays locked in READY, `Flow.overlay` already says `cursor = false`), the touch look drag, the right stick and the arrow keys all move `look` exactly as in play, since the camera applies them before it computes the eye. Nothing is added to the look path; camera.spec pins that the right stick, the arrow keys (`Camera.arrows`, which d0149's touch circle also feeds through `point`) and the mouse turn the view while the eye is at the top (design review 001 F2). The touch drag (`Camera.lookBy`, which d0149 removes) is not pinned, so as not to fail d0149's correct change; it stays Observed (OC3).
3. **The landing.** The last `Hop.TICKS / 2` = 6 steps (400 ms of real time) of the pause bring the eye down the second half of an ordinary hop's parabola (`Hop.rise` of a hop that took off `Hop.TICKS` before play starts), so the drop starts with no vertical speed and lands with a hop's landing speed, and the eye is back at the standing height at the very tick play starts. The capture's "ends with a smooth drop ... into play".
4. **When it starts.** At the start of a run the first frame is already at the top. After a death, the dying pause stays on the ground where the player died (as today); the frame of the respawn's Ready event cuts the pose to the spawn (the existing teleport, `jumpTick`) and the eye to the top in the same frame, so one cut carries both.
5. **Play is unchanged.** After the landing the hop works as before; the jumps boon's long hop does not lengthen the landing (the landing belongs to the pause, not to a hop); the look is left where the player put it when play starts, as after any mouse look (the arrows spring back as they always do).

**Rejected alternative:** start the drop when play starts (the player airborne for the first steps of play). By f0101's rule "the player is airborne only while the eye is above the wall tops", that would need the server to make the player airborne at each start (no catches, no pellets eaten), which changes the game's rules at every start and needs a new server state; the capture asks only that the player "comes down into the normal game". Landing at the tick play starts keeps every rule, and the server, as they are.

### Interfaces (the pure/adapter split)
- **`Hop.held(playAt: number?, t: number): number`** (pure, `pickman/src/shared/Logic/Hop/init.luau`): studs above the standing eye at time `t` (steps, fractional, the camera's shown tick) while a run waits for play to start at step `playAt`. `Hop.RISE` while `playAt - t >= Hop.TICKS / 2`; else `Hop.rise(playAt - Hop.TICKS, t)` (the descent of a hop of `Hop.TICKS` landing at `playAt`; 0 from `playAt` on); 0 when `playAt` is nil or either value is not finite (the module's own `finite`). No new constant: the hold is `Hop.RISE` and the landing half of `Hop.TICKS`.
- **`View.readyEnd(last: number?, play: Session.PlayFrame, tick: number): number?`** (pure, `pickman/src/shared/Logic/View/init.luau`, placed after View's local `finite`): the step a READY pause ends, folded over frames. A frame with `play.phase == "ready"` at `tick` with `play.timer` steps left returns `tick + play.timer` (Game.step leaves the pause in the step its timer reaches 0, so the first frame in play has that tick: the run's first frame is tick 1 with timer 59, play at 60); any other frame, or one whose tick or timer is not finite, returns `last` unchanged, so the landing's last step (shown between the last ready tick and the first playing tick) still sees the pause's end after the latest frame says "playing". View already requires Session; no new dependency edge.
- **`Camera.ready(play: Session.PlayFrame, tick: number)`** (adapter, `pickman/src/client/Camera.luau`): `readyEnd = View.readyEnd(readyEnd, play, tick)`, with `local readyEnd: number? = nil` beside `hopped`. The render step's eye is written inline in the one CFrame assignment: `camera.CFrame = CFrame.new(x, View.SCALE.eyeHeight + Hop.rise(hopped, shown, hopTicks) + Hop.held(readyEnd, shown), z) * CFrame.Angles(...) * CFrame.Angles(...)`, with no new local (design review 001 F5: a local `eye` there would shadow f0235's module-level `eye`, which reads the drawn height back from `camera.CFrame.Position.Y`). The sum keeps the literal `View.SCALE.eyeHeight + Hop.rise(hopped, shown, hopTicks)` that f0101's and f0126's promoted pins and f0143's sequestered pin read; it equals the larger of the two because they never overlap: every READY pause's frames carry `hopped = nil` (each READY starts with a teleport, which clears it, and a hop is started only in play; the dying and level-clear pauses may still carry a hop's descent, but `Hop.held` is 0 outside a READY's window), and `Hop.held` is 0 from `playAt` on, while the earliest hop takes off in the step after it (ready.spec pins the first; held.spec the second).
- **`Main.client.luau`**: one line after `Camera.hopTicks(frame.hopTicks)`: `Camera.ready(frame.play, frame.tick)`.
- **Server**: nothing changes. The held eye is presentation only: in READY nothing moves and nothing is caught (`Session.step` skips movement, ghosts and contacts out of play, and `Game.died` refuses outside play), so seeing over the walls there decides nothing a client could lie about. The camera sends nothing (place.spec pins no `FireServer`).
- **Records**: game-design.md gains a bullet under *Rules* before *Glows*, starting `- **Waiting at the top** (f0234`, stating the hold in READY (run, life, and the prep choice of level and new run), free look, the landing down an ordinary hop's last half (6 steps, 400 ms) at the tick play starts, and **(prep)** that it is the camera's only: the server decides nothing new, the long hop does not lengthen the landing, the look is left where it is. The exact text the prototype used is in `experiments/prototype.patch`. architecture.md's Hop row gains "; the eye held at the top of a hop while a READY pause runs, and its landing as play starts (f0234)".

### Edge cases
- **No pause** (`readyTicks = 0`, `Game.IMMEDIATE_CONFIG`): no frame is ever "ready", `readyEnd` stays nil, nothing is held.
- **A pause shorter than half a hop**: the eye starts part way down the landing (continuous to the ground); cut up at the teleport like any respawn.
- **Simultaneous events**: a death or level clear mid-hop: the hop's descent still plays in the dying or level-clear pause (f0101, unchanged); the Ready that follows clears `hopped` and lifts the eye. A Hop pressed in READY starts nothing (ready.spec).
- **Respawn and teleport**: one cut for the pose and the eye (behaviour 4).
- **A player who leaves**: no client, nothing to draw. A late joiner gets its own session, which starts in READY: at the top.
- **Frame-rate independence**: the height is a function of the shown tick (from `os.clock` and the frames), never of `dt` (camera.spec renders the same clock at 1/30 and 1/240 s).
- **A lost or stale frame**: RemoteEvents arrive in order; a stale ready frame would name the same `tick + timer` anyway, since a pause's end does not move.

### Scalability
Per frame: one extra `View.readyEnd` per received frame (a comparison and an add) and one `Hop.held` per render step (a few arithmetic operations); constant in the maze size, the ghost count and the player count. Nothing new is replicated: the phase, the timer and the tick are already in every frame.

### Maintainability
The hold is data from Hop (`RISE`, `TICKS`) and the frame (`phase`, `timer`), not a new constant or a branch in the adapter. Another wait that should hold the eye (a future continue screen, f0145) is another phase `View.readyEnd` reads, with one rule (design review 001 F6): a phase `View.readyEnd` reads must begin with `hopped` cleared (a teleport, as every READY does), or Camera must take `math.max` of the two rises instead of their sum, which needs an override chore for the f0101 and f0126 eye pins. Another height source joins the one eye sum. f0235 ("Show power pills while jumping ... including the elevated wait of f0234") can read the same `Hop.held(readyEnd, shown)` (or the eye sum) that Camera computes; this issue promises nothing to it beyond these two public functions.

### Usability
- **Player**: the maze, the ghosts in the house, the pellets and the marked gold walls are visible from 9 studs above the wall tops for 3.6 s of every READY (the pitch reaches 80 degrees down), then a 400 ms drop that feels like the end of a hop lands the player exactly as the run starts, so control is never lost to the drop and the first step in play is on the ground. The READY! banner, the HUD and the glows are unchanged.
- **Developer**: two small pure functions with typed numbers in and out, nil-safe; one setter mirroring `Camera.hopped`; one line in Main.

## Acceptance criteria

### Checked
- AC1: `Hop.held(playAt, t)` is `Hop.RISE` until half a hop (`Hop.TICKS / 2` steps) before `playAt`, then `Hop.rise` of an ordinary hop landing at `playAt` (0.75 × `Hop.RISE` three steps out), never rising and moving at most a hop's own landing rate, 0 from `playAt` on, and 0 for a nil or non-finite input.
- AC2: `View.readyEnd(last, play, tick)` is `tick + play.timer` for a "ready" frame and `last` for any other or a non-finite one; over a real `Session.GAME_CONFIG` run it names the tick of the first frame in play after the run's start, a new life, a new level and a new run, and no pause frame carries a hop, even with Hop pressed in it or a hop under way when the pause began.
- AC3: Camera's real source on a stub engine, fed a real run's frames as Main feeds them: the eye is `eyeHeight + Hop.RISE` from the run's first frame and through the pause, equals `eyeHeight + Hop.held` at the shown tick through the landing, stands at `eyeHeight` once the first step in play is shown, never rises again; after a death it is on the ground while dying and at the top in the new life's pause, landing as play resumes; the right stick, a held arrow key and the mouse turn the view at the top; a hop in play lifts the eye by `Hop.rise`; the height at one clock is the same at 30 and 240 frames a second.
