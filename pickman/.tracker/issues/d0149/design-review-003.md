### F1 [high] The delayed tick can fold a snapshot of the fingers taken at the press: a tap then points the view 0.25 s later and stays pointed for ever, and all 18 adapters cases pass
Where: prototype `src/client/Controls.luau:224-231` (the `task.delay` callback folds the upvalue `fingers`); Rules and mutants row "a look finger schedules a tick after TAP_SECONDS" (C8, C11 only); adapters.spec "a thumb held still…", "a tap off the look circle's centre hops and never points the view" (it lifts at +0.1 s and never runs `frame()` again after the timer is due).
Evidence: probe on the prototype, a scratch copy of adapters.spec loading Controls with one edit: `local snap = fingers` before `task.delay(...)` and `Touch.tick(snap, os.clock(), controlsNow())` inside it (the natural closure: "tick the fingers as they were"). Result: all 18 sequestered adapters cases pass. One added case kills it: a look finger down at 0.6 r, `clock += 0.1`, lift (the hop), `clock += TAP_SECONDS`, `frame()`, expect no point; the mutant emits `point -1`. In play, the mutant resurrects the lifted finger as a looking finger: every tap swings the view a quarter turn 0.15 s after the hop and it never springs back (no lift will come for that id), and since the fold keeps that finger, "one finger a control" ignores every later finger on the look circle for the rest of the session. The same mutant resurrects a finger dropped by a focus loss or a re-layout in the 0.25 s window.
Critique: the tick's correctness rests on the callback reading the fold as it is when the timer fires, and no case runs a timer after its finger has gone. The tempting fix is the single "tap then frame" case; the stale state can also come in through the focus-loss and re-layout paths, which reset the fold in a different function.
Consequences: three cases, one per way a finger leaves before its timer (lift, focus loss, re-layout), each followed by a new finger on the look circle that must still work (so a resurrected finger that blocks the control is caught even if it emitted nothing). No new user decision.
Recommendation: add adapters cases (a) a tap lifted at +0.1 s, then `clock += TAP_SECONDS; frame()`: no point, and a new look finger then pulled left points 1; (b) the same with `focusLost()` instead of the lift (only the focus loss's `nil`); (c) the same with `relayout(moved)` instead of the lift. Add a Rules row "the timer folds the fingers as they are when it fires" with mutant C12 (the press-time snapshot), run through mutants.py.

### F2 [medium] Every HUD refresh (a shot, a life, the first pick frame) still snaps both knobs to their centres while the thumbs hold their pulls
Where: prototype `src/client/HudView.luau:142` (`lay` ends with `HudView.knobs({})`), called from `HudView.shots`, `HudView.show` (lives changed) and `HudView.pick` (first frame); d0149.md Design, HudView ("each layout centres them") and Scalability ("HudView re-lays only on a screen change", which review 002 F1 showed is false).
Evidence: probe on the prototype (scratch adapters case with the real HudView, `hudView(true)`): `h.hud.knobs({ steer = { x = b.steer.x - b.steer.r, y = b.steer.y } })`, then `h.hud.shots(2)`. Output: `knob before -14.4 after shots 44.1`: the SteerKnob jumps from the pull back to the centre. Controls keeps the finger (review 002 F1's fix), so the thumb still steers but its knob shows no pull until the next touch move; a look thumb held still keeps the view pointed while its knob sits in the centre. Every FIRE press that spends a shot does this. OC2 ("the knob follows the thumb") and the Usability line ("knobs show each pull") are not met, and no case fails: the HudView knob cases never re-lay with a knob out.
Critique: this is review 002 F1's mechanism one module over: a refresh treated as a re-layout. Controls now compares values, HudView doesn't. The tempting fix is to drop the `knobs({})` from `lay`, which leaves a knob drawn at stale pixels after a real turn until the next touch event.
Consequences: HudView keeps the last knobs it was given and re-centres only when its own controls moved (`Touch.same(new, buttonsAt)` false); after a real move, Controls' next event also sends `{}`, so the two agree.
Recommendation: in `lay`, compute the new `Touch.buttons(l)`, and re-place the last knobs if `Touch.same` with the previous layout, else centre them. Adapters cases with the real HudView: (a) a steer knob pulled, then `hud.shots(2)` and `hud.show` with a life fewer: the knob stays at the pull; (b) a knob pulled, then a turn (`screen(...)`): the knob is at the new circle's centre. Rules rows: "a refresh keeps the knobs" (mutant: `lay` always centres, today's code, killed by (a)); "a move centres them" (mutant: never centres, killed by (b)). Correct the Scalability sentence.
Lesson: pickman-arch/source-changes-compare-values

### F3 [medium] A held-still look thumb never points if the timer fires a hair before `os.clock()` reaches t0 + 0.25 s, and nothing re-arms it
Where: prototype `Controls.luau:226-228` with `Touch.tick`'s `t - f.t0 >= Touch.TAP_SECONDS` (`Touch/init.luau:236`); d0149.md Unknowns (no experiment on the timer).
Evidence: the tick is one-shot and compares two clocks the design doesn't prove agree: `t0` is `os.clock()` read while the input event is handled, and the timer is due by Roblox's scheduler clock, which resumes delayed threads at a frame step; if its "now" at registration is the frame's start (earlier than the `os.clock()` read in the same frame), it can resume when `os.clock() - t0` is still a few ms under 0.25, and `Touch.tick` then does nothing, for good, until the thumb moves. Even with one clock, the subtraction can round below: a Lune probe over 100,000 values `t0 = 1000 + i × 0.0137` found 10 where `(t0 + 0.25) - t0 < 0.25` (e.g. `t0 = 1023.7558` gives `0.24999999999988631`). The adapters stub fires at exactly `t0 + 0.25` on an integer-friendly clock (100 + 0.25), so it can't show either.
Critique: an engine-timing assumption with no experiment and no fallback (item 1). The tempting fix is a tolerance (`>= TAP_SECONDS - 1e-6`), which covers the rounding but not a scheduler that runs ms early.
Consequences: if the timer is the authority on "held long enough", the fold needs to know which finger the timer was for; ids are unique (`lastId` never resets, even on focus loss), and that must stay so or a timer would promote a later finger early.
Recommendation: make the timer the authority: the callback promotes the finger it was scheduled for, by id (`Touch.hold(state, id, ctx)`, or `Touch.tick(state, t, ctx, id)` that skips the clock compare for that id), so it fires whatever the clocks say; a lifted, forgotten or replaced id does nothing. Cases: fold — the hold for an id not in the fold, or not a look finger, or already looking, does nothing; adapters — a stub `delay` that runs its callback with the clock 1 ms short of `t0 + TAP_SECONDS` still points once; after a focus loss, a new finger's timer never promotes it before its own 0.25 s (mutant: `lastId = 0` on focus loss). Alternatively keep the clock compare and re-arm the remainder when the finger is still down and not due, with the same 1 ms-early case. Record the choice as an experiment with its fallback.

### F4 [medium] c0182 promises to keep pinned rules no harness can pin on both sides of d0149's `Controls.start` change
Where: `pickman/.tracker/issues/c0182/c0182.md` lines 25–27 and 34 ("every rule it pins that d0149 keeps stays pinned (… the FIRE shot's aim and intent, the PICK and boost intents…)", "replace a case that pins only a replaced control with one that pins what d0149 keeps in its place", "`main` stays green after this chore alone"); d0149's Overrides.
Evidence: c0182 lands on main before d0149 and must pass on both. The cases it must narrow drive Controls through the old signature: f0110's adapters calls `Controls.start(onSnap, nil, onTouch, buttons)`, f0126's controls.spec `Controls.start(onSnap, look, nil, onTouch, buttons)`. Under d0149's `Controls.start(onSnap, onTouch, buttons, onKnobs)` their hook lands in the wrong slot: on the prototype f0110's "a tap on the fire button sends the aimed shot…" and f0126's "a finger going down on the PICK button sends the pick" fail (sweep above), though FIRE and PICK by touch are rules d0149 keeps. No call fits both signatures, so these touch-FIRE and touch-PICK pins can't be "narrowed and kept"; they can only be removed, and d0149's adapters.spec ("FIRE sends the aimed shot…; PICK sends the pick") takes them over when d0149 lands. A replacement "that pins what d0149 keeps in its place" (f0027's swipe.spec) fails on main for the same reason. Two more traps for a harness rewritten to the new signature: a buttons hook without both circles crashes `Touch.same` on the second event (probe: hook `{ fire, pick }`, a FIRE tap → `Touch:254: attempt to index nil with 'x'`; f0126's hook is `{ snap, fire, hop, pick }`), and a look circle without a `task` stub crashes on `task.delay`.
Critique: c0182's prep will start from a promise it can't keep, and an executor may "narrow" a case into one that crashes, or try to keep PICK's touch pin and stall. The fix that tempts is to drop the promise, which loses the record of which d0149 case takes each pin over.
Consequences: the touch FIRE and PICK rules are unpinned on main between c0182 and d0149 (one dependency apart); the user's ordering already makes that window short.
Recommendation: in c0182, list the cases that are removed rather than narrowed because they drive the old `Controls.start` or Touch API, each with the d0149 sequestered case that pins its kept rule after d0149 lands (f0110 adapters FIRE → d0149 adapters "FIRE sends the aimed shot…"; f0126 controls PICK → the same case; f0143 swipe boost → "two quick pulls up boost"), and drop "replace… with one that pins what d0149 keeps in its place" for any rule whose new form exists only after d0149. In d0149, either type-guard `Touch.same` against a missing circle (nil circle equals only nil) with a fold case, or say in the Design that `buttons()` returns a full `Buttons` or nil, and note the `task` stub in pickman-test for any Controls harness with a look circle.
