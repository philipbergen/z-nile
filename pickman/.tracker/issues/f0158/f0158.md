# f0158: Danger radar ring

## Problem
The capture's own text (commit 68907d65, 2026-10-25; its `./zn land` did not reach main, which holds only the one-line stub, so prep restores it here word for word):

The user, 2026-10-25, answering f0109's radar question ("should the danger radar's walls turn green along with the maze?"): "Yes, and the danger radar needs to be more visible, make it more of circle segments with a circle of translucent indicators, keep it at a size roughly 60% of the smallest dimension (normally Y)".

Today the danger radar (d0105, `DangerHud`) is a small square map in the bottom-right corner (f0029's mobile layout keeps it clear of the touch jump button), and it's hard to see. What should happen:
- **More visible:** the radar is redrawn as circle segments with a circle (ring) of translucent indicators showing where danger is.
- **Size:** about 60% of the screen's smallest dimension (normally its height).
- **Walls follow the maze colour** while ghosts are edible (decided in f0109, which makes the radar walls use the maze's wall-colour rule).

## Non-goals
- The wall-colour rule itself: f0109.
- The touch controls' layout (f0110, d0149, d0151), beyond keeping clear of them.

## Solution
- Open for prep to put to the user: what each segment and indicator shows (direction and distance of each ghost? only dangerous ghosts? edible ghosts in another style?), where the ring sits (centred on the screen around the player's view, or in a corner), its translucency, and how it stays clear of the HUD (score, lives, shot bars, power bar, f0126's pick bar and boon banner, the touch buttons) at ~60% of the screen's smaller side.
- Desktop, touch and console all show it (the user's rule, 2026-10-25: "We support keyboard, console, and touch for every feature").

## Design
Decided with the user (2026-10-26): "Replace edge glows (Recommended)" (G1): the ring replaces the eight edge glows; the corner map stays.
Decided with the user (2026-10-26): "Accept all, but just a red or something to indicate, not a color match" (G2–G7): a lit sector is one warning colour, not the ghost's colour, more opaque the closer; 8 × 6 dots; lit 35–85% opaque, unlit 15%; dots 3.5% of the smaller side; f0126's pick bar and boon banner draw over the ring on short screens.
Decided with the user (2026-10-26): "Centre below top bar" (G8, design review 001 F1): keep the ring at 60% of the smaller side, but measure and centre it in the area below Roblox's top bar (the core-UI safe area), not the whole screen.
Decided with the user (2026-10-26): "Accept those overlaps" (G9): the ring stays 60% everywhere; exactly these controls may draw over its dots: FIRE on 480×320 (with and without the top bar), the radar on 320×568 under a 58 px bar, and the radar, the jump button and FIRE on 320×480 under a bar. Any other overlap fails.
Decided with the user (2026-10-26): "Accept these too" (G10, design review 002 F2): f0126's PICK button may also draw over the ring's dots, on exactly the seven screens where it does: 667×375, 480×320 (with and without the top bar), 320×480, 568×320 and 667×375 under a 58 px bar, and 1024×768 (touch). f0158 depends on f0126, so clear.spec checks the real PICK box. Any other overlap still fails.

**What the user asked.** A more visible danger radar: "circle segments with a circle of translucent indicators", about 60% of the screen's smaller side. Today DangerHud draws two things: a square map of the maze around the player in the bottom-right corner (d0105, `Hud.radarBox`), and eight glows on the screen's edge (f0102), one per direction a ghost is in, coloured as the nearest ghost there and brighter the closer it is. The user's G1 replaces the eight edge glows with the **danger ring**: one circle of translucent dots around the middle of the area below Roblox's top bar (G8), 60% of that area's smaller side across, cut into the same eight sectors; the square map stays in its corner, its walls following the maze colour rule (f0109).

**The ring (pure, `pickman/src/shared/Logic/Awareness/init.luau`).**
- `Awareness.RING = { share = 0.6, dot = 0.035, dots = 6, lit = { min = 0.35, max = 0.85 }, idle = 0.15 }`: the ring's outer diameter as a share of the screen's smaller side, a dot's side as a share of it, dots per sector, a lit dot's opacity from `min` (closeness 0: a ghost at the farthest indicated distance, 12 tiles along the maze) to `max` (closeness 1: on the player's tile), so G5's whole 35–85% is drawn; an unlit dot's opacity.
- `export type RingDot = { x, y, w, h: number, sector: number, lit: boolean, closeness: number }`.
- `Awareness.ring(list: { Indicator }, width: number, height: number): { RingDot }`: every dot of every sector (8 × `dots`), sector 0 (ahead) at the top and the sectors round to the left as `Awareness.sector` counts them, so a ghost to the left lights the left of the ring. Each sector spans an eighth of a turn as `dots` dots and one dot's gap; the dots' outer edge is on the circle of diameter `share` × the smaller side, centred in the `width` × `height` frame it is given, and every box is in that frame's pixels (DangerHud gives it the core-UI safe area, G8). Only a ghost that can kill (look `"normal"`) lights a sector: `Awareness.ring` keeps those indicators and hands them to `Awareness.glows` (f0102), so the nearest dangerous ghost in a sector (ties to the earlier) gives the sector's **closeness**, `1 - distance / Awareness.DEFAULTS.maxDistance` clamped to 0..1 (review 003 F1: not the glows' brightness, whose 0.3 floor would draw the farthest lit dot at 50%, not G5's 35%; DangerHud passes no `maxDistance`, so the indicators' cutoff and the ring's zero are the same 12 tiles), and a nearer frightened ghost never hides a farther dangerous one. Frightened and blinking (edible) ghosts and eyes never light the ring (prep's choice under the user's "just a red or something to indicate": the ring means danger; an edible ghost is not one). A side that is not a positive finite number counts as 0.

**The adapter (`pickman/src/client/DangerHud.luau`).** The edge glows' screen becomes the ring's (`Ring`, `ScreenInsets = CoreUISafeInsets`, `DisplayOrder = -1`, under the radar, the Flow overlay and the HUD): its frame is the core-UI safe area, below Roblox's top bar and the HUD's own frame (G8), so `area` (its `AbsoluteSize`, the Ring screen's own, not the radar screen's or the camera's viewport) is that area's size. Each render step it asks `Awareness.indicators` (inView) as today and `Awareness.ring(list, area.X, area.Y)`, and draws each dot as a round frame (one `UICorner`, radius scale 0.5) anchored at its box's top-left (the default `AnchorPoint` (0, 0); the radar's own `dot` helper anchors at the centre and is not used): a lit dot in the one warning colour `View.DANGER_COLOR` (255, 60, 30) at opacity `lit.min + (lit.max - lit.min) × closeness`, an unlit one white at `idle`. The Ring screen sets `ResetOnSpawn = false`, so a respawn keeps it (review 005 F1), and never sets `IgnoreGuiInset` (in Roblox that resets `ScreenInsets` to the device area, undoing G8; review 003 F2): place.spec reads the drawn screen's `ScreenInsets`, `IgnoreGuiInset` and `DisplayOrder` on the fake engine. It does no geometry (no sin, cos, atan, sqrt; f0033's rule). `Awareness.edgePoint`, `Awareness.glowBox` and `Awareness.GLOW` stay in Awareness (f0102's pure specs pin them) but DangerHud no longer calls them. The square map (`Awareness.radar`, `Hud.radarBox`) is unchanged; its wall dots take f0109's `View.wallColor` (f0158 depends on f0109).

**Coordinate spaces (review 001 F1).** Screen pixels: HudView's blocks (`Hud.layout(w, h, core)`: score, level, lives and shots, power bar), the snap control and Roblox's jump buttons (`Hud.jumpButtons` on the core-UI frame). The device safe area: the corner radar (`Danger` screen, `Hud.radarBox(…, bandTop)`), shifted to the screen by the device inset. The core-UI safe area: the ring (`Ring` screen, G8); its boxes are shifted to the screen by the core-UI inset. clear.spec measures every box in screen pixels after those shifts.

**Clearance.** Centred below the top bar, the ring clears the HUD's edge blocks, the snap control, the corner radar and the jump buttons on every f0029 sample screen (portrait and landscape, notched, under a 58 px top bar, touch desktop), on 480×320, 568×320 and 667×375 under a 58 px bar, and on the desktop sizes, on desktop and touch. On 844×390 under the bar the ring's top is at y 120, below the score and level blocks (y 67–85). **Accepted overlaps (G9, decided with the user 2026-10-26: "Accept those overlaps"). With the ring centred below the bar, three short screens still overlap:** 480×320, with and without a top bar (touch: f0110's FIRE button, below); 320×568 under a bar on touch, where the ring's lower-right dots meet the corner radar (lifted to y 305 above the jump buttons); and 320×480 under a bar, where they meet the radar (desktop at y 340, touch at y 217) and, on touch, the higher jump button (184, 344) and FIRE. Every one of these draws over the ring (the radar's screen is DisplayOrder 0, the HUD's 1, Roblox's buttons are core GUI), so nothing is hidden behind the ring and no control is blocked (the ring takes no input). clear.spec's ACCEPTED table names exactly these screens and boxes; any other overlap fails. ACCEPTED is a permission (the user's "may draw over"): that each accepted overlap still happens is checked only at f0158's `check` and `done`, where AC2's check.sh line sets `F0158_EXACT=1` (review 003 F3; zz-test/promoted-specs-pin-rules-not-sets). Once promoted, the case checks only that each ACCEPTED key names a real screen, so a later change that moves a control off the ring (d0149's circles, a smaller FIRE) is not an override. The ring is not shrunk. HUD overlays drawn mid-screen (f0126's pick bar at 0.4 of the safe height and boon banner at 0.3, not on main yet) may cross the ring on short screens; they draw over it (G6).

**f0110's touch buttons (review 001 F5; ownership set by the coordinator 2026-10-26).** f0110 (R0051, led by another session) lands first and adds FIRE and HOP (`Touch.buttons(l)`: `fire`, `hop`, in screen pixels like the HUD blocks). Following z-design/design-the-execution-window (one owner), **f0158 owns the clearance**: clear.spec adds `Touch.buttons(l).fire` and `.hop` to its touch boxes on every screen, and f0158 `depends_on` f0110. Measured with f0110's prototype (origin/proto-f0110 a042afce, whose `Touch.fireRect`/`hopRect` match its prep's Design) and the ring centred below the top bar: HOP clears the ring everywhere. **FIRE (308, 192, 70×70) sits under the ring's lower-right dots on f0029's 480×320 sample** (no top bar, so the core-UI area is the whole screen) and on 480×320 under a bar; it also meets the ring on 320×480 under a bar (a G9 screen already). Clearing FIRE on 480×320 would need a ring of at most 48% of the smaller side (154 px); at 60% (192 px) no placement of the sectors' gaps avoids it. The user accepted it (G9); the ring stays 60%.

**f0126's PICK button (review 002 F2).** f0126 (prepared) adds a touch PICK button one margin left of FIRE and level with it (`Touch.buttons(l).pick`, `Touch.pickRect`). Measured with f0126's prototype geometry (proto-wall-picks-touch 2396ce82), PICK sits under the ring on seven of clear.spec's fifteen screens. The user accepted them all (G10); f0158 `depends_on` f0126 and owns this clearance as it does FIRE's, so clear.spec reads `buttons.pick` with no escape when it is absent (the accepted-overlap case then fails). f0126's own document does not mention the ring; the coordinator tells f0126's owner that f0158 owns the clearance.

**The warning colour (`pickman/src/shared/Logic/View/init.luau`).** `View.DANGER_COLOR = { r = 255, g = 60, b = 30 }`, a red-orange: of the reds searched (R ≥ 200, G and B ≤ 90, steps of 5), it has the best worst-case WCAG contrast against every wall colour it can sit over: 2.53 against the maze blue (33, 33, 222) and f0126's gold (255, 215, 0), 3.37 against f0109's edible green (14, 64, 14), 3.55 against the white flash. Pure red (255, 0, 0) gets only 2.25 against the blue.

**Readable while the ghosts are edible (R0056's code review F4, relayed 2026-10-26).** f0109's edible green walls (14, 64, 14) are the user's colour and stay; on the corner radar's black backdrop they drop to 1.76:1 (2.34:1 blue), so the radar is dimmest exactly during the power period. The ring does not depend on the radar: it is centred over the 3D view, not on the radar's backdrop (only G9's overlap screens put the radar over a few ring dots, and there the radar draws on top). During the power period only revived ghosts (eyes back to `"normal"`) are dangerous, so a lit sector then means exactly the danger the player must not miss. Its warning colour keeps 3.37:1 against the edible green walls (AC4 pins at least 2.5 against all four wall colours, the edible green included), and an unlit dot is white at 15%. OC1 asks for the ring to be read during a power period with a revived ghost.

**Every input (the user's rule, 2026-10-25).** The ring is display only: desktop, touch and console show the same ring; nothing is bound.

### Scalability
48 dots, one frame each, reused every render step; `Awareness.ring` is O(48) with no allocation beyond its result list; the indicators and glows are computed once per step as today. No server cost.

### Maintainability
All geometry and numbers live in `Awareness` (`RING`), colours in `View`; DangerHud only draws. The ring reuses `Awareness.glows`, so a later change to which ghost lights a direction changes the ring and its tests at once.

### Usability
A ghost's direction is read at the middle of the screen, where the player already looks, instead of at the edge; the ring is thin and translucent so the maze ahead stays visible; idle dots outline the ring so a lit sector reads as a direction.
