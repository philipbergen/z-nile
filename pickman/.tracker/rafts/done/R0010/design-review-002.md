### F1 [high] The edge indicators ignore the tunnel wrap: a ghost chasing the player through the tunnel gets no arrow, and a ghost ahead through it gets an arrow pointing backwards
Where: f0033 Design, Indicators; prototype `Awareness.indicators`, which calls `Awareness.bearing(pose, g.x, g.y)` on raw coordinates while `pathDistance` wraps
Evidence: probe on the prototype. The player is at (2,14) in the left tunnel, and a ghost is at (26,14), 4 tiles away through the wrap (`distance = 4`).
- Looking east, the ghost is 4 tiles **behind** the player through the tunnel. `indicators` returns **nothing**: the raw bearing puts the ghost 24 tiles ahead, inside the field of view.
- Looking west, the ghost is 4 tiles **ahead** through the wrap. `indicators` returns one arrow with bearing `-pi`, **straight behind**.

The 3D world does not wrap (`GhostView` draws the ghost at `View.world(26, 14)`, on the far side of the maze), so in neither case can the player see the ghost. Meanwhile the radar, fixed in round 1, shows the ghost by the short way round. The two aids contradict each other, and the arrow disappears in the classic escape-through-the-tunnel situation. No spec puts a player and a ghost on opposite sides of the wrap for `indicators`.
Critique: pillar 3 ("where the danger is") and the AC1 promise that "a ghost behind ... gets an indicator" both fail here. The tempting fix is to copy the radar's short-way rule into `bearing`.
Consequences: the short way round alone is not enough. A ghost that is "ahead" only through the wrap is inside the field of view, so it would still get no arrow, and it is invisible. The rule therefore needs a second part: a ghost reached by the short way across the wrap is never on screen. The same "row that wraps" test as the radar applies (the ghost's row, `|dx| > width / 2`), so the two aids agree.
Recommendation: in `indicators`, take `dx` by the radar's rule (on a row that wraps, the short way round). When that crosses the wrap, indicate the ghost whatever its bearing (still subject to `maxDistance` and not eyes), with the bearing of the wrapped offset. State it in f0033's Design. Add the cases `awareness :: a ghost behind the player through the tunnel wrap gets an arrow pointing behind` ((2,14) looking east, ghost at (26,14): one indicator, `|bearing|` near pi, distance 4) and `awareness :: a ghost ahead through the wrap cannot be seen, so it gets an arrow pointing ahead` ((2,14) looking west: one indicator, bearing near 0). Add a Rules and mutants row with the mutants "raw bearing across the wrap" and "wrapped ghost suppressed by the field of view".

### F2 [high] A contact across the tunnel wrap is still not pinned: a wrap-blind `occupiedTiles` passes every check, and the player walks through a ghost in the tunnel (repeat of review-001 F3, part e)
Where: f0023 Design ("a contact across a tunnel wrap counts like any other"), Rules row "... a contact across the tunnel counts"; `contacts :: a ghost in a tunnel meets a player across the wrap`; `session-ghosts :: a ghost and the player meet across a tunnel wrap`; f0021 API `Ghosts.occupiedTiles` (wrapping not stated); f0020 `Ghosts.occupied` (does not wrap)
Evidence: neither case crosses the wrap.
- The contacts case puts the ghost at (27,14) with progress 0 and the player on (27,14): a same-tile meeting.
- The session case starts the player at (0,14) heading W with progress 100. Its occupied tile is already (27,14) before the step, and the ghost (progress 0 at (27,14), 15 units at tunnel speed) never leaves (27,14).

Mutant: in `Ghosts.occupiedTiles`, `table.insert(out, Ghosts.occupied(g))` (the public, unwrapped function) instead of the wrapped helper. All 135 cases of the six issues pass. The refutation said that a wrap-blind comparison "cannot be built by a one-line edit"; this is one line. It is also the call an executor would naturally write, since `occupied` is the public API and no document says that `occupiedTiles` wraps. Probe: the player is at (0,14) heading W with progress 50, and Blinky is at (27,14) heading E with progress 50. The prototype reports `Caught` (a swap across the edge). With the mutant there is no contact: the ghost occupies "(28,14)", and the two pass through each other.
Critique: AC1 and AC2 can be passed by a wrong implementation, and the game-design rule ("a contact across a tunnel wrap counts") is not enforced. The tempting fix is to move the existing case's numbers a little.
Consequences: the meaning of "occupied" must be one rule for the contacts, the radar and the snapshot. The ghosts' `x` in the snapshot stays unwrapped and continuous (`interpolateGhosts` relies on that), but `occupiedTiles` (and so `contacts` and `prev`) is always inside the grid.
Recommendation: state in f0021's API that `occupiedTiles` returns in-grid tiles (wrapped through tunnels), unlike `occupied`. Replace both tunnel cases with ones that cross the edge: contacts, a ghost walker at (27,14) E progress 70 against `playerNow = (0,14)` (kill); session, the probe above (player (0,14) W progress 50, ghost (27,14) E progress 50, then `Caught`). Rules row: mutant "unwrapped occupied tiles", run.

### F4 [low] f0022 and f0021 disagree on the speed of a blue ghost leaving the house, and the arcade's non-uniform random wandering is an unrecorded deviation
Where: f0022 Design ("Ghosts move at the hunting speed on the way out"); f0021 Design ("Which speed: ... frightened if blue"); prototype `speedOf`; `game-design.md` R0010 details ("wander at random (seeded)")
Evidence:
- A ghost that leaves blue (round 1's new rule) is `leaving` with `frightened = true`. The prototype gives it the frightened speed (19, not 28, at level 1), as f0021 says and f0022 denies. No spec pins either.
- The design draws a uniformly random allowed exit. The arcade draws a direction and, if that one is closed, takes the next in a fixed order, so some exits are favoured. Neither the game-design file nor the raft's vetoable list records this, although every other departure from the arcade is listed.

Recommendation: make f0022 say "at the speed `Ghosts.step` gives it (frightened if blue)", or say which speed wins, and pin it with a unit case. Add "frightened ghosts choose uniformly among the open exits (the arcade's choice is biased)" to the vetoable choices in `game-design.md` and in the Theme.
