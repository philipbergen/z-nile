### F1 [high] The wrap rule still looks only at the ghost's row: a ghost that has left the tunnel row gets an arrow pointing the wrong way, or none, and the arrow flips 180 degrees as the ghost steps on or off the row (repeat of review-002 F1)
Where: f0033 Design, Indicators ("the direction of a ghost is taken the short way round on a row that wraps"); prototype `offsetOf`, which tests `rowWraps(m, round(ghost.y))` only; the radar uses the same helper
Evidence: probe on the prototype, classic maze, the player looking east (`yaw = -pi/2`):
- The player is at (25,14) in the right tunnel. A ghost on row 14 at (6,14) is 9 tiles ahead through the wrap: one arrow, bearing 0.00 (right). The same ghost one tile up the column-6 corridor at (6,13), 10 tiles away along the maze, gets an arrow with bearing 3.09: **straight behind**. Ghost y 13.4 gives bearing 3.11 and y 13.6 gives 0.04, so as the ghost walks onto or off the tunnel row the arrow **jumps from the top edge to the bottom edge** between two frames.
- The player is at (2,14) in the left tunnel. Blinky comes down the column-21 corridor after them: at (21,14) it gets an arrow behind (bearing 3.14, distance 9), but at (21,13) (distance 10) and (21,12) (distance 11) it gets **no arrow**. Its raw position is ahead, inside the field of view, so it is suppressed, yet the 3D world draws it 19 tiles away behind walls.
- The radar misses the same ghosts: the player at (27,14) and a ghost at (6,13) are 7.07 tiles apart the short way and 8 tiles along the maze, inside the 7.5 reach, but there is no blip (raw `dx = -21`).

The reverse case works (the player at (6,12), the ghost at (25,14): bearing 1.79, correct), because only the ghost's row is tested. No spec puts the ghost off the tunnel row while the player is in it, so the prototype passes every case.
Critique: this is the situation of review-002 F1 one tile later. A ghost chasing the player into the tunnel comes down column 6 or 21 and turns onto row 14, so for the last tiles before the turn it has no arrow, or an arrow pointing away from it. Pillar 3 and AC1 ("a ghost behind ... gets an indicator") fail exactly when the player is fleeing. The tempting fix is to test the player's row as well (`rowWraps(player row) or rowWraps(ghost row)`).
Consequences: on the classic maze with `maxDistance` 12 the "either row" rule happens to give the right answer, because a path through the wrap of at most 12 tiles needs `|dx| > 14`, and every such pair has one end on row 14. But it is a coincidence of the layout and the default reach. `maxDistance` is a documented tuning option ("tuning is a number"), and with a reach of 16 or more, or a maze whose tunnel row is near a cross corridor, a geometric guess and the BFS can disagree again: the arrow would point one way while the distance was measured the other way. The rule that cannot disagree is the one the distance already uses: the BFS knows whether its shortest path crossed the edge (`Maze.neighbor` returns `wrapped`).
Recommendation: make `pathDistance` (or an internal twin) also report whether the shortest path crosses the wrap, and in which direction. In `indicators`, a ghost whose path crosses is always indicated, with `dx` shifted by `-+width` accordingly; otherwise the raw offset and the field-of-view test apply. Ties between equal paths are impossible within the reach on the classic maze, but state a rule anyway (prefer the raw offset). For the radar, which has no BFS, use "the player's row or the ghost's row wraps, and `|dx| > width / 2`"; state that it agrees with the indicators within the radar's reach. Add the cases: `awareness :: a ghost that has left the tunnel row ahead of the player through the wrap still gets an arrow pointing ahead` ((25,14) looking east, ghost (6,12): one indicator, `|bearing| < 0.5`, distance 11); `a ghost coming down a corridor behind the player through the wrap gets an arrow pointing behind` ((2,14) looking east, ghost (21,13): one indicator, `|bearing|` near pi, distance 10); `the arrow does not flip as a ghost steps off the tunnel row` (ghost at (6,13.4) and (6,13.6): bearings within 0.3 of each other); and a radar case (player (27,14), ghost (6,13): one blip). Rules row mutants: "only the ghost's row wraps" and "only the player's row wraps", both run.

### F2 [medium] The speed order in a tunnel is unpinned: a blue ghost at the frightened speed in the tunnel, or eyes slowed in it, pass every check
Where: f0021 Design, "Which speed" (eyes, then tunnel, then frightened, then normal); Rules row "A blue ghost is slower than a hunting one, and either is slower in a tunnel"; `frightened :: a frightened ghost moves at the frightened speed, a hunting one faster, and either is slower in a tunnel`
Evidence: the case's tunnel part sets `g.frightened = false` before stepping in the tunnel, so it only tests a hunting ghost. Two one-line mutants of `speedOf` on the prototype:
- frightened checked before the tunnel (a blue ghost in the tunnel moves 19 units at level 1 instead of 15): all 75 cases of f0021 and f0022 plus the unit spec pass;
- tunnel checked before eyes (eyes in the tunnel move 15 instead of 60): all 139 cases of the six issues plus the unit spec pass.

Both situations are common: blue ghosts are slowest and easiest to eat in the tunnel (the arcade rule the design states), and the eyes of a ghost eaten there start in a tunnel tile, so they would crawl out at a quarter of their speed.
Critique: the case's title claims "either is slower in a tunnel" and the row claims to kill "no tunnel slowdown", but neither holds for a blue ghost, and the eyes' "whatever" speed has no tunnel case. The tempting fix is to add a blue assertion only.
Consequences: the eyes' tunnel speed is a rule the user hasn't been asked about (the design chose "eyes ignore the tunnel"). It needs no decision, but it should be listed as the author's choice with the others in the Theme.
Recommendation: in the f0021 case, also step a blue ghost on (3,14) and expect 15, not 19. In f0022's eyes case (or a new `eyes :: eyes in a side tunnel still run at twice level 1's player speed`), put eyes on (3,14) heading east and expect 60. Rules rows: f0021 mutant "frightened beats tunnel", f0022 mutant "tunnel beats eyes", both run.

### F3 [low] Document drift that an executor will trip over
Where: f0021 API; the ghost types the sequestered specs use; f0022 Rules
Evidence:

Recommendation: remove the duplicate comment; add `export type Input`, `Event`, `Look` and `Contact` to the API blocks of f0021, f0022 and f0023; note in f0022's row that the chain's mutant is killed from f0023 on; add "no points shown where a ghost is eaten (R0015's HUD shows the score)" to the vetoable choices.
