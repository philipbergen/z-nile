### F1 [medium] The ghost's own x fraction is unpinned: dropping `frac(ghost.x)` from the offset passes every spec, and the arrow then steps once per tile instead of following the ghost
Where: f0033 Design, Indicators (`dx = (tile.x - here.x) + wrap * width + frac(ghost.x) - frac(player.x)`); Rules and mutants row "A ghost that cannot be seen because the maze wraps ..." (mutant "the fractions of the positions ignored"); `awareness :: the bearing is exact across the edge ...`, `the arrow does not flicker as a chasing ghost crosses the edge ...`
Evidence: I replaced `+ frac(g.x) - frac(pose.x)` with `- frac(pose.x)` in the prototype: `awareness.spec` passes 25/25. The exactness case only moves the player (the ghost stands at x = 0), and the chase sweep only asserts `|bearing| > 3.0` and the distance step. The mutant is wrong everywhere, not only at the edge, because every indicator goes through `offsetVia`: the player at (6,26) looking north, a ghost walking east along row 29 three rows behind. Prototype bearings for ghost x 4.25, 4.5, 4.75, 5.0, 5.25: 2.614, 2.678, 2.747, 2.820, 2.897 (smooth). Mutant: 2.554, 2.820, 2.820, 2.820, 2.820: frozen for a whole tile, then a 0.27 rad (15 degree) jump. The field-of-view test is decided on the tile centre too, so for half a tile an arrow can sit on a ghost on screen, or a just-off-screen ghost has none (the player at (13,23) looking north, a ghost at (8.5,20): prototype arrow at 0.983, mutant none). The Rules row lists "the fractions ... ignored" as killed; only the player's fraction is.
Critique: the documented formula is right, but the spec cannot tell an implementation that rounds the ghost to its tile from one that does not, and "take the offset from the tiles the search used" invites exactly that. The tempting fix is to make the existing exactness case use a fractional ghost on the tunnel row; that pins the edge but not the ordinary case above, which is where the stepping is visible every frame.
Consequences: none beyond spec cases; no rule changes. The prototype already passes the assertions below.
Recommendation: add a case `the arrow follows a ghost smoothly, not tile by tile` (or extend the exactness case): a ghost walking a row off the tunnel, e.g. (4.0 .. 5.25, 29) seen from (6,26) looking north, `bearing` within 1e-9 of `Awareness.bearing(pose, g.x, g.y)` at each step; and at the edge, a ghost at fractional x through the wrap (e.g. ghost x 27.3 and -0.3 on row 14, the player at (2,14) looking east), `bearing` equal to the bearing of the wrapped raw offset. Split the Rules row's mutant into "the player's fraction ignored" and "the ghost's fraction ignored", both run.

### F2 [low] The radar's walls are placed from the player's tile, its blips from the player's position: a blip sits up to half a tile off its corridor, and the whole wall pattern jumps a tile at each tile centre while the blips glide
Where: f0033 Design, Radar ("as offsets ... The player is the origin"); prototype `Awareness.radar` (`cx, cy = floor(pose + 0.5)`, walls at `turn(dx, dy)` with integer `dx, dy`; blips at `offsetShort(pose, g)`, raw)
Evidence: pose (10.4, 5, yaw 0), a ghost standing on (12, 5): the blip is at x = 1.6, while the wall dots around it are at integer offsets from tile 10 (the ghost's corridor is drawn at 2). Walking from x = 10.49 to 10.51 moves every wall dot by one tile in one frame while the blip moves 0.02. No spec uses a fractional pose for the radar, so either behaviour passes; the Design's "the player is the origin" suggests wall offsets from the player, but nothing says so.
Critique: the radar is the aid that shows "what is around you"; a radar whose walls tick by a tile while the ghosts glide reads as jitter, and a blip drawn between two corridors is ambiguous at small sizes. Observed O1 may not catch it at a glance. The tempting fix (snap the blips to tiles too) brings back per-tile stepping of the blips.
Consequences: subtracting the player's fraction from the wall offsets keeps the existing radar specs green (they use whole poses); with the tunnel row the wall loop already wraps by tile, so only the fraction changes. The radius test can stay on whole tiles (a dot near the rim appears or goes a frame later; harmless).
Recommendation: state in the Design that wall offsets are `(x - pose.x, y - pose.y)` of each wall tile's centre (the player's fraction subtracted), as the blips are; add a case: pose (1.4, 5, 0), radius 3, the wall at (2,4) is at offset (0.6, -1).
