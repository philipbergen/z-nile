### F1 [medium] The adapter spec still passes two wrong placements: a cache that never updates, and an anchor point left on the radar (partial repeat of review-001 F1)
Where: tracker/issues/d0105/acceptance/adapter.spec.luau, the case "places the radar from the screen size before the snapshot guard, and only when that size changes" and the case "the old top-right corner is gone"; d0105.md Rules and mutants, the rows "The box is recomputed only when `AbsoluteSize` changes" and "The old top-right anchor and position are absent"; design-refute-001.md F1.
Evidence: I tried two mutants on the prototype, and both passed:
- Deleting `placedX, placedY = view.X, view.Y` from inside the size `if` leaves the `if` but never updates the cache, so `radarBox` (one `layout` and two tables) runs on every frame. That is the cost the caching decision exists to avoid. `lune run tests/run -- tracker/issues/d0105/acceptance/adapter.spec.luau` reports 4 passed, 0 failed. The spec matches the `if` line, the call and the assignment as strings. It never checks that the cache is written, so the Rules row "recomputed only when `AbsoluteSize` changes" is marked `run` against the one mutant it does catch (no `if` at all), not against the rule.
- Adding `radar.AnchorPoint = Vector2.new(1, 1)` after `radar.Name = "Radar"` also passes. The adapter spec and f0033 `adapters` report 11 passed, 0 failed. `radarBox` returns the top-left corner, so with that anchor the radar is drawn 120 pixels up and to the left of its box: it no longer sits one margin above the bar or one margin from the right edge, and AC3's claim that the radar is placed from `Hud.radarBox` is false. The "old corner is gone" case looks only for the exact literals `Vector2.new(1, 0)` and `UDim2.new(1, -16, 0, 16)`.
Critique: this is the second round in which a string-order spec over `DangerHud`'s source has a hole. The tempting fix is to add two more literals: `placedX, placedY = view.X, view.Y` between the `if` and the call, and the absence of `radar.AnchorPoint`. That closes these two mutants. But every rule about what happens at run time then becomes another exact string, and the next spelling (`placedX = view.X; placedY = view.Y`, or `Vector2.one`) gets past the spec or fails a correct implementation.
Consequences: there are two ways out, and each brings its own work. Running the real `DangerHud` source under `tests/lib/fakeengine.luau` would check behaviour: after a step with no snapshot, `radar.Position` equals `radarBox` of the screen and `AnchorPoint` is `(0, 0)`, and a second step at the same size leaves `Position` alone. But that fake is built for the lobby adapters. Extending it to `RenderStepped`, `workspace.CurrentCamera` and `AbsoluteSize` falls under its own rule that f0061's mutants are re-run whenever it changes, which is out of proportion for a 30-line defect. The other way is to stop checking the caching rule. A `layout` call per frame costs one 5-field table and one 4-field table, so the rule is a design preference, not an acceptance property. Then Scalability must say that the cache is reasoned, not checked.
Recommendation: keep the source-shape spec only for what source shape can actually pin: the call before the guard's `return`, the call's arguments, and no CoreGui switch. Add one assertion that the source does not contain `radar.AnchorPoint`, because the radar must keep the default top-left anchor that `radarBox`'s box assumes. Say so in the Files and API section, and give it a Rules row whose mutant is "an AnchorPoint on the radar". For the caching rule, choose one of these and record the choice in E3 and the Rules table:
- (a) Mark the row `reasoned`, with the never-updated-cache mutant listed as the one the spec does not catch, and say in Scalability that one comparison per frame is the design and a lost cache would cost one `layout` per frame.
- (b) Assert `placedX, placedY = view.X, view.Y` between the `if` and the call, and accept that it pins a spelling. The Rules row must then name that mutant and be re-run.
Do not extend the fake engine in this raft.

### F2 [low] "The two cannot drift apart" holds only where the HUD's ScreenGui has no safe-area inset on the right or the bottom
Where: d0105.md Maintainability ("so the two cannot drift apart as long as both read `layout`"), Usability, AC2.
Evidence: `HudView`'s ScreenGui leaves `IgnoreGuiInset` at its default (false), so as I understand Roblox's `ScreenInsets`, its frame is inset by the core UI safe area. `DangerHud`'s ScreenGui sets `IgnoreGuiInset = true`, so it uses the full screen. On a desktop the only inset is the top bar. The power bar is anchored to the bottom by scale, so the bar and the radar agree, which is what E1 models. On a phone in landscape with a notch and a home indicator, the safe area also insets the right (or left) and the bottom. `HudView`'s bar then moves up and inward while the radar stays at `width - margin`, `height - (barHeight + 2·margin)`. With a bottom inset larger than `margin`, the bar's top rises past the radar's bottom edge, and they overlap while power runs.
Critique: mobile layout is a non-goal (f0029), so this is not a defect of this raft. But the Maintainability sentence states the property more widely than it holds, and f0029 will inherit it as a fact.
Consequences: none for this raft's code.
Recommendation: qualify the sentence ("on screens without a side or bottom safe-area inset; the two ScreenGuis differ in `IgnoreGuiInset`"). Extend the f0029 non-goal line to name safe-area insets next to the jump button.

### F3 [low] The radar now covers the edge arrows of ghosts behind and to the right, not those ahead and to the right
Where: d0105.md Usability ("The arrows are unchanged"), Observed O2.
Evidence: from `Awareness.edgePoint` at 1280×720 (aspect 1.78), an arrow lands on the right edge at `v = (dv/du)·aspect`. The radar spans y 555–675, which is about v 0.46–0.95 once the 56-pixel arrow's half size is included. That is a ghost about 14° to 28° behind the right-hand side. Both are in the same ScreenGui, so the arrow and the radar overlap there. The old top-right box overlapped arrows for ghosts ahead and to the right but out of view. The change is not worse in area, but it moves the overlap onto chasing ghosts, which are the ones O2 is about.
Critique: the arrows are created after the radar, so they most likely draw over it, and the ghost also shows as a blip when it is within the radar's radius. The visible risk is small. But "unchanged" is not quite true, and O2 does not look at that bearing.
Consequences: none for the code.
Recommendation: in Usability, say that a ghost behind and to the right puts its arrow over the radar's left or top part. Add to O2: "a ghost behind and to the right: its arrow is still visible over the radar".
