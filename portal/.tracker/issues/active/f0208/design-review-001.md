### F1 [high] A store read that hangs past CHECK_SECONDS fails the play instead of letting the player in, and server.spec pins the failure
Where: f0208.md §5 "The play" / "The check" (line 157), Edge cases "The read is busy" (line 198), first Guess (line 27); `acceptance/server.spec.luau:655-672` ("a check still busy after CHECK_SECONDS fails, with no teleport and no pass"); Rules and mutants row "busy reads again, at most CHECK_SECONDS" (line 338).
Evidence: Wallet keeps one slot per Player object and title, and f0205's `Cards` and this issue's `Plays` share it. f0201's prototype `Wallet.read`: while `slot.reading` it answers `nil, "busy"`; a read not back within `WAIT_SECONDS` (5 s) answers `"down"` to its own caller and sets `slot.mayPass = true`, but "the call ends later; until then the player stays busy". `Cards` reads every published title when the screen opens and at every ask, so in practice the read in flight is usually Cards'. When the store is slow (a `GetAsync` that hangs longer than 10 s: a throttle queue, an outage), every read `Plays` makes answers busy, and after `CHECK_SECONDS` the play is `failed` ("COULD NOT START. TRY AGAIN."). Each retry does the same until the hung call ends. The first Guess says "a read of the player's record that fails (the store down or slow) lets the player go on the portal's pass", and G10, quoted in the design, says "a store that cannot be read or written lets the player in". The server case asserts `#w.passes == 0` after the 10 s, so an implementation that follows G10 fails AC3.
Critique: In the portal, a busy that lasts past `WAIT_SECONDS` can only come from a late read, because `Plays` reads one at a time and the portal uses no other Wallet in-flight path (no revives). That means the store is slow, and the user's rule for a slow store is the pass. Lowering `CHECK_SECONDS` or reading again for longer looks like a fix but is not one, because the hang has no bound.
Consequences: The pass after a long busy is accepted, because `Wallet.pass` writes only when `mayPass` is set and the late read has already set it. If the hung read later returns a record that says "buy", the player has had one free play, which is G10's "Let them play free", the user's own rule. The relation `CHECK_SECONDS > Wallet.WAIT_SECONDS` becomes load-bearing. A busy that ended before any read went late would pass without `mayPass`, so `Wallet.pass` would refuse, the player would go without a pass and the game place would send them back. AC3's wording changes in both the document and `check.sh`.
Recommendation: A check that is still busy at `CHECK_SECONDS` goes on the portal's pass in every mode, as the first Guess says: `Wallet.pass`, then go on. Put it in Logic, for example `Play.decide(read, problem, mode, waited)`, where `"busy"` with `waited >= CHECK_SECONDS` answers `"pass"`. State in §5 and in Edge cases that `CHECK_SECONDS` must exceed `Wallet.WAIT_SECONDS` (5) and why. Replace server.spec's case with "a check still busy after CHECK_SECONDS writes the pass, then teleports" (one pass, written before the teleport). Add a play.spec case for the decision, and a Rules row with the mutant "a long busy fails". Keep S11 and S12 (no failure at once; the busy loop is bounded). Update AC3's text in f0208.md and check.sh together.

### F2 [high] One client can loop 2 PLAYERS, INVITE and CANCEL with no bound; each loop writes one MemoryStore entry and broadcasts twice, and a drained MemoryStore quota lets every "buy" arrival in the experience play free
Where: §5 intents and "Going on: pair" (lines 155, 156, 162); §6 `offer` (line 173); Scalability (line 213: "Per wait: at most one invite `SetAsync`"); `acceptance/server.spec.luau:1065-1086`.
Evidence: The portal never uses up a free start or a held play, and a free start is due on each title once an hour. While the player has either, `Wallet.read` answers from the record it saw under 60 s ago without yielding. So `("play", "invaders", "pair")` runs check, go, `Pairing.join`, `waiting` and `FireAllClients("waiting", …)` without yielding. `"invite"` spawns `Invites.offer` (one `SetAsync`) because `run` makes a new slot with `invited = false` on every play. `"cancel"` runs `unwait` and `FireAllClients` again. A client that fires `[play, invite, cancel]` in a loop therefore causes one MemoryStore write and 2 × N client messages per loop, limited only by how fast it can fire remotes. MemoryStore's quota is shared by the whole experience ("1000 + 120 × [number of concurrent users] request units per minute", E4). f0201's arrival counts a pass map that "cannot be read" as a pass ("Let all of them play"). So while the loop runs, a throttled quota makes every arrival in every game place that should buy play free instead, and it also fails ARENA starts (sixth Guess) and other players' invites. A bound "per wait" is not a bound in time, because a wait costs nothing.
Critique: Lesson zz-arch/client-triggered-reads-guarded asks how many store calls one client causes in a minute of back-to-back requests. The design answers per wait, and waits are free. The tempting fix, "one invite per player per session", would break a second legitimate invite later in the same session.
Consequences: A bound needs per-player state that outlives the wait (dropped on leave), and a rule for a press inside the bound (dropped, like other dropped presses). If broadcasts are coalesced instead, other clients see "A PLAYER IS WAITING" up to one tick later.
Recommendation: (1) Invites: remember per player the last (job, title) written and when it was written. Write again only when that changed, or after a span such as `Invites.SECONDS / 2`. Case: N back-to-back wait, invite, cancel cycles inside the span make one `SetAsync`; one after the span writes again. (2) The lines: put a per-player cool-down on joining a line (a `Play` constant), or send the counts at most once per heartbeat tick when they changed. Case: many join and cancel cycles in one tick send a bounded number of broadcasts. (3) Restate Scalability per client per minute, not per wait. (4) Add Rules rows whose mutants remove each bound.
Lesson: zz-arch/client-triggered-reads-guarded

### F3 [medium] The writes between the decision and the teleport have no time limit, and the stubs never yield inside them, so the leave, flight and re-entry rules the design states for them are unpinned
Where: §5 "The purchase" (line 158), "Going on" (line 161), "A leave" (line 164); the stub `UpdateAsync` (`server.spec.luau:179-195`) and the stub `wallet.pass` (`:244-247`), neither of which yields; the receipt-wait cases (`:542-585`).
Evidence: (a) Because the arena write and the pass write never yield in the stub, no case can leave during them. The prototype's `if not current(player, flight) then return end` after the arena `pcall` can be deleted with every case still green. (b) The 30 s receipt delay tests `current(player, flight) and now.phase == "buying"`. The mutant that drops the flight test survives, because none of the three receipt cases starts a later purchase. The failing path: Snake's screen closes bought at t=0, the grant at t=1 starts the teleport, the teleport fails, and at t=5 the player presses Invaders PLAY with nothing held and is `buying` again. At t=30 the old delay sets `idle` in the middle of that purchase, so the Invaders receipt becomes a held play and nothing starts. (c) During the arena write that follows a grant, the phase is still `buying`. A second play grant for that title (an older receipt retried while this one is open) therefore enters `proceed` again: two `UpdateAsync` calls (two seats counted), and the second `go` finds Travel `going` and sets the player `failed` while the first teleport runs. (d) Neither the arena `UpdateAsync` nor `Wallet.pass`'s `SetAsync` has a time limit (f0201's `pass` has none). If MemoryStore hangs, the player stays in `checking` or `buying` with every press dropped and no way out. (e) No case has a player rejoin, so dropping `Travel.left` survives.
Critique: §5 promises that "a read, a grant, an arena write or a delay that returns after the leave sends nothing", and the Lessons pass cites zz-arch/spends-recheck-after-the-write and pickman-arch/open-stores-guarded. But nothing can fire an event inside these writes, and the open-stores lesson's time-limit half is missing. Adding only a leave case with a yielding stub leaves (b), (c) and (d) open.
Consequences: A time limit on the arena write needs a stated meaning for a late answer: the count was already incremented, so the seat is lost and the arena closes early, which §3 already accepts. A time limit on the wait for the pass needs one too: an arrival during a MemoryStore hang finds the map unreadable and plays. Marking the slot before the writes (a `going` phase, or a field) changes what the card shows during them.
Recommendation: Make the stub `UpdateAsync` and `pass` yield until the case releases them. Add these cases:
- a leave during the arena write sends nothing and leaves no Travel entry;
- a second grant during the arena write makes no second write and no second teleport;
- the receipt wait's end after a later purchase began leaves that purchase open;
- a press during each write is dropped;
- a rejoin after a leave plays at once.
Mark the slot as going on before any yield in `proceed`. Bound the arena write and the wait on the pass write (for example by `Wallet.WAIT_SECONDS`), and say what a late answer means. Add a Rules row and mutant for each.
Lesson: zz-arch/spends-recheck-after-the-write; pickman-arch/open-stores-guarded

### F4 [medium] "COULD NOT START" never clears, and only teleport failures cool down, contrary to the tenth Guess and Usability
Where: tenth Guess (line 37), §5 "Start" (line 163), §1 "The card's view" (line 119), Usability (line 219); `client.spec.luau:593-610`.
Evidence: The Guess says the note shows "until Travel's 3 s cool-down ends", and Usability says a failed start "can be tried again 3 s later". §5 and the prototype set `failed`, and nothing ever sets `idle` again, so the note stays until the player's next play, hours later if need be. The view enables PLAY under the note at once, so a press inside the 3 s is dropped by the server with no status: the card looks dead. Failures that never reached Travel have no cool-down at all: a purchase screen that cannot open, a failed arena write, and today's busy timeout. PLAY retries at once, so during a MemoryStore outage a held key on ARENA makes one `UpdateAsync` per press.
Critique: The design does not implement this Guess, and no spec pins it either way. The user will rule on text that is not what gets built.
Consequences: Clearing the note needs a server timer (the once-a-second heartbeat exists) and a rule for which status follows. Showing the buttons disabled during the cool-down changes the client cases. A cool-down for failures outside Travel needs a per-slot deadline.
Recommendation: Implement the Guess. Every failure starts a 3 s cool-down: `Travel.failed` for teleport failures, a slot deadline for the others. The buttons show disabled during the cool-down, and the heartbeat sends `idle` when it ends. Cases: the note clears at 3 s; a press inside the cool-down is dropped and drawn disabled; a failed arena write retried within 3 s makes no second `UpdateAsync`. The alternative is to reword the Guess and Usability to what is built (the note stays until the next play; a press within 3 s does nothing) for the user's ruling. Add Rules rows either way.

### F5 [medium] OC1's purchase steps cannot be reached in the playtest it describes
Where: Observed OC1 (line 396).
Evidence: Nothing in the portal claims a free start. The game place's `Wallet.arrive` does (Interfaces, line 223), and in OC1 the teleport's "destination need not run", while Snake's arrival (f0213) does not exist at f0208's time. The playtest is an unpublished `rojo build` file, so the test store is Wallet's per-server stand-in (f0201 E8), fresh on every playtest. So the free start is due on every visit, and Snake's PLAY goes at once every time. "Snake's PLAY on a later visit opens the test purchase screen … CANCEL brings the buttons back, and BUY starts the teleport" never happens. The human never sees the purchase path, which is the one place this design's prices and states meet Roblox's real screen. f0201's E8 also names "f0208's or c0202's Observed steps" as the first playtest of the library that buys with API access off.
Critique: An Observed step a tester cannot perform turns into a silent skip.
Consequences: Seeding a record needs a store the tester can reach: a published place with Studio API access on, or a route into the stand-in.
Recommendation: Give OC1 a setup that reaches the purchase screen. For example: in a playtest of the published portal with Studio API access on, run `game:GetService("DataStoreService"):GetDataStore("SnakePaidPlayTest"):SetAsync("<your user id>", { free = os.time(), plays = 0, continues = 0, receipts = {} })` in the server command bar before pressing (store name from f0201's Catalog); Snake's PLAY then opens the test screen. Also say whether OC1 is the playtest that answers f0201's E8 (API access off), and if it is, how it buys there.

### F6 [medium] E3 and E6 miss the teleport guide's Known limitations: the server kind misreads in Team Test and Studio, and Roblox recommends retrying reserved-server teleports
Where: E3 (line 254), E6 (line 257), Interfaces "The server kind" (line 224), seventh Guess (line 33), tenth Guess (line 37), OC3 (line 398).
Evidence: create.roblox.com/docs/projects/teleport, Known limitations (read 2026-11-15):
- "PrivateServerId and JobId: In Test mode, both are always empty, even for private servers."
- "In Team Test, every server is private, so PrivateServerId is never empty, even for teleports to public servers."
- "Code that checks whether PrivateServerId is empty behaves differently in Team Test than in production."
- "ReservedServerId vs. PrivateServerId: These values can differ in Team Test, though they match in production."
- "Some failures benefit from retries, particularly those involving reserved servers, so we recommend retrying some number of times on failure."

E6 calls a reserved server's `PrivateServerId` "not documented", and its fallback (`PrivateServerId ~= ""` alone) is exactly the check the guide says misfires in Team Test. f0212, f0218 and f0222 will rely on this interface. In Team Test (OC3's mode) they will see every Snake and Invaders server as shared, and in Studio Test every reserved server as solo. The tenth Guess ("nothing retries by itself") does not mention Roblox's advice, and for a pair one transient failure ends the pairing.

In Interfaces, record that the server kind holds in production only (Team Test: every server reads as private; Studio Test: none does), so f0212, f0218 and f0222 must not rely on it in Team Test or Studio. Replace E6's fallback. Put the retry advice beside the tenth Guess so the user rules knowingly; alternatively, retry a pair's reserved-server teleport once before failing both, with a case and a row.

### F7 [medium] An invite does not hold the wait for the friend: a stranger can take the inviter, and the invite record outlives the wait
Where: ninth Guess (line 36), §5 "Going on: pair" (line 162), §6 (lines 173-174).
Evidence: INVITE writes `{ job, name }` for 600 s, and nothing removes it. While the friend accepts and loads (tens of seconds), any other player in the server who presses 2 PLAYERS on that title is paired with the inviter, because `Pairing.join` takes the first player waiting. The inviter then leaves, and the friend lands in, or is followed to, a server where nobody waits for them, with nothing to tell them. A friend who arrives after the inviter cancelled or left is still teleported to that server for up to 600 s. The captured outcome is that "a second player in the portal server (or an invited friend) joins, then both teleport together".
Critique: In a busy portal this race is likely, and the flow that loses is the one the inviter chose.
Consequences: Holding the wait for the friend means strangers cannot pair with an inviting player (a second line, or a held flag with a time limit). That is a product decision. Removing the record adds one MemoryStore call when an invited wait ends, bounded by F2's span.
Recommendation: State a Guess (pending user) on whether an invited wait is held for the friend, and for how long, and design and pin either answer. In every case, remove the invite record when the wait ends (cancel, pair, leave), with `RemoveAsync` in a `pcall`, so a friend is followed only to an inviter who still waits. Add cases and Rules rows (mutant: the record kept after the wait).

### F8 [low] place.spec pins exact sets that later portal work may extend

Keep L1, L2 and L6 killed. Keep the exact seven keys only if they are meant as a permanent contract, and say so.
Lesson: zz-test/promoted-specs-pin-rules-not-sets

### F9 [low] `Arena.open` rejects a record opened slightly "in the future", so a small clock skew between portal servers splits arenas
Where: §3 (line 142); the A3 row.
Evidence: Take two portal servers whose `os.time()` differ by 1 or 2 s. The lagging server sees the other's new arena as `opened > now`, gets nil, and overwrites the shared record with a new arena. The first arena keeps the single player sent to it.
Recommendation: Tolerate a small skew (for example, `opened` up to 30 s ahead counts as now) and still reject anything further ahead. Add cases at the edge of the tolerance, and keep A3 for values beyond it.

### F10 [low] `buying` never ends if no close arrives
Where: §5 "The purchase" (line 158); Unknowns.
Evidence: Only `closed(…)` ends `buying`, and only a bought close has the 30 s wait. If Roblox never reports `PromptProductPurchaseFinished` for a prompt that fails to show, the slot stays `buying`. The card then shows only "COMPLETE THE PURCHASE", and every press on every card is dropped until the player rejoins. This assumption about Roblox is not listed as an unknown.
Recommendation: List it as an unknown with a fallback, either a long bound on `buying` when no close arrives, or a rule that a press on any card while buying with no screen open ends the purchase. Pin it with a case.
