## Problem
Pillar 3 says the player can always tell where the danger is. In first person with free look, that's false: a ghost behind the player, or around the next corner, is invisible until it's too late. Top-down Pac-Man shows the whole board, so its difficulty tables assume full information. Without an aid, the classic ghost AI will likely feel unfair in first person.

**Decided by the user (2026-10-01):** a **minimap/radar** and **edge indicators**, where an indicator must give "some sense of distance not just direction, bigger or brighter as it gets closer". (Slower ghosts than the classic tables are a separate decision: the tuning factor of f0200.)

## Non-goals
- Ghost rendering (f0310). Changing ghost AI. Audio and the proximity vignette (not chosen).
- A radar that shows pellets or the whole maze: it shows the walls and the ghosts around the player.

## Solution
- Any aid reads only the replicated snapshot, so it adds no new authority.
- The pure layout and selection logic (which ghosts get indicated, their size and brightness, the radar projection) lives in a testable feature, `Awareness`; a thin client adapter, `DangerHud`, draws what it returns.
- Playtest on Beta before choosing the tuning factor of the classic speed tables.

## Design
### Files and API
- `src/shared/Logic/Awareness/init.luau` (new feature; depends on `Maze` only, a new edge declared in `tracker/architecture.md` in this prep).
- `src/client/DangerHud.luau` (new), `src/client/Camera.luau` (`Camera.pose`), `src/client/Main.client.luau` (wiring).
```lua
export type Look = "normal" | "frightened" | "blinking" | "eyes"
export type Ghost = { x: number, y: number, look: Look }          -- tile coordinates, as in the snapshot
export type Pose = { x: number, y: number, yaw: number }          -- yaw as View: 0 looks north (-y), positive turns left
export type Options = { verticalFieldOfView: number?, aspect: number?, maxDistance: number?, minSize: number?, maxSize: number?, minBrightness: number? }
export type Blip = { x: number, y: number, look: Look }
export type Radar = { radius: number, walls: { { x: number, y: number } }, blips: { Blip } }
export type Indicator = { index: number, bearing: number, distance: number, size: number, brightness: number, look: Look }
Awareness.DEFAULTS = { verticalFieldOfView = 80 degrees (as View.SCALE.fieldOfView), aspect = 16/9, maxDistance = 12, minSize = 0.25, maxSize = 1, minBrightness = 0.3 }
Awareness.RADAR_RADIUS = 7
Awareness.bearing(pose, x, y): number                    -- (-pi, pi], positive to the left
Awareness.edgePoint(bearing, aspect): (number, number)   -- (u, v) on the screen border in -1..1, u right, v down; 0 is up, pi is down
Awareness.pathDistance(m, from: Tile, to: Tile, limit: number): number?   -- tiles along the ghosts' routes, wrapping tunnels; nil beyond `limit`
Awareness.indicators(m, pose, ghosts, options?): { Indicator }            -- outside the field of view, within maxDistance along the maze, not eyes; nearest first
Awareness.radar(m, pose, ghosts, radius?): Radar   -- offsets from the player, up = where the camera looks
```
- **Indicators**: a ghost is indicated when it is not `eyes`, its bearing is outside half the camera's **horizontal** field of view, `2 * atan(tan(verticalFieldOfView / 2) * aspect)` (Roblox's `FieldOfView` is the vertical one: 80 degrees is about 112 across on a 16:9 screen and about 122 on a phone, so an arrow never sits on a ghost you can see; the adapter passes the real aspect and the camera's field of view every frame) and its path distance to the player's tile is within `maxDistance` (12 tiles). `closeness = 1 - clamp(distance / maxDistance, 0, 1)`; `size = minSize + (maxSize - minSize) * closeness`, `brightness = minBrightness + (1 - minBrightness) * closeness`: **strictly growing and brightening as the ghost nears**. The distance is along the maze (a breadth-first search over ghost routes), because a ghost behind a wall is not close. Indicators are sorted nearest first (ties by index). **The tunnel wrap**: the breadth-first search also reports whether the shortest path crosses a tunnel wrap and in which direction; the ghost's offset is then taken along that path: `dx = (tile.x - here.x) + wrap * width + frac(ghost.x) - frac(player.x)` with `frac(v) = v - floor(v + 0.5)`, so it is **continuous when either position crosses the edge of the map** (positions are not wrapped, tiles are: a ghost heading east runs on from 27.0 to 27.99 before it is back at tile 0). A ghost is **unseen**, and then always indicated, when the 3D positions (which do not wrap) are on opposite sides of the maze from where the path puts it, `|(ghost.x - player.x) - dx| > width / 2`, and (subject to the reach and not eyes) with the bearing of that offset: it cannot be seen, whatever its raw bearing. This follows the *path*, not the ghost's row, so a ghost one row off the tunnel, or coming down a column towards the tunnel, gets the right arrow and the bearing does not flip as its y crosses a tile boundary. A ghost 4 tiles behind through the tunnel gets an arrow pointing behind; one 10 tiles ahead through it gets an arrow pointing ahead.
- **Radar**: the walls of the tiles within `radius` (7) of the player and the ghosts within `radius + 0.5`. **Nothing wraps except a tunnel row**: a row wraps when both its edge tiles are open to ghosts (the classic maze wraps row 14, sideways, and nothing wraps vertically), so tiles above the top wall or below the bottom one, and tiles beyond the left or right edge of any other row, are not drawn, and a ghost is placed by the short way round when the player's row or the ghost's row wraps (the first prototype wrapped every row and column and put a ghost 38 tiles away three tiles ahead); as offsets from the player's real position (walls shifted by `frac(player)`, so walls and blips move together) in a frame turned by the camera's yaw so that up is where the player looks (`right = dx cos(yaw) - dy sin(yaw)`, `back = dx sin(yaw) + dy cos(yaw)`; x grows to the right, y downward on the radar). The player is the origin.
- **`DangerHud`** (adapter): a `ScreenGui` with a radar frame in the top-right corner (a dot per wall, the player in yellow, a blip per ghost coloured by look) and, per indicated ghost, an arrow (a `TextLabel` with a triangle) placed by `Awareness.edgePoint`, sized by `size` and tinted by `brightness` and `look`, rotated to the bearing; it reads the latest snapshot (`DangerHud.push(snap, maze)`) and the camera's pose each frame (`DangerHud.start(Camera.pose)`). It does none of the geometry (no `atan2`, no path search), decides nothing and sends nothing.
- **`Camera.pose()`** returns the pose of the last frame (`x, y` interpolated, `yaw` = the turned heading plus the free look's yaw), stored by the render step. It must **not** call `View.interpolate` a second time: a promoted spec (`f0520/adapters`) pins exactly one call in `Camera`. (The prototype first did and the full gate failed: that is how it was found.)
- **`Main.client.luau`**: `DangerHud.push(snap, Maze.layout(snap.maze))` for every snapshot and `DangerHud.start(Camera.pose)` at start.

### Pure/adapter split and authority
Every number is Logic and specified; the adapter only creates frames and sets positions, sizes and colours. The server's snapshot is the only input, so nothing here can be exploited or desynchronised.

### Scalability
Per frame: four BFS searches limited to 12 tiles and one radar pass over (2 x 7 + 1)^2 tiles: 0.2 ms for the indicators and 0.08 ms for the radar, measured in Lune on four ghosts (the spec allows 50 times that). The HUD keeps a pool of frames (about 225 wall dots at most) and updates them in place.

### Maintainability
The aid's rules are data (the options), so tuning is a number, and a new aid (audio, say) is a new function over the same inputs. The indicators and the radar share `bearing` and the wrap test (`rowWraps`); the indicators also use the path search that `pathDistance` exposes.

### Usability
Pillar 3 in practice: the radar shows what is around you, rotating with your view; the arrows say which way to turn, and grow and brighten as danger nears, so a ghost three tiles behind is a big bright arrow at the bottom of the screen and one at the edge of the reach is a small dim one. Blue ghosts are tinted blue and white-blinking ones pale, so harmless ones read as such; eyes are not shown.

## Acceptance criteria

### Checked
- **AC1**: the radar and the edge indicators are computed as specified: bearing, border point, path distance, field of view, nearest first, strictly growing and brightening as a ghost nears.
- **AC2**: the danger HUD is a thin client module that draws only what Awareness returns, and the camera hands over its pose without a second interpolation.
- **AC3**: every existing client, view and place spec still passes.
- **AC4**: the architecture rules hold (Logic is pure, every require declared in `tracker/architecture.md`, every feature has unit specs).

### Judged
None.

### Observed
- **O1** (Studio, on Beta, play a level): the radar sits in the top-right corner, shows the walls around you and turns as you look around; ghosts show as coloured dots, blue ones blue.
- **O2**: with a ghost close behind you a large bright arrow shows at the bottom edge; as it nears the arrow grows and brightens, and when it is out of reach (over 12 tiles of corridor) or you look at it, the arrow goes away. A failure is filed as a defect; sizes and colours are tuned after a Beta playtest.
