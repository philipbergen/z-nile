# f0104: New key controls

## Problem
The key map has to change for the new ghost-hunt tools and to separate looking from turning. Today (`View.KEYS`) W and Up are `Forward`, A and Left turn left, D and Right turn right, S and Down are `Back`, and Space snaps the look to the heading. The arrow keys duplicate WASD.

## Non-goals
- The actions themselves: the blaster (f0100) and the jump (f0101) are implemented there; this issue binds the keys and aims the shot.
- Touch controls (f0027, f0028, R0012), and a touch fire control and hop: f0110 (captured in R0035's prep). This issue keeps what R0012 builds on (below).
- Mouse look, which stays free yaw and pitch.
- Rebinding by the player (the keys are a default the user may change in code).

## Solution
_Captured from the request (user, 2026-10-17; game-design "Desktop keys"); the design below follows it._
- W: `Forward` (take the straight exit, cancel a buffered turn), as today. A: turn left. D: turn right. S: reverse (`Back`). All unchanged.
- Space: jump (f0101). It is no longer snap look to heading.
- E: fire the blaster (f0100). The shot goes the way the player is looking, in two dimensions along the maze grid: the view's yaw is rounded to the nearest of the four headings and the shot travels down that row or column from the player's tile. Pitch is ignored. With an arrow held, that is the arrow's direction relative to the heading. With mouse look, it is the cardinal heading nearest the camera's yaw. The client sends only the heading; the server checks it is one of the four and resolves the hit (f0100).
- Arrow keys: look relative to the direction of travel, while held. Up looks ahead, down behind, left and right to the sides, and two arrows together give the diagonals (up and left is 45 degrees to the left). On release the view springs back to the heading. Pressing an arrow also snaps the view out of any mouse look.
- The snap-look *key* goes away (the arrow springback replaces it) and `Controls` stops handling a Snap key. `View.snap` stays (`f0014/look` pins it) and so does `Camera.snap`, which the touch snap button (R0012) uses.
- The promoted key-map spec `tests/acceptance/f0014/keys.spec.luau` was narrowed by c0103 (done): this issue does not edit it or c0103's `narrowed.spec`.

## Design
### Decisions made in prep (author's choices; the user may veto any)
- **Exactly ten keys**: W A S D, Space, E and the four arrows. Q and F stay unbound (the old drafts' drop and fire keys).
- **The arrow look turns smoothly at the camera's turn rate** (a quarter turn in `View.TURN_SECONDS`, 0.12 s), level pitch, both on press and on the spring back. "Snaps out of mouse look" means the mouse's offset is dropped at once: the look heads for the arrow's direction from wherever it was. While an arrow is held any other look input (the mouse, R0012's touch drag) is ignored; during the spring back, any other look input stops it and takes over from there.
- **The arrow look runs after the mouse, on the look it left** (design review 001 F1): Camera keeps today's `look = View.look(look, delta.X, delta.Y)` line as it is (R0012 guards exactly that line), then `steer = View.steer(steer, look, arrows, dt)` and `look = steer.look`. `View.steer` sees any other look input as "the look differs from last frame's result", so a touch drag (R0012's `Camera.lookBy`, which writes `look` between frames) takes over a spring with no change to it.
- **Opposite arrows cancel** on their axis (up + down is ahead; left + right is ahead; all four is ahead).
- **Ties go the way of travel**: up + left aims ahead (as the capture says, "up for up-and-left"), down + left aims behind; a mouse look at exactly 45° aims ahead (or behind at 135°). This is the even-quarter rule, so one rule covers arrows and mouse.
- **With arrows held, the aim is the arrows' direction even mid-turn**, not the half-turned view; with none held it is the current look's yaw (spring included).
- **R0012 compatibility** (prepped in parallel, not yet landed; either may land first): its sequestered specs pin `Controls.start(Camera.snap, Camera.lookBy,` in Main; `function Camera.snap(`; in Camera `GetMouseDelta()`, `lookedThisFrame = true`, and `not mouseHeld and not lookedThisFrame` before `View.look(look, delta` before `lookedThisFrame = false` before `snap == nil`; and `View.action(`, `View.KEYS`, `BindActionAtPriority` in Controls. This issue keeps `Controls.start(onSnap)` with the same first parameter, keeps `Camera.snap`, keeps the mouse line `look = View.look(look, delta.X, delta.Y)` (R0012 wraps it in its guard) and puts the arrow look on the next lines, and adds the camera's key work as a separate `Controls.keys(...)` call, so R0012 can append its arguments to `Controls.start` unchanged. Its specs and this issue's then hold on the merged Camera. See R0035 Risks.

### Logic: `src/shared/Logic/View/init.luau` (changed)
```lua
export type Action = Movement.Intent | "Jump" | "Fire" | "LookUp" | "LookDown" | "LookLeft" | "LookRight" | "Snap"  -- "Snap": no key's any more (R0012's touch button)
export type Arrows = { up: boolean, down: boolean, left: boolean, right: boolean }
export type Steer = { look: Look, spring: boolean }   -- spring: springing back to the heading after the arrows were let go
View.KEYS = frozen { W = "Forward", A = "Left", D = "Right", S = "Back", Space = "Jump", E = "Fire",
                     Up = "LookUp", Down = "LookDown", Left = "LookLeft", Right = "LookRight" }
function View.press(a: Arrows, action: Action?, down: boolean): Arrows   -- a fresh table; only a Look* action changes its arrow
function View.arrowQuarters(a: Arrows): number?
  -- nil with none held; side = left − right, ahead = up − down; side 0 → (ahead < 0 and 2 or 0);
  -- ahead 0 → side; ahead > 0 → side · 0.5; ahead < 0 → side · 1.5   (exact numbers, so ties are exact)
function View.steer(prev: Steer, look: Look, a: Arrows, dt: number): Steer
  -- one frame of the arrow look, after the mouse (or a touch drag) moved `look`; prev is last frame's result.
  -- step = turnRate() · dt (0 for a dt that is not positive and finite)
  -- arrows held: from prev.look toward { yaw = wrap(q · π/2), pitch = 0 } by at most step on yaw (approachYaw, shorter way)
  --   and on pitch (so `look`, the other input, is ignored); spring = true
  -- else look ≠ prev.look (yaw or pitch differs: another input moved it): look as given; spring = false
  -- else prev.spring: from look toward {0, 0} by at most step; spring stays true until both are exactly 0
  -- else: look as given, spring false. Always fresh tables; the arguments are untouched.
function View.aim(heading: Maze.Heading, s: Steer, a: Arrows): Maze.Heading
  -- q = arrowQuarters(a), else wrap(s.look.yaw) / (π/2) (0 when not finite); n = floor(q + 0.5), and if q + 0.5 == n with n odd, n −= 1;
  -- n mod 4: 0 heading, 1 Maze.left(heading), 3 Maze.right(heading), 2 Maze.opposite(heading)
```

### Adapters (`src/client`)
- `Controls.luau`:
  - `export type Keys = { jump: () -> (), arrows: (View.Arrows) -> (), aim: () -> Maze.Heading? }`; `function Controls.keys(k: Keys)` stores it (it may come before or after `start`).
  - `Controls.start(onSnap)` unchanged in signature; it binds every key of `View.KEYS` with `BindActionAtPriority("PickmanControls", handle, false, ContextActionPriority.High.Value, …)` as today.
  - `handle`: `action = View.action(input.KeyCode.Name)`. On `Begin`: `"Snap"` → `onSnap()`; a movement intent → `intent:FireServer(action)`; `"Fire"` → `h = keys.aim()`, and if `h` then `intent:FireServer(Blaster.intent(h))` (the beam is drawn from the server's `Frame.shot`, f0100); `"Jump"` → `keys.jump()`. On `Begin`, `End` and `Cancel`: `held = View.press(held, action, state == Begin)`, and if it changed, `keys.arrows(held)`. Returns `Sink`.
- `Camera.luau`: `look` stays; it gains `steer: View.Steer` (last frame's result) and `arrows: View.Arrows`; `Camera.arrows(a)` stores a copy; `Camera.aim()` returns `View.aim(latest.player.heading, steer, arrows)` or nil before a snapshot. Each frame: today's `look = View.look(look, delta.X, delta.Y)`, then `steer = View.steer(steer, look, arrows, dt)` and `look = steer.look`, which feeds the existing pose and CFrame lines. `Camera.snap()` stays `look = View.snap(look)` (the next frame's `View.steer` sees the change and stops any spring). The one `View.interpolate` call, `setCursor` and the mouse-lock lines stay as they are.
- `Main.client.luau`: before `Controls.start(Camera.snap)`: `Controls.keys({ jump = Camera.jump, arrows = Camera.arrows, aim = Camera.aim })`.

### Pure/adapter split and authority
Which key means what, how the arrows combine, how the look moves and where a shot goes are View (pure, Lune-tested); Controls and Camera only relay. The server still receives only the four movement intents and the four fire intents and validates them (f0100).

### Scalability
A few comparisons a key event and a frame.

### Maintainability
The key map is one frozen table; a new action is one row there and one branch in `handle` (or none, for a camera action through `Controls.keys`). The arrow combination is data-free arithmetic in one function, and the aim's tie rule is shared with every caller.

### Usability
- **Player**: WASD is unchanged; the arrows now look instead of duplicating WASD, smoothly, and come back to the heading by themselves; E fires where you look; Space hops. A tie never sends a shot sideways by surprise.
- **Developer**: `Controls.keys` keeps the camera's hooks in one typed table; `Controls.start` keeps its R0012-facing signature.

## Acceptance criteria

### Checked
- AC1: Exactly ten keys: W A S D as before, Space Jump, E Fire, the arrows LookUp/LookDown/LookLeft/LookRight; no Snap key; Q and F unbound; frozen.
- AC2: The arrow look: presses tracked per arrow, the arrows' direction in quarter turns (diagonals, cancels), turning at the camera's turn rate from last frame's look with level pitch while other look input is ignored, the spring back at the same rate, and any other look input (yaw or pitch) taking over a spring.
- AC3: The aim: arrows' direction (even mid-turn) or the look's yaw, rounded to the nearest heading relative to travel, ties ahead or behind, pitch ignored, nonsense aims ahead.
- AC4: Controls binds View.KEYS, hears key-up and cancel, tracks arrows with `View.press`, sends `Blaster.intent` of the aim on the Intent remote, and takes the camera's hooks through `Controls.keys`; Main wires them before Ready and keeps `Controls.start(Camera.snap`; Camera keeps the mouse line `View.look(look, delta.X, delta.Y)` and runs `View.steer(steer, look, arrows, dt)` after it, aims with `View.aim`, and sends nothing.
- AC5: End to end: a dropped shot, Right held, E: the key map, `View.aim`, `Blaster.intent` and `Session.submit` lead to the ghost on the right being eaten on the chain, and the player never turned.
- AC6: Logic stays engine-free and every dependency is declared.
