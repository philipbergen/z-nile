### F1 [high] c0116: option (b) leaves no way to give back an abandoned raft, and the new `issues:` scan then strands its members and breaks today's abandon flow
Where: c0116 Design, paragraph 3 ("A second `new raft` is refused when any member id is already inside an origin raft's `issues:` list ... Abandoning the raft leaves the members `prepared` on origin"); recombine paragraph ("stamps both rafts and every member with one fresh `preparing-` stamp"); `scripts/tracker/status.luau:6-13` (`ALLOWED`), `:48-50`; `main.luau:920-999` (`setStatus`); `tests/acceptance/c0053/commands-prep.spec.luau:44-60`
Evidence:
- **The raft can't be given back.** Under (b) a z-raft that is given up leaves raft `R` on origin as `preparing-…` with `issues: [c0001, c0002]`, and both members `prepared`. The only published give-back is `status R new`. `setStatus` adds origin's listed members to the targets, and for each one checks `canMove(prepared, new)`. `ALLOWED` has no `prepared>new`, so the whole move is refused: "c0001 is prepared on origin/main: cannot move to new". `status R prepared` is carried (`carriedByPr`), so it publishes nothing. `status R preparing` is refused, because `canClaim` needs a status that isn't stored. `recombine` refuses a `preparing-` raft. So `R` stays `preparing-` on origin until someone takes the raft over and lands it by PR.
- **The scan strands the members.** The scan rule has no filter on the raft's status. While `R` exists on origin, `new raft` over c0001 or c0002 is refused forever. Publish has no deletion, so no command can remove `R`.
- **It breaks today's flow too.** `c0053/commands-prep.spec.luau:44-60` abandons prep with `status rz new`, which leaves `zeta` `new` with an empty `raft:`. The point of that move is to free the issue. `rz` stays on origin with `issues: [zeta]`, so under c0116 any later `new raft … zeta` is refused. No promoted spec re-rafts `zeta`, so nothing turns red. The regression is silent, and `Overrides: none` is technically true.
- **The fold path is unguarded.** c0116 sends the one-issue fold to "the existing `status` commands", and `status c0001 preparing` (`prepared>preparing`) is allowed for any prepared issue. A second session can fold c0001 into its own raft while `R` lists it. Only `new raft` reads the scan.
- **The skip drops today's guard.** The `setStatus` skip lets a carried move take a member that origin shows as `prepared` but that already belongs to another raft. Today that move is refused with "claim it first".
- **Two hold rules.** `new raft` holds prepared members only through the raft's `issues:` list. `recombine` holds them by restamping the members `preparing-`, and its give-back is `status <keeper> new`, which throws away their landed designs (the problem review-003 F1 named).
- This is the fourth round with a hole in "who holds an issue while it is being rafted" (review-001 F7, review-002 F4, review-003 F1).

Critique: The tempting fix is one more sentence: "the scan ignores rafts whose status is `new`". On its own, that still leaves a raft whose members are all prepared unable to reach `new`.
Consequences: Any fix needs one hold predicate that every claiming command reads (`new raft`, `status <issue> preparing`, the carried skip, `recombine`), plus a published give-back for a raft over prepared members. Each of these touches `setStatus`, which `c0053`, `c0067` and `c0068` pin.
Recommendation: Stop adding per-command clauses. Write the hold as one table in c0116: origin state × command (`new raft`, `status <issue> preparing`, `status <raft> prepared`, `status <raft> new`, `recombine`, `ready`, abandon) → result and what is published. I suggest this rule:
- **The rule.** A prepared issue is held by an origin raft whose effective status is `preparing` or `prepared` and whose `issues:` list names it. Prepared members are never restamped, and that covers `recombine` too.
- **The checks.** `new raft`, `status <issue> preparing` and the carried skip each refuse an issue that a different raft holds, and the error names that raft.
- **The give-back.** `status <raft> new` publishes only the raft's line when every listed member is `prepared`. A raft at `new` no longer holds its members.
- **`recombine`.** It stamps the two rafts only. The moved member keeps its `prepared` status, and AC1's "status equals the keeper's" becomes "status unchanged on origin and in the worktree".

Spec cases, each run through the real commands:
- `new raft` over c0001 and c0002, then `status R new`, succeeds. The diff on origin is only R's document, and both members are still `prepared`.
- After that, `new raft` over c0001 succeeds.
- The c0053 shape: `new raft` over a `new` issue, `status R new`, then `new raft` over the same issue, succeeds.
- `status c0001 preparing` is refused while a `preparing-` raft lists it, and the error names that raft.
- A carried `status R2 prepared`, whose local list names an issue that raft R holds, is refused.

Before the next round, prototype this as a scratch patch to `setStatus` and `cmd.new`, run it against `c0053`, `c0067`, `c0068` and `recombine.spec`, and record the outcome.
