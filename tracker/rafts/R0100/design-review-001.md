### F2 [high] A revived ghost comes out blue while the power still runs: the opposite of the arcade, pinned by a spec that contradicts its own name
Where: f0210 Design, Eyes ("takes the level's frightened state if the power is running"); `tracker/issues/f0210/acceptance/eyes.spec.luau`, the case "...comes out again, not blue", whose last line is `t.expect(blinky.frightened).toBe(true) -- ... it comes out blue again`; f0210 Rules and mutants row "a revived ghost never blue again"
Evidence: in the arcade (Dossier), an eaten ghost returns to the house as eyes, revives, and leaves in its normal, dangerous state, even while the other ghosts are still blue. Being able to die to a revived ghost during an energizer is a well-known classic rule. The prototype sets `g.frightened = set.frightened > 0` when any ghost reaches the tile above the door, so a revived ghost turns blue again. The spec's title says "not blue" and its assertion says blue. The mutant listed as "killed" is the arcade behaviour.
Critique: pillar 1 says the classic rules come first. `game-design.md` does not record this as a chosen deviation, and once the spec is promoted it becomes permanent. The tempting fix is to flip the one assertion.
Consequences: blue-ness has to be per ghost and remembered across the house. A ghost that is in the house or leaving when the energizer is eaten turns blue and leaves blue (arcade). A ghost that was eaten and revived leaves normal for the rest of that power period, so `contacts` must report a *kill* for it while others are still `eat`. A second energizer eaten while the ghost is still eyes leaves it unaffected (already the case). A second energizer eaten after it has revived turns it blue (it is a normal active ghost again). `look`, `flash`, the snapshot and the radar colour follow the per-ghost flag, so the HUD shows that ghost in its normal colour among blue ones.
Recommendation: on exit, keep the ghost's own `frightened` flag (set by `power` while it is in the house or leaving, cleared by `eat`) instead of copying the set's clock. Fix the eyes spec assertion to `false` and keep the title. Add cases: "a ghost in the house when the power starts leaves blue"; "a revived ghost is dangerous while the others are still blue" (a session- or contacts-level kill); "a second power pellet after revival turns it blue". Update the Rules and mutants rows (the mutant becomes "revived ghost blue again").

### F3 [high] The session's swap contact is not pinned: a Session that never detects swaps passes every check
Where: f0220 Design (the step order, "contacts"), AC1 and AC2; `contacts.spec` (calls `Ghosts.contacts` with hand-built arguments); `session-ghosts.spec`
Evidence: I mutated the prototype's `Session` call to `Ghosts.contacts(set, Ghosts.occupiedTiles(set), playerNow, playerNow)`, which takes the "previous" tiles after the step, so a swap can never be seen. `tracker.sh check f0220` 5/5 PASS and `check f0310` 5/5 PASS. The game-design rule "or when they swap tiles within one tick" is therefore enforced only at the `Ghosts` level, never for the wiring that decides real deaths. The same gap applies to the order edges the design calls a contract. None of the following has a session-level case: a power pellet eaten on the tile where a normal ghost arrives (the design implies it is eaten); a blue ghost met in the tick the power ends (the design implies a kill); two blue ghosts in one tick (200 then 400 in `NAMES` order); and a contact while the player wraps through the tunnel.
Critique: AC2 says "in a session ... a kill beats an eating", but the most likely wiring mistakes (capturing `prev` at the wrong moment, or passing the player's tile from after the move as `playerPrev`) go undetected. The tempting fix is one swap case.
Consequences: each of these edges is a product decision the design makes implicitly: power-at-contact means eat, and the tick the power ends means kill. State them in f0220's Design and in `game-design.md` (Collisions) so the user can veto them, and add a Rules and mutants row for each.
Recommendation: add `session-ghosts` cases that drive real movement. (a) The settled player and a normal ghost heading toward each other on adjacent tiles, with speeds and progress set so that they swap within one tick: Caught. (b) A power pellet on the tile a normal ghost reaches the same tick: GhostEaten, no LifeLost. (c) A blue ghost met on the tick of `PowerEnded`: LifeLost. (d) Two blue ghosts at once: 200 and 400. (e) A contact across the tunnel wrap through `Session`. Rows: "prev captured after the step", "power applied after contacts".

### F4 [high] The radar wraps every row and column: it shows walls from the far side of the maze, and a ghost 38 tiles away 3 tiles ahead of the player
Where: f0320 Design (Radar: "the ghosts within radius + 0.5 (the short way round a tunnel)"); prototype `Awareness.radar`
Evidence: probe on the prototype. With the player at (6,1), yaw 0, and a ghost at (6,29), `Awareness.radar` returns a blip at `(0, -3)`: straight ahead, 3 tiles away, through the outer wall. `pathDistance` between them is 38. The same call lists 35 wall dots above the maze's top wall, drawn from rows 25 to 30, because the wall loop does `(cy + dy) % m.height`. The tunnel shortcut applies to both axes and to every row, but the classic maze wraps only row 14, horizontally.
Critique: this breaks pillar 3 ("where the danger is") in its own feature: the aid shows phantom danger and phantom corridors near every edge of the maze. The spec only tests a ghost across the real tunnel. The tempting fix is to drop the vertical wrap only.
Consequences: "the short way round" must mean a real wrap. That is a row whose two edge tiles are passable (the same test `Ghosts.tunnel` uses), and only along x. Tiles outside the grid on other rows and columns are drawn as nothing, or as wall, which is a choice to state. A ghost near a tunnel exit is shown through the wrap only if it is on the tunnel row.
Recommendation: build the radar from offsets without `%`. For each offset, map it through a wrap only when the row wraps horizontally, and skip tiles outside the grid otherwise. Do the same for blips. Add cases: "a ghost at the bottom of the maze is not on the radar of a player at the top", "no wall is drawn outside the grid above row 0", "a ghost across the real tunnel is still shown". Add the mutant rows "wrap on every axis".

### F5 [high] A ghost eaten before its power reversal turns back by itself after it leaves the house
Where: f0200 Design (`power`: "every active ghost reverses"), f0210 Design (`Ghosts.eat`); prototype `Ghosts.eat` does not clear `reverse`
Evidence: probe. `power(set, 30)`, Blinky at (6,5) with progress 60 heading E, `eat(set, "blinky")`; `reverse` is still `true` after the eat. Stepping until `GhostLeft`, Blinky's headings are `WWWWWEEEEE…`: it heads west out of the door and reverses to east at the next centre, with no mode change. That breaks the binding "Ghosts never reverse, except when the mode changes". Eating a ghost right after an energizer, before it reaches a centre, is the most common way to eat one. The same stale flag survives `release`/`leaving` for a ghost whose mode-change reversal was pending when it was eaten.
Critique: no spec looks at headings after a revival. The tempting fix is `g.reverse = false` in `eat`.
Consequences: also clear it whenever the status changes to `eyes`, `leaving` or `house`, including in `reset` (`place` builds new ghosts, so that is fine). Decide whether a mode change that happens while a ghost is leaving gets applied when it becomes active (arcade: no).
Recommendation: clear `reverse` in `eat` and when the status changes. Add an eyes-spec case: "a revived ghost leaves west and does not turn back before the next mode change". Add a row with the mutant "reverse kept through eyes".

### F7 [medium] The edge indicators' field of view is narrower than what the camera shows, so arrows sit on visible ghosts
Where: f0320 Design ("the default 100 degrees is a little wider than the camera's 80, so an arrow never sits on a ghost you can see"); `View.SCALE.fieldOfView = 80`, `Camera.FieldOfView = 80`
Evidence: Roblox's `Camera.FieldOfView` is vertical (the default `FieldOfViewMode`). At 80 degrees vertical, the horizontal field is 2·atan(tan 40°·aspect): about 112 degrees at 16:9 and about 122 degrees on a 19.5:9 phone. Ghosts at bearings of 50 to 56 degrees (61 on phones) are on screen and also get an arrow, the opposite of the stated intent. It is also wrong in the other direction on portrait screens.
Critique: this is Logic that can be checked, but it is hidden behind a constant and the Observed O2 criterion. The tempting fix is a bigger constant, which then fails on portrait screens.
Consequences: the visible horizontal field depends on the aspect, which `DangerHud` already passes to `edgePoint`. `indicators` needs the aspect (or the horizontal field computed from it) as an input.
Recommendation: give `Awareness.indicators` a vertical field of view and an aspect, and compute `hfov = 2·atan(tan(vfov/2)·aspect)` plus a margin. Cases: at 16:9, a ghost at bearing 54 degrees gets no arrow and one at 60 degrees does; a narrow aspect gives a narrower cut-off. Correct the Design text.

### F8 [medium] The arcade's pause when a ghost is eaten is missing and not recorded as a deviation
Where: R0100 Theme ("Choices made in this prep that the user may veto"), `game-design.md` R0100 details, f0220 Design
Evidence: in the arcade, eating a ghost freezes the game for about one second (Pac-Man and the other ghosts stop; the score is shown at the spot, and eyes already in flight keep moving). Neither the design nor the list of vetoable choices mentions it. Cruise Elroy, the overflow bug and the other deviations are listed.
Critique: pillar 1, plus a principle the raft follows elsewhere: every deviation is recorded so the user can veto it. Leaving it out silently also changes the game's timing (the blue time is not extended by the freeze).
Consequences: building the pause adds a `Game` phase (or a Session-level freeze counter) that interacts with the power clock (frozen too), the eating slowdown and the ready/dying phases. Recording it is cheaper.
Recommendation: add it to `game-design.md` and the raft's vetoable choices ("no pause when a ghost is eaten; the user may ask for it") now, and leave building it to a later issue if the user wants it.
