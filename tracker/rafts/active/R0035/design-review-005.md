### F1 [high] The camera's "shown tick" is not the blend the ghosts are drawn at: it is unclamped, so the eye overshoots between snapshots and jumps back on each arrival, and the sequestered place.spec pins that form
Where: f0101 Design, Adapters, `Camera.luau` (`local shown = if prev then prev.tick + (snap.tick - prev.tick) * View.alpha(os.clock() - arrived) else snap.tick`); `f0101/acceptance/place.spec.luau` "the eye is the standing height plus Hop.rise at the tick being shown" (its regex requires exactly `* View.alpha(os.clock() - arrived) else snap.tick`); prototype `src/client/Camera.luau`.
Evidence: `View.alpha(elapsed)` is `elapsed * Session.STEP_RATE`, unclamped (View/init.luau:132). `View.interpolate` and `View.interpolateGhosts` clamp it themselves (`math.clamp(alpha, 0, 1)`, NaN → 1), so the player's pose and the ghosts stop at the latest snapshot when the next one is late. `shown` does not: snapshots leave the server inside Heartbeat whenever `Session.advance` yields a step, so they arrive 4 or 5 heartbeats apart (67–83 ms at 60 Hz) plus network jitter, and alpha routinely reaches 1.25 before the next one arrives. At alpha 1.25 the eye is drawn a quarter step past `snap.tick`; when the next snapshot arrives `prev.tick` becomes the old `snap.tick` and alpha restarts at 0, so `shown` jumps back a quarter step. Near take-off `Hop.rise` climbs 4.67 studs a step, so the eye drops back about 1.2 studs on every such arrival: a visible judder through every hop. On a 0.5 s hitch the eye runs the whole parabola and lands while the ghosts and the player stand frozen, then jumps back into the air. The Design's sentence "the same blend of the two latest snapshots the ghosts are interpolated with" is false for alpha > 1, and the regex makes the faithful fix (a clamp around `View.alpha(…)`) fail AC5.
Critique: the tempting fix is to wrap the expression in `math.clamp(…, 0, 1)` in Camera and loosen the regex. That leaves a branch that can be wrong (`if prev then … else …`, the clamp, the NaN rule) in an adapter, where no Lune spec can run it (architecture rule 3), and keeps a second copy of the clamp `View.interpolate` already owns.
Consequences: a pure function has to agree with `View.interpolate`'s edge rules: prev nil, `prev.tick >= latest.tick`, alpha NaN, negative or above 1. After a teleport (`to.jumpTick > prev.tick`) `interpolate` shows the latest snapshot; `frame.hopped` is nil after every teleport, so the hop doesn't care which tick is shown there, but the function should say what it returns. `f0054/adapters` pins the text of the one `View.interpolate` call and no `TICK_RATE` in Camera: both still hold. F2's mapping only holds once `shown` is clamped.
Recommendation: add `View.shownTick(prev: Session.Snapshot?, latest: Session.Snapshot, alpha: number): number` (View's Design and Touched list): `latest.tick` when prev is nil, `prev.tick >= latest.tick` or alpha is NaN; else `prev.tick + (latest.tick - prev.tick) * math.clamp(alpha, 0, 1)`. Camera computes `local shown = View.shownTick(prev, snap, View.alpha(os.clock() - arrived))` and keeps `View.SCALE.eyeHeight + Hop.rise(hopped, shown)` in its one CFrame. Rewrite place.spec's blend regex to pin that call with those three arguments (lesson testing/pin-call-site-arguments). Add an acceptance case (jump.spec or a new View case): alpha 0 gives `prev.tick`, 0.5 the midpoint, 1.5 and NaN `latest.tick`, −1 `prev.tick`, prev nil and `prev.tick == latest.tick` give `latest.tick`. Add mutant rows: no clamp (killed by the 1.5 case), NaN not handled.

### F2 [high] The airborne window is sampled at the end of each step, half a step later than the camera shows it: the player is caught while visibly above the wall tops, against the user's "airborne only while the eye is above the wall tops"
Where: f0101 Design, "Airborne" and "The camera shows the server's hop" ("So the eye is over the walls exactly while the server says the player is airborne"); `Hop.airborne(last, tick) = rise(last, tick) > CLEARANCE`; AC1 ("the 2nd to the 10th step (0.6 s)"); jump.spec "airborne is the eye over the wall tops: steps 2 to 10"; heights.spec "the player is airborne exactly while the eye is over the wall tops"; airborne.spec "except in the 2nd to the 10th step"; the user's decision in f0101's Problem.
Evidence: step N moves the world from tick N−1 to tick N. Its contacts compare the tiles before and after (`Ghosts.contacts`: the same tile at N, or a swap within the step), and the client draws that motion while `shown` runs from N−1 to N (prev = frame N−1, snap = frame N). The prototype protects step N when `rise(T, N) > 5`, the rise at the *end* of that interval. `rise(T, t) > 5` holds for t in (T+1.19, T+10.81). Step T+11 is a catch, and it is drawn while `shown` goes from T+10 to T+11, with the eye above the wall tops for the first 0.81 of it (rise 7.78 down to 4.28). A ghost that meets the player in step T+11 (on the screen in the 54 ms after shown T+10) catches a player the screen shows above the walls. At the other end step T+2 is protected but drawn with the eye still below the wall top for its first 0.19. Latency doesn't change this: the ghosts and the eye are delayed alike. heights.spec's "exactly" case only checks `CLEARANCE == wallHeight − eyeHeight`, and jump.spec's window compares `airborne` with `rise > CLEARANCE` at the same integer ticks, so no spec relates the rule to what the camera shows.
Critique: the tempting fix is to shift the camera by half a step (`Hop.rise(hopped, shown + 0.5)`). That gives an eye above the walls over (T+0.69, T+10.31) against protection over (T+1, T+10]: still caught above the walls at landing, and now shown above the walls while grounded at take-off.
Consequences: sampling each step at its middle, `Hop.airborne(last, tick) = rise(last, tick − 0.5) > CLEARANCE`, makes the airborne steps 2 to 11: 10 steps, 0.67 s. Protection then covers the display interval (T+1, T+11] against an eye above the walls over (T+1.19, T+10.81). The largest mismatch is 0.19 of a step (13 ms), always in the player's favour. That is the closest whole-step reading of the user's "about 0.64 s" (the continuous hop is above the walls for 9.62 steps, 0.641 s). It is a new author's choice the user may veto (rounding in the player's favour). The cooldown, `canStart` and `rise` don't change. The numbers restated in AC1, the Design, R0035 (Risks: "the 9 airborne steps (0.6 s)"), game-design's Spy hop **(prep)** and check.sh's AC1 label move together (lesson ticket-prep/restated-rules-move-together).
Recommendation: define and document that step N covers shown time N−1 to N, and make `Hop.airborne` the midpoint rule above (or argue for another rule against this measure). Add a jump.spec case that does not restate the formula: for each step N from −1 to TICKS+2 after a take-off, sample `Hop.rise(last, N − 1 + f)` at 100 evenly spaced f in (0, 1). `airborne(N)` must be true when more than half the samples are over `CLEARANCE` and false when fewer than half are. This kills end-of-step sampling (step 11), start-of-step sampling (step 2) and a camera-shift mutant. Update the window cases to 2..11 and 10/STEP_RATE, airborne.spec's k loop, and the Design's "exactly" sentence to state the 0.19-step margin. Add mutant rows for end-sampling and start-sampling. Candidate lesson (architecture): when a rule decided per step is shown continuously, name which shown interval a step covers and pin the rule to that interval, not to the step's end tick. The check: does each per-step rule the player sees say which slice of rendered time it decides?

### F3 [high] An implementation that freezes the ghosts while the player is airborne passes every acceptance spec in the raft
Where: f0101 Design ("The rail movement, the ghosts and a shot go on as usual"); Session `step` (`if member.ghosts then stepGhosts(…)`); airborne.spec; f0101 Rules and mutants table (no row).

State in the Design that `stepGhosts` runs every step and only its contact check is skipped. Add the mutant row ("H17 stepGhosts skipped while airborne").

### F5 [medium] OC1's head-on escape, the user's "jump over ghosts", has a measurable proxy and should be Checked
Where: f0101 Observed OC1 ("run at a ghost head-on and press Space just before it reaches you: you pass over it and are not caught; the pellets under the hop are still there"); R0035 Risks ("a whole ghost can be crossed only if the contact falls inside the 9 airborne steps"); airborne.spec (every catch case teleports a ghost onto the player's tile).
Evidence: every contact case puts the ghost on the player (`same`). None drives a ghost into a moving player, so the swap contact (`swapped` in `Ghosts.contacts`) is never exercised while airborne, and the claim that a real head-on meeting fits inside the window is unmeasured. At level 1 the player covers about 0.25 tiles a step and closes on a ghost at about 0.45, so a meeting spans 2 or 3 steps. Hop timing decides whether it fits, which is exactly what the testing skill says to turn into a proxy ("an idle player is caught within N–M s").
Critique: leaving it Observed means the one outcome the user asked for on 2026-10-21 is checked only by a human in Studio.
Consequences: the case needs a straight corridor with no junction between the two, and a ghost that cannot reverse there (no mode change in the steps used). The measured range of hop start steps that escape becomes a pinned number, so changing `Hop.TICKS`, `RISE` or the airborne rule (F2) re-measures it.
Recommendation: add a case (airborne.spec, or a `tests/scenario`-style sim case if the executor's tier rules prefer it). Find a corridor on the classic maze. Place an active ghost several tiles ahead, heading at the running player, and run without a hop to find the first catching step C. Then, for each hop start C−d with d from 1 to 8, record whether the player survives to C+6 and the ghost ends behind them. Assert that a contiguous range of at least 3 values of d escapes (the measured range, written into the Design), that d = 1 is still caught (take-off is on the ground), and that the pellets on the tiles entered while airborne remain. Keep OC1 for feel only.
