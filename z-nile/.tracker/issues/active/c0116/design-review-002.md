### F1 [high] A member can still be redesigned during a rafting: a full z-design cycle that lands on either side of the carried move is never seen, and `ready` hands out a multi-issue raft whose coherence review covered the old design
Where: §1 "Who else can claim a member" row for z-design's `status <id> preparing` ("if the carried move already ran, `ready <R>` refuses the landed raft while the member is `preparing`"); G15 ("refuses the raft until the claim lands or is given back"); z-design §1 ("An issue already in a raft is redesigned through its raft"); z-raft §4.
Evidence: reproduced (`scratchpad/c0116-review002/clone3/tests/scratch/rv2.spec.luau`, two cases, both through `scripts/tracker.sh` in fixture worktrees).
- S1 (G15's own window): A runs `new raft chore late c0010 c0020`, writes Theme, Order and `design-review-001.md`, and runs the carried `status R0010 prepared`. B runs `status c0020 preparing` (allowed: origin `raft:` is empty). A lands. `ready R0010` is refused (as designed). B then appends `## Design REDESIGNED: a new interface`, runs `status c0020 prepared` (**accepted**: nothing checks the origin `raft:` on the carried prepare of an issue alone), and lands cleanly (A changed `raft:`, B changed `status:` and the body). Origin c0020 is `raft: R0010`, `prepared-silver-lucky-heron`, with the new design. `./zn ready R0010` **succeeds**: "readied R0010 … (c0010, c0020)".
- S5 (the window before the carried move): C runs `new raft chore early c0030 c0040` and writes the raft's design and its coherence review. D claims c0040, redesigns it and lands (`REDESIGNED-EARLY`). C syncs (clean), and the carried `status R0020 prepared` **succeeds**, because c0040 is `prepared` on origin and is "taken as it is". C lands and `ready R0020` **succeeds**; the readied c0040 carries the redesign that no coherence review read.
Critique: this is the third hole in the raft-only stamp (review 001 F1 and this round's two orders). The refute's table checks who can *hold a claim* at each moment, but a design change is a claim plus a landing, and once the landing is complete the member is `prepared` again and nothing in the mechanism remembers that its design changed. So the user's decision ("runs one short raft-only coherence review when a raft groups several issues") is bypassed, and z-design's "redesigned through its raft" rule doesn't hold for the window. The tempting fix (refuse B's carried `status c0020 prepared` when origin `raft:` is set) closes only S1 when A lands before B's carried move. It misses S5 and the order where B's carried move runs before A lands (lesson architecture/lock-reads-live-state: it reads state that catches up at a merge).
Consequences: any rule must detect a design change, not a claim. A member that is legitimately redesigned in the window then forces the rafting to rerun its coherence review (or drop the member), and that is a new refusal z-raft §5 must explain. Old-flow members, which are stamped with the raft's own value, must be exempt. A single-member raft has no review but has a Theme that quotes the design, so say whether it is checked too.
Recommendation: question the mechanism, not just this hole. Record what was grouped. `new raft`, `fold` and `recombine` write each member's origin `status:` value as of the claim into the raft document (for example a `grouped:` frontmatter list `c0030=prepared-…`, written in the worktree commit). The carried `status <R> prepared` refuses a member whose origin `status:` differs from its recorded value ("c0040 was redesigned on origin/main since it was grouped (prepared-A → prepared-B): rerun the coherence review, or drop it"); rerunning the review rewrites the record. `ready <R>` refuses the same mismatch (S1). Alternatively, restamp members after all, and say why that no longer meets review-004 F1's `prepared>new`. Either way, state it in §1's rule, the "Who else" table, G15, zz-rules' **Rafting** bullet and z-raft §5. Spec cases: S1 and S5 above, each expecting a refusal that names the member and both values. Mutant: the record not compared (today's code).
Lesson: architecture/claims-name-who-else-can-claim
