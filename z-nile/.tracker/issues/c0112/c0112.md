# c0112: Lessons backfill

## Problem
c0111 (raft R0042; designed in R0040's prep) adds skill lessons: one file per lesson under `.claude/skills/<skill>/lessons/`, and a `harvest.md` ledger in each reviewed item's folder that maps every review finding to a lesson or marks it one-off. New rafts harvest their own reviews, but everything already learned stays buried. On 2026-10-20 there were 203 review files (146 design, 57 code) holding 880 findings (213 high, 308 medium, 359 low), in about 40 rafts and issues.

The user wants all lessons already learned backfilled. Every finding in every reviewed item on `main` gets a harvest line, and the recurring ones become lessons. Once that is done, full coverage becomes a gate invariant: a raft whose reviews are not all harvested cannot merge.

Scope decision (user, 2026-11-01): "c0112 takes them": the backfill also harvests the past stream reviews (`stream-review-<issue>-NNN.md`, about 40 findings), which c0136 makes harvest sources; the stream-review Non-goal below no longer applies.

## Non-goals
- The lesson mechanism, format and command: c0111.
- (Lifted by the scope decision above, 2026-11-01.) Stream reviews (`stream-review-*`), which `done` and `metrics` already ignore.
- Experiment copies of reviews (for example `tracker/issues/c0083/experiments/r0026-review-history/`).

## Solution
- Fan out over the reviewed items: each worker reads one batch's reviews and refutes, and proposes harvest lines and lesson drafts. A merge pass then deduplicates the lessons by kind.
- Seed with the 12 R0039 lessons, now in the author's private memory (`design-first-pass-checklist`), and replace that memory with a pointer to the repo.
- A gate spec asserts that `scripts/tracker.sh lessons --uncovered` prints nothing for the whole tree.
- Prep this raft after R0042 lands, since it uses c0111's format.

## Design

Chore. No change for players: nothing under `pickman/src/` or `pickman/tests/` is touched. Not `solo`; `size: 1400` (the lesson files the merge pass writes, about 100 of them at 10 to 15 lines, the sentences that sharpen existing ones, three documents, one pattern in `lessons.luau`; the ledgers are `.tracker` documents and the specs are prep's, neither counted). The estimate is soft (E4): 13 drafts came from 24 old findings, 1 from 11 already-harvested entries, and the merge pass decides how many survive.

**Facts on `main` at e0b266e7 (2026-11-01), measured with c0136's prototype applied** (`./zn lessons` on a throwaway clone, E1): 157 lessons; 92 item folders hold 1937 findings in design, code and stream reviews (stream: 19 files, 40 findings); 1011 review entries and 60 notes (1071 harvest lines) name no step; 926 findings (886 design and code, 40 stream) have no line at all, in 39 items; 35 batches of at most 150 KB of review and refute text hold all of it (5.8 MB; E3). About 48 of the 1011 entries name lessons of two skills or more, 4 of them of three.

### The method (executed by the raft's lead; workers are Sonnet subagents)

Tools, tried in prep (E2, E8, E10). In `experiments/` (the lead's, free to adjust): `worker-brief.md` (the workers' whole brief), `batches.sh <repo> [--settled | --open]` (the plan: items needing work packed into batches of at most 150 KB, an item never split, biggest first), `held.sh <repo>` (which items are held, below) and `backfill-check.sh <repo> <out>` (checks one worker's folder against a copy of the tree, below). In `acceptance/` (**sequestered with `check.sh`**, because AC3's whole logic is in it and code a `check.sh` line runs must not be editable by the branch it judges): `sources-kept.luau`, the set comparison of lessons and sources that AC3 runs at `done` (strict), that the validator and the merge pass run too, so that workers and `check.sh` apply one rule, and that the conflict sentence runs with `--merges` against each merge parent; and `sources-kept.spec.luau`, its promoted spec (both modes, on hand-made trees).

1. **Waves.** Run the batches in waves of at most six workers, each wave taking only items that are **not held**. `held.sh` decides, by the ledger and not by the folder, and fails closed (exit 2 with the reason when `gh` is missing or errors, when origin's main or a PR head cannot be fetched; `batches.sh` stops likewise when `held.sh` or `./zn lessons` fails, and never prints an empty plan): an item is held when it is under `active/` on `origin/main` (read after fetching it, not from the worktree), or when its `harvest.md` differs from `origin/main`'s on the head of an open pull request (`gh pr list`, the exact commit it reports, fetched when it is not local) or on a branch checked out in a worktree whose last commit is at most 14 days old; every other branch is ignored (`./zn busy` is not used: it names every active raft for any path, and the stale prototype branches `proto-c0117`, `-c0118` and `-c0121` "change" about 128 `done/` folders). It prints each hold with its reason; the lead reruns it right before each wave, and may override a hold it can show is stale, writing why in the wave's commit. `batches.sh <repo> --settled` plans the items not held, `--open` the held ones. Held items wait for the last wave (`Agent`, `subagent_type: general-purpose`, `model: "sonnet"`; the repository rule for subagents). A worker gets the brief with `<REPO>` (the raft's worktree), `<OUT>` (`<state>/b<NN>`, a folder under the lead's scratchpad, never the repository) and `<CHECK>` (the path of `backfill-check.sh`), its batch's item ids, and `<state>/lesson-index.txt` (every lesson as `id | when | tags | title`, regenerated before each wave so a wave sees the lessons the earlier waves added). The lead writes `<OUT>/items.txt` (the batch's item ids) before the launch. The worker writes only under `<OUT>`: `harvest/<item>.md` (the complete new `harvest.md` of each item of its batch), `lessons/<skill>--<slug>.md` (new lesson drafts in the final format), `when-change.txt` (the `when:` corrections its lines need, so it can reach `ok`) and `report.md` (`unsure`, `sharpen`, `refuted`, `counts`). It never writes a lesson file, runs `./zn new`, or touches the repository (the Lessons rule for a subagent).
2. **Validation per worker** (`backfill-check.sh`): it copies the tree to a `mktemp -d` folder (rsync, no `.git`), overlays the worker's harvest files and drafts, applies `when-change.txt`, and prints every problem of `./zn lessons --check` (with `--policy` when the engine has it, below), `--uncovered <its items>` and `--unattributed <its items>`, and what `acceptance/sources-kept.luau` reports against the pristine copy (a lesson id moved to a note headed for its finding passes; a dropped one does not; a note reworded by `reword` is allowed, and the lead does that rewording once before any wave, below). It ends with `ok` or exits 1. The worker runs it before it ends; the lead runs it again on return, and a failure goes back to the worker with `SendMessage`, never fixed by the lead unless it is a single line.
3. **The merge pass**, once per wave, by the lead, in the raft's worktree:
   - List every draft of the wave with its `check` question and tags next to the 157-plus lessons' (`lesson-index.txt`). A draft that states the same rule as an existing lesson, or as another draft, is **merged**: one lesson keeps the clearest `title` and `check`, its `Why` gains the other draft's source sentence, and every harvest file of the wave has the dropped id rewritten to the kept one (one map, reviewed before it runs, each id anchored on its separators: after `: ` or `, `, before `, ` or the end of the line, so that `z-design/foo` never rewrites `z-design/foo-bar`). A draft that is only a new case of an existing lesson becomes a **sharpen**: one sentence appended to that lesson. A draft that restates one finding (a symptom, a game-specific number or name) is dropped and its lines become one-offs with a reason, each naming the step that made the mistake (`(erred z-design): one-off: …`; `(erred none)` only where the finding points out no mistake). What is left is **new**: written at `<skills folder>/<skill>/lessons/<slug>.md` (`z-nile/skills/` or `pickman/skills/`, the real folder, never through `.claude/skills`), its tags taken from the existing vocabulary, its `when:` set by the rule (`design` or `both` for a design step or a design-review finding, except a lesson filed under `zz-review` for the reviewer's own mistake).
   - A merge can change which skill a line's lesson is filed under: the line's group is then corrected by hand (a lesson is filed under `erred` or `upstream`, and a line names at most two skills; a third lesson goes to a note headed `<stem> F<n>`).
   - Apply each worker's `when-change.txt` (`<lesson id> <design|build|both>` per line, which the validator already applied to its scratch copy: edit the frontmatter, once per lesson), then copy each worker's `harvest/<item>.md` (only the items its `items.txt` lists; the validator refuses any other) over the item's `harvest.md`.
   - Run `./zn lessons --check`, `--uncovered <wave's items>` and `--unattributed <wave's items>` in the worktree: all three must pass; then `git archive HEAD` (the commit before the wave) into a `mktemp -d` folder and `lune run acceptance/sources-kept.luau -- <that folder> .`, which catches a merge map or a hand fix that moved a source to the wrong lesson while both ids exist. A lesson id already in the tree survives the pass: a draft that duplicates an existing lesson has its id rewritten to the existing one, never the reverse (strict AC3 and this check reject dropping an id the tree has). Commit the wave (`./zn commit`, one commit per wave, so the spend rows and the diff read by wave).
4. **After the last wave**, three passes: (a) the **one-off audit**: every `one-off` line of the tree is listed with its finding text and given to workers (batches of the items that hold them) with the lesson index and the same brief, with one change: they write the same outputs (`harvest/<item>.md` with the changed lines given a step and lessons, drafts, `when-change.txt`, `report.md`) and change only `one-off` lines they think another item could repeat. `backfill-check.sh` validates them like a wave's and the merge pass applies them. The share of one-offs among the backfilled entries is recorded in the Verification log, with the c0111-era share (87 of 924, 9%) beside it. (b) the **seed**: the twelve notes of R0039 (below) and the memory pointer. (c) the **straggler pass**: `git merge origin/main`, rerun `batches.sh`, which now lists only what main added since the plan (items in flight, new reviews), and give that (small) batch to a worker or do it by hand.
5. **Land quickly after the straggler pass**: `./zn commit`, the code review, `z-nile/check.sh --affected` (which selects every spec: the skills and the engine are touched), then the one `--full` at the merge (memory: full gate only at merge). Do not run the gate between waves: `./zn lessons --check` and the two lists take half a second and are the loop.

**The seed.** `design-first-pass-checklist` (the author's private memory at `~/.claude/projects/-Users-philipbergen-code-pickman/memory/`) has twelve points from R0039's five design reviews. After R0039's own 29 findings are harvested (a prepared raft not yet executed, `z-nile/.tracker/rafts/R0039`: they get a line like any other, in the wave `held.sh` allows, the last wave if its executor has started), the lead maps each point to the design lessons that hold its rule, writing for each `- note design-first-pass-checklist point N (erred <the lessons' skill>[, upstream z-design]): <lesson ids>` in R0039's `harvest.md` (N from 1 to 12, once each, no colon in the text; `z-design` is the usual skill, and a point whose rule lives in a `zz-test` lesson, such as points 7 and 8, is written `(erred zz-test, upstream z-design)`, with that lesson, never a near-duplicate `z-design` lesson written to fit the group). Every point must name at least one existing lesson (`when: design` or `both`) that one of R0039's own design-review findings is also a source of; where no lesson holds the point's rule, the lead writes one under `z-design` (or the topic skill) with its `Why` quoting the point, harvested from the finding that taught it. Then the memory file keeps its frontmatter and its description, and its body becomes a pointer: the checks are lessons now (`./zn lessons --when design --full`, which z-design step 5.1 already runs), and the twelve points are the notes of R0039's `harvest.md`; the `MEMORY.md` index line is updated to say so (OC1). **Before it replaces the memory body, the lead copies the twelve points verbatim into this issue's Verification log** (`experiments/design-first-pass-checklist.md` is a copy of the memory file as of 2026-11-01, so the log can quote it), because once the memory is a pointer the old text is the only record of what each point demands, and the code reviewer judges the seed against it (JC1, stratum 6). That file is outside the repository: it is the one edit of this raft that is not in the diff, and the lead reports its path.

**The attribution rule** is c0136's ("The rule"), which the brief states for the workers in their words. Two readings are fixed here because the first experiments showed workers hesitating (E2): a design-phase finding about a spec or code defect is `erred z-design` when the defect is the design process (a missing Rules row, an unrun experiment, a stale document) and `erred <topic skill>, upstream z-design` when it breaks that skill's own rule; and a finding whose refute says `deferred` or `partly fixed` is a valid finding (a lesson), while `refuted` makes it a one-off candidate. The reviewer judges the sample (JC1).

**One engine change.** c0136's near-head rule (`lessons.check`, a grouped note that is not a head) takes any word `[Ff]%d+` for a mistyped finding, so a grouped note that names an issue id (`f0110`) is refused: 5 notes on `main` do (`f0028`, `f0100`, `f0110`, `f0126`, `f0146`; E1). The pattern becomes `^[Ff]%d%d?%d?%p*$` (a finding id has at most three digits; issue ids have four), pinned by `note-ids.spec` (the ordinary words, and `f1`, `f12`, `F123` still refused). If c0136's execution already narrowed it, step 2 below is a no-op. The other 5 near-head notes (`F1` as a bare word, as in "(design review 005 F1, a final-round high)", or a first word such as `design-review-004,`) are reworded by the backfill, by one fixed function (`reword` in `acceptance/sources-kept.luau`: a finding-shaped word becomes `finding <n>`, hyphens in a first word containing `review-` become spaces) which is also the only rewording AC3 allows, so a note main adds before `done` needs no change to a sequestered file.

**The gate.** `z-nile/tests/acceptance/c0112/` is promoted by `done` and runs in every full gate (`coverage.spec`: the three lists empty, and non-empty on a hand-made tree with one gap each; `corpus.spec`: the reader finds design, code and stream reviews and every finding has an entry, no two lessons share a title or a check (the exact corpus, 1937 findings and 40 in stream reviews, is `check.sh`'s: a count of live items does not belong in a promoted spec); `seeds.spec`; `note-ids.spec`; `docs.spec`). A tracker-only change selects no spec (c0114), so `--affected` does not run them; the confirm steps of z-design and zz-execute (`--uncovered <id>`, `--unattributed <id>`) are the early warning and the full gate at merge is the wall. The same three lists are the tree-wide form of what c0111 and c0136 asked of each item.

**Docs** (the text, pinned by `docs.spec` and `check.sh` AC6): `zz-test` (a paragraph before *Spec layout*: the invariant, where it runs, the early warnings), `zz-rules` (the `lessons` row ends with the gate sentence, which names `--check`, `--uncovered` and `--unattributed`; the Lessons bullet of Items gains the `harvest.md` conflict sentence, which `docs.spec` pins and is the one source of the rule: one line per finding with `main`'s step, the other side's lessons kept by the line or in notes headed for the finding, and `sources-kept.luau` run against both merge parents), `zz-execute` Phase 5 step 3 (the full gate runs the same three commands), `AGENTS.md` (one sentence).

### Scalability
The cost is the workers: 35 batches, 5.8 MB of review and refute text (about 1.5 million input tokens) and about 25,000 tokens of brief and lesson index per worker; the two experiment batches took 114,000 tokens for 24 findings and 90,000 for 11 entries, so the whole backfill is 3 to 6 million Sonnet tokens, plus the lead's merge passes and the one-off audit. Waves of six keep the lead's context small (it reads reports and drafts, not reviews). Locally, `./zn lessons --check` over the tree takes 0.4 s and the validator about 2 s per worker; the new specs take 1.5 s together (one load of the tree each, cached per file). The coverage spec grows with the tree, linearly, like `lessons --check` does.

### Maintainability
Everything the gate enforces is the engine's own lists (`check`, `uncovered`, `unattributed`): there is one reader for the ledger format and the spec adds no second one. A new review kind joins the invariant when it joins `isStem` (c0136), a new design step when it joins `DESIGN_STEPS`. The only counts are in `check.sh`, which never runs again after `done`; the promoted specs pin rules and non-vacuity. The backfill's tools are one-off and live in `experiments/`; the next change to the grammar is c0181's or a later chore's.

### Usability
A failing gate prints each gap as `<item> <review> <finding>` (the same lines `./zn lessons --uncovered` prints), so the author of a branch that fails at merge knows which finding to harvest and runs `./zn lessons --unattributed <id>` for the steps. The workers' brief is one page, with the forms of a line and a draft; the validator names the file, finding and rule.
