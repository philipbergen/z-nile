# c0132: Sweeper killed under load

## Problem
tests/run's jail sweeper was killed with signal 9 during the full gate of tracker.sh done R0035 (2026-10-22), on a machine running several other worktrees' suites; no process of the run was left and the repository was unchanged; the retry passed. Find what sends the SIGKILL (a spec signalling a reused pid in its own sandbox, or the OS under memory pressure) and make the runner tell the two apart.

**Second occurrence (2026-10-26).** `z-nile/check.sh --full` in `.worktree/prep-finished-claude-scans` (c0137's prep, head 0de66343) failed at the start of "== tests (full): z-nile", right after pickman's suite passed (1415/1415), with `tests/run: the jail's sweeper was killed (signal 9): processes the specs left may still run. Stop and report this.` Full log: `/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-philipbergen-code-pickman/90b21ea5-d3e7-48bf-8ead-b2e9b8f802ce/scratchpad/gate-c0137.log`. Load was about 66 on 18 cores, with about 220 lune/tests/run processes from parallel gates in other worktrees. No leftover processes from that worktree were found afterwards, and the agent running the gate killed nothing. The user decided to rerun that gate once and capture this.

**Third occurrence (2026-10-26).** `./zn land` of c0160's capture (in `.worktree/capture-check-paths-third-project`) failed its local full gate the same way: pickman's suite passed (1415/1415), then "== tests (full): z-nile" printed `tests/run: the jail's sweeper was killed (signal 9): …` (the output ended at 16:19:37; it is `/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-philipbergen-code-pickman/90b21ea5-d3e7-48bf-8ead-b2e9b8f802ce/tasks/b6co1bbv8.output`). Both of the day's kills were printed right as z-nile's suite began.

**Memory was fine** that time (96% free, no swap), which makes memory pressure an unlikely cause.

**Suspects.** The sweeper (`fence.SWEEPER` in `z-nile/tests/lib/fence.luau`, c0076) ignores INT, TERM and HUP but can't ignore KILL. The jail's signal rule (`(allow signal (target same-sandbox))`, c0078) lets any spec signal processes of its own sandbox, the sweeper included, so a spec in the same jail is one suspect. The others are outside the jail: a Claude Code background task being stopped, a `pkill`, or a session restart killing its process group. The launcher (`fence.REAPER`) was alive (it printed the message), so its watchdog's group kill (c0079) didn't fire on a dead launcher.

**Diagnostics.** Today `fence.REAPER` reports only the signal number, for any signal death of its child. When the sweeper is killed, the failure message should name enough to tell the suspects apart: at least the run's pid and process group, and whether its launcher and watchdog are still alive. That way the next occurrence can be diagnosed.

## Non-goals
- Linux and other unjailed runs, which have no sweeper (c0075).
- Making the sweeper survive a SIGKILL: it can't be caught. The issue is to find the sender and diagnose it, and a killed sweeper still fails the run.
- A jail probing other runs' exiting processes, and a sweep counting them in its `stopped <n>` (E7): they can't be harmed, and fixing the count is a follow-up of its own.

## Solution
When the sweeper dies of a signal, the launcher (`fence.REAPER`) looks before it stops the specs' group and prints a second line after the existing one: when, how many seconds after the specs started, its own pid, the run's top process and whether it lives, the memory the OS saw (percent free and swap used), the specs' group (the sweeper's own pid), whether the watchdog lives, and every other process still in the group with its command line. Those facts separate the suspects: a signal to the sweeper's pid alone leaves the watchdog and the specs running, and the in-flight spec files are listed; a signal to the whole group (a `kill 0` from a member, or `kill -<group>` from anywhere) leaves nothing, the watchdog included; a kill of the whole tree from outside takes the launcher too, so no report is printed at all. The memory figures answer the capture's other suspect (the OS under memory pressure), which also kills one pid: low free memory or swap in use beside `watchdog alive` points at the OS, plenty of both at a signal. Prep's experiments (Unknowns & experiments) rule out one suspect and fail to reproduce the kill, so the report is how the sender gets found: the next occurrence's report goes into this issue's follow-up.

## Design
### What prep found
- **No spec can reach the sweeper by pid.** The sweeper (`perl -e SWEEPER -`) is the jail's first process, started before every spec, and lives until the run ends, so no pid a spec records (its own children, or a dead one reused) can be the sweeper's. Every `kill -KILL <pid>` in z-nile's specs (c0076, c0078, c0079, `fence-leftovers`, `sweeper-interlock`) names a process the spec started, and every nested sweep is narrowed by `PICKMAN_SWEEP_ONLY` to pids the case lists.
- **What could reach it from inside:** only a group signal. The specs' group is the sweeper's (the launcher's child calls `setpgrp(0, 0)` and then `exec`s `sandbox-exec … perl -e SWEEPER`), and every spec worker is in it unless it moves, so a `kill 0` (or `kill -KILL 0`, or perl's `kill 'KILL', -getpgrp()`) from any spec process in that group kills the sweeper, the watchdog and every spec at once. No spec does that today: the nested launchers and watchdogs (c0076, c0077, c0079 specs) signal their own new groups.
- **Two runs never share a sandbox** (E1): a jail can't signal a live process of another jail, with a different or an identical profile, even while it `exec`s (E6). One run's sweep can't reach another run's sweeper. A jail *can* probe any process of the same user that is exiting (a zombie, or one in its last moments: other worktrees' `git` and `pwd`), so a sweep's `stopped <n>` count can include other runs' dying processes (E7); a SIGKILL to those changes nothing.
- **A reused pid can't be the sweeper.** The capture's first suspect, a spec signalling a reused pid, would need a pid the spec recorded to come back as the sweeper's; the sweeper's pid is taken before any spec starts and held until the run ends.
- **From outside**, anything of the same user can signal the sweeper or its group. A tree kill (Claude Code stopping a task, a session restart killing its process group) would take the launcher too, and the launcher printed the message in all three occurrences, so it lived. A `pkill -f` by command line would match the launcher as well (its argv contains the sweeper's whole command line).
- **Timing.** The runner prints nothing until every worker is done, and a dead sweeper's launcher stops the whole group, so the failure appears right after the stage header whenever the sweeper died: "at the very start of tests (full): z-nile" is when it was printed, not when the sweeper died. That all three occurrences hit z-nile's suite (never pickman's, which runs the same runner first) still points at z-nile's specs, or at how long z-nile's suite runs.
- **`ps` can't run in the jail.** `/bin/ps` is setuid root, and the sandbox refuses to exec it (`Operation not permitted`), so a launcher started inside the jail (every spec of the launcher) can't use it; `pgrep -lf -a -g <group>` (not setuid) lists a group's members with their command lines there and outside. The c0079 spec pins that the launcher's source holds neither `kill 0` nor `getppid` (no pid polling), so the report reads the top process's pid from its environment, not from `getppid`.

### `fence.REAPER` (`z-nile/tests/lib/fence.luau`)
1. `close $r; setpgrp($pid, $pid); my $t0 = time;` records when the specs started.
2. After `waitpid($pid, 0); my $st = $?;` and **before** `kill 'KILL', -$pid`:
   - `my $swept = ($ENV{PICKMAN_REAP_SWEPT} // '') eq '1' && ($st & 127);` (the rule c0076 had, computed once).
   - When `$swept`, it builds the report:
     - `$secs = time - $t0`;
     - `$top = $ENV{PICKMAN_REAP_TOP} // ''`, and `$up` = `unknown` when `$top` is not a pid, else `alive` when `kill(0, $top)` succeeds or fails with `EPERM`, else `gone`;
     - the memory: `$mem` = the leading digits of `sysctl -n kern.memorystatus_level` (macOS: percent of memory free), `$swap` = the `used = <x>` value of `sysctl -n vm.swapusage`; either is `?` when it can't be read (Linux has neither). `sysctl` isn't setuid, so it runs in the jail;
     - its own command line `$me`: the line for `$$` in `pgrep -lf -a -g <its own getpgrp()>`;
     - the group: each line `<pid> <command>` of `pgrep -lf -a -g $pid`. The one whose command equals `$me` is the watchdog (a fork of the launcher, never exec'd): `$wd = "alive (pid <p>)"`, else `$wd = 'gone'`. Every other line becomes `tests/run:   <pid> <command, first 200 characters>`.
     - Two lines and the list:
       ```
       tests/run: the jail's sweeper was killed (signal <n>): processes the specs left may still run. Stop and report this.
       tests/run: who: at <localtime>, <secs>s after the specs started; launcher <$$> (the run's top process <top or ?>, <alive|gone|unknown>); memory <mem or ?>% free, swap used <swap or ?>; specs group <pid> (the sweeper's own pid); watchdog <alive (pid p)|gone>; <n> other process(es) still in the group[:]
       tests/run:   <pid> <command>
       ```
       The colon ends the who line only when processes follow.
3. The group kill and the unlink of links at the top of `.cache` stay as they are; then `if ($swept) { print STDERR @report; exit 1 }` replaces the old `printf`. The first line is c0076's message, unchanged.

### `fence.enter()`
- The launcher's environment gets `PICKMAN_REAP_TOP = myPid()` (the top process's pid) beside `PICKMAN_REAP_CACHE` and `PICKMAN_REAP_SWEPT`.
- The runner's `bash -c` line unsets it with the others: `unset PICKMAN_REAP_SWEPT PICKMAN_REAP_CACHE PICKMAN_REAP_TOP;`, so no spec, and no launcher a spec starts, inherits it (c0076's rule for the launcher's settings).

### Chore checklist
- **No behaviour change**: a clean run, a failing run and a run whose sweeper exits are exactly as before (AC4's cases, and c0076's "the launcher fails a run whose sweeper died of a signal"). Only a run already failing with "the jail's sweeper was killed" prints more, after the same first line.
- **Overrides**: none. The c0079 spec pins that `REAPER` holds neither `kill 0` nor `getppid`; the design reads the top process from `PICKMAN_REAP_TOP` and probes it with `kill(0, $top)`, which that pin (the text `kill 0`) doesn't match, so the pin stays as it is.
- **Existing work**: no prepared, active or open branch changes `REAPER`, `fence.enter`'s launcher settings or the Sweeper bullet (`./zn busy z-nile/tests/lib/fence.luau z-nile/skills/zz-test/SKILL.md` lists R0055's, c0117's, c0118's and c0121's branches, whose diffs touch other lines of the skill and none of `REAPER`). A launcher started by an older runner copy (a fixture of a spec) has no `PICKMAN_REAP_TOP` and no `PICKMAN_REAP_SWEPT`, so it prints nothing new.
- **Shared state**: none; nothing here is written on `main` during the raft.
- **Restated rules**: the zz-test Sweeper bullet (changed and pinned by AC6). c0076's document describes the old message; done items' documents stay as they were.
- **Moves**: none.
- **Guards**: the report only reads (`pgrep`, `sysctl`, `kill 0`) and never changes what the launcher does next: the group kill, the unlink of links at the top of `.cache` and the failing exit run as before, whatever the report finds or fails to read.

### Scalability
The report costs two `pgrep` and two `sysctl` calls and runs only on a failed run. A clean run pays one `time` call.
### Maintainability
- One block of Perl in `REAPER`, next to the rule it reports on, with a comment naming c0132 and why it uses `pgrep`.
- The watchdog is found by an exact rule (its command line is the launcher's own), not by a pattern on its source.
- One new launcher setting, `PICKMAN_REAP_TOP`, set and unset where the other two are.
### Usability
- The failure stays one recognisable first line; the who line says, in words, which of the two readings applies.
- The skill says how to read it and where the report goes.

## Unknowns & experiments
All run 2026-10-26 on the pinned Lune 0.10.5 (`~/.rokit`, which `zn`, `z-nile/check.sh` and `z-nile/lib/ac.sh` put first on PATH), on macOS, at loads of about 60 to 130 on 18 cores from other worktrees' gates.
- **E1: can one jail signal another's processes? Failed (they can't).** A process under `sandbox-exec` with the jail's signal rules was probed with `kill -0` from a second jail with a different profile, and from one with an identical profile: both `Operation not permitted`. Another run's sweep can't reach this run's sweeper.
- **E2: do z-nile's signal-heavy specs kill the sweeper under load? Not reproduced.** Eight jailed runs of c0058 `lock`, c0076, c0077, c0078, c0079, c0127 `reuse` and `rules`, `fence-leftovers`, `lock-start-ids`, `repo-fence`, `sweeper-interlock`, c0049 `parallel-runner`, `run-lane-error` and `run-guard-relaunch` (14 files, about 40 s each) all passed; no sweeper was killed. One run's sweeper stopped one leftover process (`sweeper: stopped 1 process(es)`), as it should.
- **E3: does the full z-nile suite reproduce it? Not reproduced.** Three jailed runs of the whole z-nile suite (177 files, 1222 cases) in the prep worktree, at loads of 127, 86 and 74: all passed in 1462, 1743 and 1601 s; no sweeper was killed (the first run's sweeper stopped 6 processes, see E7).
- **E4: can the report be made where a launcher runs jailed? Yes, with `pgrep`.** `/bin/ps` is setuid root and the jail refuses to exec it; `pgrep -lf -a -g <group>` works in the jail and outside and lists every member with its command line.
- **E5: prototype and mutation check (run).** The design above was prototyped in a scratch worktree (`.worktree/proto-sweeper-report`, branch `proto-sweeper-report` at 5c622209, cut from `origin/main` at c809181f; never merged). The acceptance spec passes there jailed on macOS (13 cases), as do c0076 `sweeper`, c0077 `replace-file`, c0079 `watchdog` and `fence-inherited-env`. Each mutant below was applied to the prototype's `fence.luau` by a runner (`mutants.py`, in the prep session's scratchpad) and the spec run jailed: all eleven fail on an assertion. On Linux, CI (`z-nile/ci-verify.sh` on the prototype branch) passed the full z-nile gate with the spec in it (178 files, run 40424199358, at aef634f, before the memory field); <ci3>. The first CI run failed only on format and one type error in the spec, both fixed. `/bin/ps` first, then `pgrep` (E4): the first prototype used `ps` and its jailed cases failed.
- **E6: can a jail reach a live process of another jail while it `exec`s? No.** Jail B ran one pid that re-exec'd itself about 3000 times; jail A probed that pid with `kill -0` in a tight loop for 6 s: 196,508 probes, every one refused.
- **E7: what can a jail signal besides its own? Only exiting processes.** A fresh jail with a unique profile listed every pid `kill -0` reached, 15 times a second apart: up to about 40 at once, all short-lived processes of other worktrees' runs and sessions (`git add -A`, `git rev-parse`, `pwd -P`, `git fetch`'s children), gone or `<defunct>` by the time `ps` looked. So the jail's signal rule protects live processes (E1, E6, c0078's spec), but a sweep finds, and counts, other runs' dying processes. A finding for the follow-up, not this issue's scope (Non-goals).
- **The sender itself stays unknown.** No experiment reproduced the kill, and macOS doesn't tell a process who sent its SIGKILL without root (`dtrace`, `eslogger`), so the report is the instrument. Its two readings narrow the next occurrence to a group signal or a pid signal, and its list names the spec files in flight.
- **Lessons pass** (`./zn lessons --when design`): z-design/prove-specs-pass-on-a-prototype sent the prototype to Linux CI (`z-nile/ci-verify.sh`), which found a format slip and a type error in the spec; zz-test/specs-set-host-markers-explicitly made the memory case state what each OS gives (`?` off macOS) instead of assuming macOS; zz-arch/clock-before-subprocess put the seconds before the report's `pgrep` and `sysctl` calls; zz-test/run-the-pinned-tools: every experiment ran on the pinned Lune 0.10.5; z-design/never-narrow-captured-scope kept both captured suspects (a spec's reused pid, the OS under memory pressure) answered, the first by the Design's argument, the second by the memory figures.

## Acceptance criteria

### Checked
- AC1: when the sweeper dies of a signal, the report names the launcher's pid, the run's top process and whether it lives (`alive`, `gone`), and the specs' group (the sweeper's own pid).
- AC2: the report says whether the watchdog lives (`alive (pid <p>)` or `gone`) and lists every other process still in the specs' group with its command line, made before the group is stopped; the group is still stopped afterwards.
- AC3: the report says when (local time) and how many seconds after the specs started the sweeper died, and the memory the OS saw then (percent free and swap used on macOS, `?` where they can't be read).
- AC4: a launcher without `PICKMAN_REAP_SWEPT=1`, or whose command exits rather than dying of a signal, prints no report and keeps its exit status (128+n, or the command's own).
- AC5: the runner gives its launcher its own pid as `PICKMAN_REAP_TOP` and unsets it for the specs.
- AC6: the testing skill's Sweeper bullet says how to read the report: `watchdog alive` means only the sweeper's pid was signalled, `watchdog gone` with nothing left that the whole group was.
