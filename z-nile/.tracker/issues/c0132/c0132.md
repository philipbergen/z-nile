# c0132: Sweeper killed under load

## Problem
tests/run's jail sweeper was killed with signal 9 during the full gate of tracker.sh done R0035 (2026-10-22), on a machine running several other worktrees' suites; no process of the run was left and the repository was unchanged; the retry passed. Find what sends the SIGKILL (a spec signalling a reused pid in its own sandbox, or the OS under memory pressure) and make the runner tell the two apart.

**When they died.** All three of the day's kills came in z-nile's stage. The runner stops as soon as the sweeper dies, and the failing gates lasted 4 min 11 s (c0137's) and 2 min 40 s (c0160's prep), where a passing gate takes about 10 minutes and its z-nile stage alone runs past 400 s. So each sweeper died within the first minute or two of z-nile's suite, while the slowest-first schedule (`tests/lib/schedule.luau`) had its longest files in flight. (The logs above lived in a session's temporary folder and are quoted here because those folders don't last.)

**Memory was fine** that time (96% free, no swap), which makes memory pressure an unlikely cause.

**Suspects.** The sweeper (`fence.SWEEPER` in `z-nile/tests/lib/fence.luau`, c0076) ignores INT, TERM and HUP but can't ignore KILL. The jail's signal rule (`(allow signal (target same-sandbox))`, c0078) lets any spec signal processes of its own sandbox, the sweeper included, so a spec in the same jail is one suspect. The others are outside the jail: a Claude Code background task being stopped, a `pkill`, or a session restart killing its process group. The launcher (`fence.REAPER`) was alive (it printed the message), so its watchdog's group kill (c0079) didn't fire on a dead launcher.

**Diagnostics.** Today `fence.REAPER` reports only the signal number, for any signal death of its child. When the sweeper is killed, the failure message should name enough to tell the suspects apart: at least the run's pid and process group, and whether its launcher and watchdog are still alive. That way the next occurrence can be diagnosed.

Scope decision (user, 2026-10-26): "Yes, report then observe". The diagnostic report lands now; finding the sender stays OC1, confirmed from the next occurrence.

## Non-goals
- Linux and other unjailed runs, which have no sweeper (c0075).
- Making the sweeper survive a SIGKILL: it can't be caught. The issue is to find the sender and diagnose it, and a killed sweeper still fails the run.
- A jail probing other runs' exiting processes, and a sweep counting them in its `stopped <n>` (E7): they can't be harmed, and fixing the count is c0168.
- Reading the next report and naming the sender: c0167 (the observe half of the Scope decision).

## Solution
When the sweeper dies of a signal, the launcher (`fence.REAPER`) looks before it stops the specs' group and prints a second line after the existing one: when, how many seconds after the specs started, its own pid, the run's top process and whether it lives, the memory the OS saw (percent free and swap used), the specs' group (the sweeper's own pid), whether the watchdog lives, every other process still in the group with its command line, and its reading of them in words. The watchdog is forked before `sandbox-exec`, so it is in the specs' group but outside the jail, and that is what tells the senders apart (E8):
- `watchdog alive` with processes listed: the sweeper's pid alone was signalled (by a spec, the OS or something outside), with the specs still running and named;
- `watchdog alive` with nothing left: the whole group was signalled from inside the jail (a spec's `kill 0`, which can't reach the watchdog), or the sweeper's pid alone once the specs had ended;
- `watchdog gone` with nothing left: the whole group was signalled from outside the jail;
- `watchdog gone` with processes listed: the watchdog and the sweeper were signalled, the rest was not;
- `watchdog ?`, `group unread`: the report couldn't read the group (no `pgrep`, a failed fork), so it draws no conclusion.

A kill of the whole tree from outside takes the launcher too, so no report is printed at all. The memory figures answer the capture's other suspect (the OS under memory pressure), which also kills one pid: low free memory or swap in use beside a pid-alone reading points at the OS. Prep's experiments rule out two suspects and fail to reproduce the kill, so the report is how the sender gets found: by the Scope decision, the next occurrence's report goes to c0167, which names the sender, and the skill says so.

## Design
### What prep found
- **No spec can reach the sweeper by pid.** The sweeper (`perl -e SWEEPER -`) is the jail's first process, started before every spec, and lives until the run ends, so no pid a spec records (its own children, or a dead one reused) can be the sweeper's. Every `kill -KILL <pid>` in z-nile's specs (c0076, c0078, c0079, `fence-leftovers`, `sweeper-interlock`) names a process the spec started, and every nested sweep is narrowed by `PICKMAN_SWEEP_ONLY` to pids the case lists.
- **What could reach it from inside:** only a group signal. The specs' group is the sweeper's (the launcher's child calls `setpgrp(0, 0)`, forks the watchdog, and then `exec`s `sandbox-exec … perl -e SWEEPER`), and every spec worker is in it unless it moves, so a `kill 0` (or `kill -KILL 0`, or perl's `kill 'KILL', -getpgrp()`) from any spec process in that group kills the sweeper and every spec at once. It can't kill the watchdog: the watchdog was forked before `sandbox-exec`, so it is outside the jail, and the jail's rule refuses a spec's signal to it (the profile's own comment says so). A group signal from outside the jail does kill it. No spec sends a group signal today: the nested launchers and watchdogs (c0076, c0077, c0079 specs) signal their own new groups.
- **A pid signal late in a run looks like an inside one.** When only the sweeper's pid is signalled after the specs have ended (its sweep is the last thing it does), nothing is left in the group either, and the watchdog lives (E8's first "alone" run, whose probe spec ended at once). The reading names both.
- **Two runs never share a sandbox** (E1): a jail can't signal a live process of another jail, with a different or an identical profile, even while it `exec`s (E6). One run's sweep can't reach another run's sweeper. A jail *can* probe any process of the same user that is exiting (a zombie, or one in its last moments: other worktrees' `git` and `pwd`), so a sweep's `stopped <n>` count can include other runs' dying processes (E7); a SIGKILL to those changes nothing.
- **A reused pid can't be the sweeper.** The capture's first suspect, a spec signalling a reused pid, would need a pid the spec recorded to come back as the sweeper's; the sweeper's pid is taken before any spec starts and held until the run ends.
- **From outside**, anything of the same user can signal the sweeper or its group. A tree kill (Claude Code stopping a task, a session restart killing its process group) would take the launcher too, and the launcher printed the message in all three occurrences, so it lived. A `pkill -f` by command line would match the launcher as well (its argv contains the sweeper's whole command line).
- **Timing.** The runner prints nothing until every worker is done, and a dead sweeper's launcher stops the whole group, so the failure appears right after the stage header whenever the sweeper died: "at the very start of tests (full): z-nile" is when it was printed, not when the sweeper died. That all four occurrences hit z-nile's suite (never pickman's, which runs the same runner first), each within its first minute or two (the Problem's **When they died**), still points at z-nile's specs, or at how long z-nile's suite runs.
- **`ps` can't run in the jail.** `/bin/ps` is setuid root, and the sandbox refuses to exec it (`Operation not permitted`), so a launcher started inside the jail (every spec of the launcher) can't use it; `pgrep -lf -a -g <group>` (not setuid) lists a group's members with their command lines there and outside. The c0079 spec pins that the launcher's source holds neither `kill 0` nor `getppid` (no pid polling), so the report reads the top process's pid from its environment, not from `getppid`.

### `fence.REAPER` (`z-nile/tests/lib/fence.luau`)
1. `close $r; setpgrp($pid, $pid); my $t0 = time;` records when the specs started.
2. After `waitpid($pid, 0); my $st = $?;` and **before** `kill 'KILL', -$pid`:
   - `my $swept = ($ENV{PICKMAN_REAP_SWEPT} // '') eq '1' && ($st & 127);` (the rule c0076 had, computed once).
   - When `$swept`, it builds the report:
     - `$secs = time - $t0`;
     - `$top = $ENV{PICKMAN_REAP_TOP} // ''`, and `$up` = `unknown` when `$top` is not a pid, else `alive` when `kill(0, $top)` succeeds or fails with `EPERM`, else `gone`;
     - the memory: `$mem` = the leading digits of `sysctl -n kern.memorystatus_level` (macOS: percent of memory free), `$swap` = the `used = <x>` value of `sysctl -n vm.swapusage`; either is `?` when it can't be read (Linux has neither). `sysctl` isn't setuid, so it runs in the jail;
     - its own command line `$me`: the line for `$$` in `pgrep -lf -a -g <its own getpgrp()>`. Its own group always holds it, so no such line means `pgrep` couldn't be read;
     - the group: `pgrep -lf -a -g $pid`, with its status `$x` (`$? >> 8`, or -1 for a failed fork). `pgrep` exits 0 (found) or 1 (none); any other status, or no `$me`, makes the group **unread**: `watchdog ?`, `group unread`, reading `unread, no conclusion`, and no list;
     - otherwise each line `<pid> <command>`: the one whose command equals `$me` is the watchdog (a fork of the launcher, never exec'd): `$wd = "alive (pid <p>)"`, else `$wd = 'gone'`. Every other line becomes `tests/run:   <pid> <command, first 200 characters>`, and the count is `<n> other process(es) still in the group`;
     - the reading, from the watchdog and the count:

       | watchdog | others | reading |
       |---|---|---|
       | alive | 1 or more | `the sweeper's pid alone was signalled` |
       | alive | 0 | `the whole group was signalled from inside the jail, or the sweeper alone after the specs ended` |
       | gone | 0 | `the whole group was signalled from outside the jail` |
       | gone | 1 or more | `the watchdog was signalled with the sweeper, the rest of the group was not` |
       | ? | unread | `unread, no conclusion` |
     - Two lines and the list:
       ```
       tests/run: the jail's sweeper was killed (signal <n>): processes the specs left may still run. Stop and report this.
       tests/run: who: at <localtime>, <secs>s after the specs started; launcher <$$> (the run's top process <top or ?>, <alive|gone|unknown>); memory <mem or ?>% free, swap used <swap or ?>; specs group <pid> (the sweeper's own pid); watchdog <alive (pid p)|gone|?>; <n other process(es) still in the group|group unread>; reading: <reading>
       tests/run:   <pid> <command>
       ```
3. The group kill and the unlink of links at the top of `.cache` stay as they are; then `if ($swept) { print STDERR @report; exit 1 }` replaces the old `printf`. The first line is c0076's message, unchanged.

### `fence.enter()`
- The launcher's environment gets `PICKMAN_REAP_TOP = myPid()` (the top process's pid) beside `PICKMAN_REAP_CACHE` and `PICKMAN_REAP_SWEPT`.
- The runner's `bash -c` line unsets it with the others: `unset PICKMAN_REAP_SWEPT PICKMAN_REAP_CACHE PICKMAN_REAP_TOP;`, so no spec, and no launcher a spec starts, inherits it (c0076's rule for the launcher's settings).

### Chore checklist
- **No behaviour change**: a clean run, a failing run and a run whose sweeper exits are exactly as before (AC4's cases, and c0076's "the launcher fails a run whose sweeper died of a signal"). Only a run already failing with "the jail's sweeper was killed" prints more, after the same first line.
- **Overrides**: none. (`repo-fence.spec.luau` is a tooling spec, not a promoted one: its allow-list gains one reviewed entry, above.) The c0079 spec pins that `REAPER` holds neither `kill 0` nor `getppid`; the design reads the top process from `PICKMAN_REAP_TOP` and probes it with `kill(0, $top)`, which that pin (the text `kill 0`) doesn't match, so the pin stays as it is.
- **Existing work**: no prepared, active or open branch changes `REAPER`, `fence.enter`'s launcher settings or the Sweeper bullet (`./zn busy z-nile/tests/lib/fence.luau z-nile/skills/zz-test/SKILL.md` lists R0055's, c0117's, c0118's and c0121's branches, whose diffs touch other lines of the skill and none of `REAPER`). - **Shared state**: none; nothing here is written on `main` during the raft.
- **Restated rules**: the zz-test Sweeper bullet (changed and pinned by AC6), and the `REAPER` comment, which says why the watchdog is outside the jail. c0076's document describes the old message; done items' documents stay as they were. - **Moves**: none.
- **Guards**: one allow-list entry in `repo-fence.spec.luau` (above), with c0078's reasons, for the promoted spec only. ### Scalability
The report costs two `pgrep` and two `sysctl` calls and runs only on a failed run. A clean run pays one `time` call.
### Maintainability
- One block of Perl in `REAPER`, next to the rule it reports on, with a comment naming c0132 and why it uses `pgrep`.
- The watchdog is found by an exact rule (its command line is the launcher's own), not by a pattern on its source.
- One new launcher setting, `PICKMAN_REAP_TOP`, set and unset where the other two are.
### Usability
- The failure stays one recognisable first line; the who line ends with its reading in words (the table above), so nobody has to work the readings out from the facts.
- The skill says how to read it and where the report goes (c0167).
