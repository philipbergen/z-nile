### F1 [medium] A note about a code or stream finding the reviewer attributes to design (`Upstream: z-design`) is judged by the note's own group only, so the finding's third lesson may be `when: build`, while the same lesson on the finding's entry is refused
Where: Design decision 2 ("a note headed `<stem> F<n>` … judged by the same phase rule"); zz-rules Items ("is judged by the same phase rule as the finding's entry"); `lessons.check` (`designStep` is read from the entry's or note's own `erred`/`upstream`, `designReview` from the review's phase; nothing reads the answered finding's bound lines or its entry's group); the c0112 bullet (the third lesson of 48 entries goes into such a note, "judged by the same phase rule").
Evidence: Probe on the prototype, a raft whose `review-001.md` F1 has the lines `Erred: zz-execute` and `Upstream: z-design` (column 0), harvested as `- review-001 F1 (erred zz-execute, upstream z-design): z-design/a` plus `- note review-001 F1 a spec rule too (erred zz-test): zz-test/build-only`: 0 problems. Put the same `zz-test`-style build lesson on the entry instead (`… (erred zz-execute, upstream z-design): zz-execute/build-only`) and check refuses it (`the line names a design step`). So one finding, which its reviewer says design should have caught (the code-mode rule decision 2 adds for exactly this case), gets one lesson that reaches design and one that does not, depending only on which record holds it. The same happens in c0112's backfill with no lines at all: the entry carries `upstream z-design`, the third lesson's note does not. The folded c0140 line asks for `design` or `both` "whenever the mistake was design's or could have been caught there", which is a property of the mistake (the finding), not of the line that records one of its lessons.
Critique: This is the third round to find a record that answers a design-phase mistake and escapes the rule (round 1 F1 by the topic skill, round 3 F1 by the stem of the record), and each time the cause is the same: the rule is evaluated per line from what that line says, not once per finding. The tempting fix is to copy the reviewer's lines into the note's check, which still misses the legacy and backfill case where only the entry's group says `upstream z-design`.
Consequences: Judging per finding means a headed note inherits the finding's design-ness from three places: the review's phase (as now), the finding's bound `Erred:`/`Upstream:` lines, and the group of the finding's entry when the lines bind nothing (a legacy or waived review). A disagreement note (`(erred zz-execute)` against a reviewer's `Upstream: z-design`) is then held to `design` or `both` too; `both` always satisfies it, so the author's way to record a disagreement stays open and the lesson still reaches design, which is the outcome the user asked for. The waiver and phase notes are `erred zz-review` and filed under `zz-review`, so the exemption still covers them. An unheaded note is unchanged (it answers no finding). Nothing on main changes: no review has lines, and no grouped entry or note exists.
Recommendation: In `check`, compute once per answered finding whether it is design's: the review's phase is design or unresolved, or the finding's bound lines, or else its entry's group, name a design step; judge the entry and every note headed `<stem> F<n>` against that one fact (the `zz-review` exemption unchanged). Say so in decision 2 and zz-rules Items ("judged as the finding is: its phase, and the steps its review or its entry names"). check.spec cases: the probe above refused; the same note with no review lines but the entry `(erred zz-execute, upstream z-design)` refused; the note's lesson `both` accepted; a note about a code finding with no design step anywhere, `build`, accepted.
