#### `snake/.tracker/game-design.md`


The source of truth for product decisions (captured 2026-10-31 by c0183 from the user's request; moves to `snake/.tracker/game-design.md` when the project is registered). Changes happen through the user, never during execution.

##### Game design: Snake (`snake/`)
Pillars: (1) Snake seen from the head. (2) Deterministic Logic, simulated headless, as in Pickman. (3) Fair and readable in first person: you always know where walls, bodies and food are.

**Arena and view**
- A square grid of tiles with a solid border. The camera sits low in the snake's head (eye about 0.6 tile up; walls and bodies are 1 tile high, so you can't see over them).
- A top-right **minimap** shows the whole arena: walls, food, your snake and every other snake. In first person this is what makes it fair.
- On a turn the camera yaw eases round over about 120 ms, so turns stay readable.
- Solo arena: 20×20. With N players, the side is `ceil(sqrt(400 + 250·(N−1)))`: 26 for 2, 34 for 4, 48 for 8, 66 for 16, 79 for 24. The size is fixed when a map starts.

**Movement**
- The snake moves one tile per step at the map's speed: 4 tiles/s real time on map 1, +0.25 per map, capped at 7.
- Intents are `Left` and `Right`, relative to the heading. There is no `Back`: reversing would be suicide. Up to **two turns are buffered**, so a quick Left, Left makes a U-turn over two tiles.
- **Boost:** while held, speed doubles and the snake sheds one tail segment every 0.5 s, never below 4 segments. A shed segment becomes a crumb (below). This is the move for cutting someone off.
- Keys follow Pickman's habits: **A/D** turn, **arrow keys** look, **Space** boosts. Gamepad: stick or D-pad turns, RT boosts. Touch: LEFT and RIGHT buttons on the bottom corners (44 pt minimum) and a BOOST button.

**Food**
- **Apple:** +1 length, 10 × map number points. There are always (players + 1) apples on the board.
- **Golden apple:** every 20 s one appears for 6 s. +3 length, 50 × map number points.
- **Crumbs:** when a snake dies in a shared arena, every other segment of its body becomes a crumb: +1 length, 5 points.

**Maps (the user's progression)**
- Map 1 is empty. **Map k has k−1 walls.** Each wall is one straight segment, horizontal or vertical, 3 to max(4, side/3) tiles long.
- **Walls never connect:** there is at least one empty tile all round every wall, diagonals included, and between every wall and the border. Spawn areas stay clear. Map 2's single wall is near the middle.
- Placement is a seeded roll with retries. If the walls don't fit, the arena grows by 2 tiles.
- **Goal length** for map k is 10 + 2k (12 on map 1). Reaching it ends the map: a 2 s "MAP CLEAR" with a bonus of 100 × k, then the next map, the snake reset to 4 segments, and a little more speed.
- **Shared arena:** the first snake to reach the goal **wins the map** (bonus 250 × k and a banner naming the winner). Everyone moves to the next map together, respawned at spread-out spawns with 4 segments.

**Death, lives and revives**
- A snake dies when its head enters a wall, the border, or any snake's body, its own included. Two heads meeting (the same tile, or swapping tiles) kill both.
- 3 lives. A death waits 1.5 s, then respawns the snake with 4 segments at the free tile farthest from every head, on the same map, after a 2 s "Ready".
- With no lives left: game over and the revive offer (1 R$ for 3 new lives, keeping score and map). In a shared arena the offer lasts 10 s while play goes on.

**Players**
- **Solo:** your own arena (Pickman's solo-instances model).
- **Arena:** a shared arena of up to 24 players (a tuning constant). It is the server's max players, set after a load experiment. You can join mid-map: you spawn at the safest tile after a 2 s Ready. The arena's size changes only at the next map.
- Each snake has a colour and a name above its head. The HUD shows length / goal, map, lives and score, plus a top-3 of lengths in the arena.
- Every run, solo or arena, goes to Snake's boards. A row shows score, map reached and revives used.


#### `invaders/.tracker/game-design.md`


The source of truth for product decisions (captured 2026-10-31 by c0183 from the user's request; moves to `invaders/.tracker/game-design.md` when the project is registered). Changes happen through the user, never during execution.

##### Game design: Invaders (`invaders/`)
Pillars: (1) Space Invaders from the cannon's seat. (2) Deterministic Logic. (3) Readable: the whole field fits the view, and a field radar covers what doesn't.

**The field and view**
- The rules are 2-D. The classic vertical axis becomes depth: you stand on the ground at your base line looking down a long field, and the formation hovers ahead and advances toward you.
- The camera sits just behind and above the cannon and follows it from side to side. A small radar shows the whole field from above.
- Keys: **A/D** (or the arrows) move, **Space** fires. Gamepad: stick or D-pad moves, A or RT fires. Touch: LEFT and RIGHT buttons on the left, a FIRE disc on the right.

**Solo (the classic)**
- **Formation:** 5 rows × 11 aliens. The back row is worth 30, the middle two 20, the front two 10.
- **March:** the formation steps sideways. When it touches a side it advances one row toward you and reverses. It speeds up as it thins, and the beat sound speeds up with it.
- **Shots:** you have one shot on the field at a time. Aliens have up to 3 at a time: one aimed at your column, the others from random columns.
- **Barriers:** 4 bunkers between you and the formation. Each is a grid of blocks (8×6) that any shot erodes from either side, and that aliens marching through erase.
- **Mystery ship:** crosses the far end about every 25 s, worth 50, 100, 150 or 300.
- **Lives:** 3, with one extra at 1,500 points. A hit costs a life.
- **Invasion:** aliens reaching your base line is **game over at once** (as in the arcade). A revive gives 3 lives and restarts that wave from its starting depth.
- **Waves:** each cleared wave starts the next formation one row closer, as in the arcade, cycling after 8.

**2 players (the user's design)**
- The field is twice as deep. Player 1 is at the south end, player 2 at the north end, facing each other, each with 4 barriers in front.
- Each wave **flies in from the east side at the midline** in single file and **peels off alternately into two groups**: one forms up toward player 1, the other toward player 2 (two formations of 5 × 8). The fly-in takes about 3 s and aliens can be shot during it.
- Each group marches and advances toward its own player by the solo rules.
- **A shot crosses the whole field.** It can hit either group, the mystery ship, and **the other player's barriers**. Default: it can't hurt the other player (it stops at their base line). The user may change this.
- Each player has their own score, lives and revives. A wave ends when both groups are gone.
- If one player's game ends with no revive, their group flies off and later waves come to the remaining player as one solo formation.
- **Pairing:** in the portal, choosing "2 PLAYERS" on Invaders puts you on a waiting card. A second player in the same portal server (or a friend you invite) joins, and both are teleported together to a reserved server.


#### `asteroids/.tracker/game-design.md`


The source of truth for product decisions (captured 2026-10-31 by c0183 from the user's request; moves to `asteroids/.tracker/game-design.md` when the project is registered). Changes happen through the user, never during execution.

##### Game design: Asteroids (`asteroids/`)
Pillars: (1) Asteroids from the cockpit. (2) Deterministic Logic. (3) Readable: you always know what's behind you and what's about to wrap round.

**The field and view**
- The rules are 2-D: a flat, wrap-around field (leave one edge and you come back on the opposite edge), as in the arcade. Rocks tumble in 3-D for looks only.
- The camera sits in the ship's cockpit and looks along the ship's heading.
- **Wrap-around is drawn honestly:** the view shows the field tiled around you, so a rock about to wrap in from behind the far edge is visible at its true distance.
- A round **radar** in the HUD shows the whole field from above, with your ship at the centre. A **rear-danger glow** lights the screen edge when a rock or saucer shot closes in from behind (Pickman's glow idea).
- Keys: **A/D** rotate, **W** thrusts, **Space** fires, **Shift** jumps to hyperspace, and the **arrow keys** look round the cockpit. Gamepad: left stick rotates, RT thrusts, A fires, B hyperspace, right stick looks. Touch: ROTATE LEFT and RIGHT on the left, THRUST, FIRE and HYPER on the right.

**Rules (the classic)**
- **The ship** turns at a fixed rate and thrusts with inertia and slight drag, up to a top speed.
- **Shots:** up to 4 on the field at a time, each lasting about 1 s, and shots wrap round the field too.
- **Hyperspace** puts the ship at a random spot, with a 1 in 6 chance of exploding on re-entry (a tuning constant).
- **Rocks:** large splits into 2 medium, medium into 2 small, and small is destroyed. They score 20, 50 and 100. Ramming one costs a life (the rock still splits).
- **Waves:** wave 1 has 4 large rocks, and each later wave adds 2, up to 11. Clearing every rock starts the next wave after 2 s.
- **Saucers:** the large saucer (200) fires at random. The small saucer (1,000) aims at you, gets more accurate as the score rises, and appears more often in later waves. They cross the field edge to edge.
- **Lives:** 3, with one extra every 10,000 points. After a death the ship respawns at the centre once the centre is clear, protected for 2 s (shown by a flicker).
- **Game over:** the revive offer (1 R$ for 3 new lives, keeping score and wave).

**2 players**
- Both ships share one field, starting on opposite sides, with the same waves (the rock count is ×1.5).
- Each has their own score, lives and revives, so it is a race for the most points on shared rocks.
- Default: shots pass through the other ship (no friendly fire), but ships bounce off each other. The user may change either.
- If one player is out with no revive, the other plays on alone.
- **Pairing** works as for Invaders: a 2 PLAYERS card in the portal, then a party teleport into a reserved server.


#### `portal/.tracker/game-design.md`

##### Design: Portal (`portal/`)
- The experience's **start place**, replacing Pickman's lobby place. Every game's game-over "back" returns here.
- **A carousel with one card per game** (Pickman, Snake, Invaders, Asteroids). Scroll with A/D, the arrows, the mouse wheel, a swipe, or the gamepad's shoulder buttons. Enter or A plays.
- **Each card shows:**
  - the title, a tagline and a live 3-D preview or art;
  - the **price badge: "5 R$" struck through, then "1 R$ SALE"**, or **"FREE GAME READY"** when that game's free hour is available, else "Next free game in mm:ss";
  - play buttons: PLAY, plus ARENA on Snake and 2 PLAYERS on Invaders and Asteroids;
  - **the game's hiscores** with tabs All-time / Today / This week. A row shows rank, name, score, the game's progress figure (Pickman level, Snake map, Invaders wave, Asteroids wave) and **revives used** ("—" for none). Below them, the "your best" line.
- **Purchase:** PLAY with no free game prompts the 1 R$ play product. The server grants exactly once (ProcessReceipt) and records an entitlement that the game place consumes on arrival. TeleportData is never trusted. The revive product is bought in each game place. Playtests show the same screens and charge nothing (f0145).


##### Open item for the user (not blocking the captures)

The portal's `game-design.md` records it as an open decision. The default is to build it as asked, with the badge text in one config table so it can change without code.

##### Technical skeleton

**A fourth new project: `arcade/`, a shared library.** It holds the code that every place uses. It is its own project with its own gate, specs and `arcade-arch` rules.
- `arcade/src/shared/Logic/<Feature>` maps to `ReplicatedStorage.Arcade`. `arcade/src/server` maps to `ServerStorage.Arcade`, which never replicates, so receipts and DataStore code stay off clients.
- Each place's `*.project.json` maps both with `"$path": "../arcade/..."`.
- Adapters require `@game/ReplicatedStorage/Arcade/...` (and `@game/ServerStorage/Arcade/...` from server roots). `graph.srcAliasAllowed` in `z-nile/lib/graph.luau` must allow that.
- **Game Logic never requires arcade Logic.** Arcade features are leaves that adapters wire in, and game Logic gets them as parameters (an RNG, a store, a travel status). That keeps pickman-arch rules 2 and 5. Lune tests reach arcade through an `@arcade` alias in each home's `.luaurc`.
- **What moves into arcade, in order:**
  1. Hiscores: per-game store names (Pickman keeps today's names), a `revives` field on rows, one generic `Boards` server adapter.
  2. Travel.
  3. PaidPlay: the per-game free hour as an atomic UpdateAsync, purchase decisions, an idempotent receipt ledger, and a `Receipts` adapter that every place installs.
  4. Rng.
  5. The Pad and Touch cores (generic folds only; each game keeps its key map).
  6. First-person Look.
  - The Session clock and intent limiter move only when Snake needs them.
- **Pickman migrates one feature at a time:** an arcade issue copies the feature and its specs, then a pickman issue switches the adapters and deletes the old copy. A raft never spans two projects.
