## Solution
The lesson is written, kept to a few lines, with its checks beside it (c0181: a lesson becomes a check wherever it can; the lesson then only points at the check). The captured outcome, the zz-arch lesson with that rule and check question, is delivered whole; the checks make the part of the rule that a machine can hold into a gate failure:
- **A lint over every loop** (`tests/lib/loops.luau`, run by the gate): a `while` or `repeat` loop whose own top level steps its variable by a positive literal in the direction of its condition (or through `scan.advance`) is proven. Every other loop is on a reviewed list with the reason it ends, and a loop that reads text names the sweep that covers it.
- **A hostile-input sweep** (`tests/lib/hostile.luau`, `tests/lib/scanners.luau`): every text scanner runs over every text of up to four characters over the characters that make scanners branch, in a child process with a limit, so a scanner that never returns fails within the limit and names the texts it stopped in.
- **A fail-closed step** (`engine/scan.luau`): `i = scan.advance(i, next)` raises when `next` is not past `i`.

## Design
The captured outcome is the lesson (`zz-arch/scanning-loops-advance`, the rule and its check question). Per c0181 the design asks first what a machine can hold of that rule, and the answer is three things: a loop can be **proven** to advance only in one simple shape, a **fail-closed step** can make the other shapes safe by construction, and a **sweep over hostile inputs** catches a scanner that spins for any reason (the incident: a helper returning its start, which no static reading of the loop's own lines shows). What a machine cannot hold stays in the lesson's check question: "is this loop's advance argument sound?" for the loops on the list, which a reviewer reads.

### What "proven" means (loops.find)
A loop is proven when **all** of these hold (F4: the proof states its whole obligation):
1. the header is one line `while V < B do`, `<=`, `>` or `>=`, V a plain name and B a number, a name, a dotted path or `#name` (not `math.*`, a call, an `and`/`or` or anything else);
2. at the loop's own indentation plus one tab, a statement `V += n` or `V = V + n` (`-` for `>`/`>=`), n a positive integer literal, or `V = scan.advance(V, …)` (for `<`/`<=` only; the call must be closed by the statement's last character, so `scan.advance(i, j) - f(k)` is not it), appears before any `continue` (a trailing `-- comment` is ignored);
3. no other statement in the body, at any depth, assigns V except further steps in the same direction (an assignment is a line that starts with a list of names ending in `=`, `+=` or `-=`: `i = 1`, `i, j = j, i`, `local i`, `for i =`), so a reset, a step back, a shadowing `local` or an inner loop's `for i` voids the proof;
4. the body never writes B: no assignment to it or one of its fields or indexes, no `table.insert/remove/…(B`, no method call on it (`B:push`), so a queue that grows by the step is unproven;
5. the loop reads no text (below): **a loop that reads text is never proven**, however clean its step looks;
6. the loop's end is found by structure: the first non-blank line at or above the loop's indentation must be `end` (a trailing `-- comment` allowed), or `until …` for a `repeat`; anything else (a stray `else`, a dedent) is a loop whose end was not found.
"Proven" means *no write that the loop's own lines show* (design review 002 F4): a write through a call, a closure, an alias or a method on the bound's owner is invisible to a line scan, so the proof is never the only line for a scanner, which is why a loop that reads text is always listed and swept. Everything else is unproven: `while true`, `repeat`, a bound that is a call, `math.huge` or compound, a header on several lines, a one-line loop, a step inside an `if` or a nested block, a step of the wrong direction, a non-literal or zero step. Unproven means "on the list", never "wrong". **Why keep the proof at all** (3 of today's 36 loops are proven, and the holes of reviews 001 and 002 were in it): it gives new code a route out of the list that costs a reader nothing (`i = scan.advance(i, next)` at the top level, which also fails closed at run time), and a loop that would otherwise be one more entry for a reviewer to argue stays proven only while every condition above holds; dropping it would list three more loops and delete about a third of the lint, and is the author's alternative, not a captured-scope question. Indentation is tabs because stylua (the gate) enforces it. A loop "reads text" when its header or body mentions `string.find`, `string.sub`, `string.match`, `string.byte` or the `:find(`, `:sub(`, `:match(`, `:byte(` forms.

### What the list holds (loops.problems, loops.ALLOWED)
Per covered file, in file order, each unproven loop is the next entry: `loop` equals the loop's header text, `why` (at least 12 characters once spaces are collapsed) says why it ends or advances, and a loop that reads text has `sweep = "<scanners case name>"` or `sweep = false` (the reason says why none is needed: not callable, shrinking path, a literal needle). A problem names `file:line`, the header, the lesson id and the fix ("step its position by a literal at the loop's top level, or through scan.advance(i, next), or list the loop in z-nile/tests/lib/loops.luau with why it ends"). A listed loop that disappears or becomes proven is a stale entry and fails the same way, so entries cannot rot. The list is a reviewed claim, like `cachepaths.ALLOWED`: the lint makes every new unproven loop pass through a reviewer's eyes; it does not prove the reason true.

### Scalability
The lint reads about 70 files once (a few milliseconds of pattern matching per file). The sweep is one child process for all cases: 6 scanners over 76,955 texts take about 2 s of CPU (E5), and a case adds about 0.3 s; `MAX_TEXTS` caps a request at 200,000 texts, and a failing sweep costs `SECONDS` (30) wall seconds once. The list grows with the unproven loops (33 of 36 today, most of them poll, retry and shrinking-path loops with a one-line reason).

### Maintainability
The next scanner adds one `Case` to `scanners.luau` and, if it has a loop that is not provable, one entry in `loops.luau`; the next covered root adds a folder to `loops.ROOTS`. One reader of the format (`loops.find`) serves the lint and its specs; the sweep helper takes any `Case`, so it also serves pickman's own text parsers if a chore adds them. `hostile.bounded` is one call of c0191's bounded runner, so there is one runner, not two (`depends_on: [c0191]`).

### Usability
Every refusal names the file, the line, the loop, the lesson and the fix; a hang names the case and the 16 texts to try; a case that never ran its scanner says so with the first error; a crash shows the child's code and its last output; a limit that is not a whole number of seconds is refused with the value.
