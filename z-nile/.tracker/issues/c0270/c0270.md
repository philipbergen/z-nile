# c0270: Lesson no duplicate prep

## Problem
z-design: before claiming or resuming a prep, search every remote branch and PR (open and closed) for the issue id and read its claim; a prep already under way on another branch or in another session is joined or stopped, never duplicated, because c0264 was prepped twice at once (4 rounds by hand on prep-c0264-readme-intent, 9 rounds by an agent on PR #256) and the agent's work was lost; check: do `git branch -r --list '*<id>*'`, `gh pr list --state all --search <id>` and `./zn status <id>` name another worker or branch for this issue?

## Non-goals
- **c0271** (read a PR's state and its newest comments before every push or report into it) and **c0272** (a session that supersedes another prep hands over or gives back the claim and names the live branch) are the incident's other two lessons. This one is the search before a claim or a resume, and what to do with what it finds.
- **Taking over a lost claim** (`./zn lost`, `./zn worktree --takeover`, the numbered copy) is R0100's (c0265, c0274, c0277, active). The step-1 bullet says only "join it" for a prep under way and leaves how a lost one is taken over to the bullets R0100 rewrites (E2: the two merge cleanly in either order).
- **A mechanism** that refuses `./zn status <id> preparing` when a branch or PR names the issue was not captured, and the user decided that claim ownership "is documented, not checked" (zz-rules, Whose claim). This issue writes the rule where the claiming step reads it. A refusal at the claim would be a new capture.
- **Repointing `origin`** after the repository's rename (E1) is the shared git configuration, which no session repairs itself (AGENTS.md). The bullet's `--repo` works either way.
- **The stale claim memory** seen under the dispatcher (E5) is a separate defect, reported in the prep's handoff for capture.

## Solution
<!-- The approach in one paragraph. -->
One new lesson, `z-design/one-prep-per-issue` (`when: design`), and one new bullet, **One prep per issue**, at the end of z-design's step 1, where the claim is made. The bullet is the rule: before a claim and at every resume, search the remote branches by id and by slug, every PR of the renamed repository, the local worktrees and the claim; sort each hit as yours (this worktree's branch and PR, and the prototypes the issue document names), another prep (a branch or worktree holding a design of the issue, or a PR that is its prep) or only read (a PR that mentions the id, a capture, a closed or merged PR); at a resume the claim alone proves nothing; another prep is joined only with its owner's say, or you stop; under the dispatcher the session first reads earlier rulings, then asks with the three choices that work. The lesson keeps the captured check question word for word, adds the three searches the capture's commands miss, and tells the c0264 incident briefly. A harvest note in c0270's folder is the lesson's source. A sequestered docs spec, promoted at `done`, pins the bullet's rule in step 1, and `check.sh` pins the lesson's shape and words once.

## Design
The captured outcome is a z-design lesson. A lesson alone is read at step 5.1 (`./zn lessons --when design --full`) and whenever a step "is about to do something a lesson might cover"; step 1 says neither, so a lesson read after the claim cannot stop a second claim. The rule therefore goes where the claim is made, in z-design step 1 (lesson `z-design/rules-on-a-step-live-in-its-skill`: a rule on a step lives in the skill that step reads), and the lesson points at it, holds the question a reviewer asks and keeps the incident.

### Decisions
1. **A new lesson, not a note.** `z-design/quote-other-preps-before-promising` is about *another issue's* prep that this design leans on; `z-design/refusing-memory-names-how-its-owner-gets-back-in` and `z-design/claim-memory-rules-list-every-claim-path` are rules for designing the claim tooling. None is about a second prep of *the same* issue by the session doing the prep, and a note there would reach the wrong reader (a designer of other work or of the tool). `./zn lessons duplicate prep claim branch` and `./zn lessons twice` find no lesson on it.
2. **Filed under `z-design`, `when: design`.** The capture opens "z-design:", the step that erred is z-design's claim (c0136's filing rule: under its `erred` skill), and a prep is a design step. Tags `coordination, claims, process` (existing tags, `./zn lessons --tags`).
3. **The captured question is kept word for word**, with its first letter capitalised, and the three searches it misses are added after it (z-design: prep keeps captured text and adds detail):
   - **the slug**: a prep branch is `prep-<slug>` (z-design step 1, `./zn worktree prep-<slug>`) and need not hold the id. The agent's c0264 branch was `prep-readme-states-znile-intent`, and `git branch -r --list '*c0264*'` does not find it (E1);
   - **`--repo`**: this checkout's `origin` is `https://github.com/philipbergen/pickman.git` and the repository is now `philipbergen/z-nile`. `gh pr list --search` builds `repo:philipbergen/pickman`, and GitHub's search does not follow the rename: `gh pr list --state all --search c0264` lists nothing, while the same with `--repo "$(gh repo view --json nameWithOwner -q .nameWithOwner)"` lists 15 PRs, #256 among them (E1). An empty search looks like "no other prep" (lesson `zz-test/no-vacuous-pass`);
   - **`git worktree list`**: another session on this machine holds a worktree before it pushes anything.
4. **The step-1 bullet** (reference: `experiments/ref-skill-bullet.txt`; the executor's text may reword around the pinned words) is one bullet with five sub-bullets (Search, Yours, The claim, Another prep, Then). It goes after step 1's last bullet ("If an issue is missing…"), which no open branch changes. R0100 rewrites the **Sync first** and **Claim it** bullets and the worktree paragraph, and a bullet inserted next to them conflicts: design review 001 F6 placed it directly above **Claim it** and it conflicted with all five R0100 layers and `R0071-no-leaked-processes`. E2 merged it at the end with every one of the 20 open branches that edit the file, both ways round: all clean. A rule that runs before the claim is better read before the claim bullet, so once R0100 has landed the executor puts it directly above **Claim it** (Implementation step 2; `docs.spec` finds it anywhere in step 1).
5. **What counts as another prep** (design review 001 F1, F2). Each hit of the searches is one of three kinds, decided from what a resuming session can read (lesson `z-design/instructions-runnable-where-they-run`):
   - **Yours**: the branch this worktree is on and its PR, and every prototype branch or worktree the issue document names (z-design already records prototypes in Unknowns & experiments), with a worktree named after one, detached or not (`proto-c0283-mut` after `proto-c0283`). The bullet asks each prep to name every prototype it makes, so that a resume knows it.
   - **The claim**: before the claim, a `preparing` claim is another prep's, and `./zn status <id> preparing` refuses it anyway (`status.canClaim` allows `preparing` to `preparing` only through a raft, `status.luau:101-103`). At a resume the claim alone proves nothing, because nothing records who made it: `./zn status <id>` prints only the state, and `.cache/tracker-claim-<id>` was seen holding the pre-claim value (E5). Under the dispatcher the claim is the chain's: `dispatch.luau:353` starts z-design on a `preparing` issue only when `f.ownClaim` is set, which `dispatcher.luau:1641-1646` sets from the chain's own z-design step, and stops otherwise ("is being prepared by another terminal"). In a terminal it is yours when this prep claimed it. So at a resume a `preparing` claim is evidence only together with another prep that the branch, worktree or PR searches find. **Accepted blind spot**: a claim given back and taken again by a session elsewhere that has pushed nothing is not found; Whose claim (zz-rules) already forbids giving back a claim you did not make.
   - **Another prep**: any other branch with an open PR or none, or worktree, that holds a design of this issue (its copy of the issue document has a Design section or `acceptance/` files that main's lacks), and any open PR whose head is such a branch or whose title names it as `prep <id>`. c0264's hand branch had no PR while it was being prepped, so "or none" is needed.
   - **Read, not counted**: a PR that only mentions the id (it can still name the live prep, as #256's closing comments did), a branch or worktree with no design of the issue (a capture's worktree), and a closed PR, whose comments say where its work went (follow it). A merged PR is history: c0264 has merged prep PRs #265 and #326 from its re-preps.
   c0264's hand prep was an unclaimed prep (its one claim, 2026-11-07 19:44 PST, `380ed0c6`, was the agent's), which no claim check sees and the branch search does.
6. **What to do with another prep** (design review 001 F3): join it only with its owner's say (ask the user), or stop and take another issue; never design the issue a second time beside it. Headless (`ZN_DISPATCH=1`), nobody answers, and "join" is not something a dispatched step can do: the dispatcher always runs z-design in its own `prep-<slug>` worktree (`dispatcher.luau:794`). `./zn dispatch retry` moves `questions.md` to `answered-<n>.md` in the state folder (`dispatcher.luau:2020-2026`) and reruns the step with the same prompt, which names neither. So the session first reads `$ZN_DISPATCH_DIR/answered-*.md` and follows a ruling that names exactly the hits it finds now (a ruling that names other hits, or misses one, is stale, and it asks again). Otherwise it writes the hits to `$ZN_DISPATCH_DIR/questions.md` with the three choices that work, and stops: (a) the user ends the other prep (closes its PR, deletes its branch, gives its claim back) and retries; (b) the user keeps it, runs `./zn dispatch stop <id>` and carries it on in a terminal (this is "join" under the dispatcher); (c) the user rules that a hit is not a prep of this issue (lesson `z-design/dispatched-refusals-name-their-way-through`).
7. **Landing order with c0136 and c0181.** c0136 (active, R0068) adds a step group to notes: `- note <text> (erred <skill>): <ids>`, read by `^(.-)%s*%((erred [^()]*)%)$`. The executor writes the note with `(erred z-design)` in either order: today's parser reads it as part of the note's text (E3's prototype passes `lessons --check` with it), and c0136's reads it as the group. AC3 runs `--unattributed c0270` once `main.luau` knows that flag. c0181 (prepared, R0074) makes every lesson state `enforced_by` or `not_codifiable`. **If c0270 lands first**, c0181's backfill gives this lesson its state. **If c0181 lands first**, the executor adds `not_codifiable: whether a session searched for another prep before it claimed or resumed is an act no text records, and claim ownership is documented, not checked (zz-rules, Whose claim)` and the harvest line `- codify z-design/one-prep-per-issue: not codifiable: <the same reason>`, and runs `./zn lessons --check --policy --item c0270`; if that reports an overlap pair, the lesson gains `distinct_from:` naming it (Decision 1 is the reason). AC5 holds this and says plainly when c0181 has not landed.

### Moves and guards
None: no file moves, and nothing near the guards, the publisher or the gate changes.

### Scalability
The searches are four commands that read refs and one GitHub search per claim; their cost does not grow with the number of lessons. The lesson adds about 900 bytes to `./zn lessons --when design --full` and one line to its index.

### Maintainability
One sentence of rule, in the step that acts on it; the lesson is short and names the bullet, so a later rewording edits one place. The pins are the rule's words, not the sentence, so a later item can reword around them; one that drops a word overrides `tests/acceptance/c0270/docs.spec.luau` by a chore, as usual.

### Usability
The bullet gives commands to paste, with the reason for each non-obvious flag beside it (`'*<slug>*'`, `--repo`), says which hits are yours, and gives the one action for the rest, headless included.
