### F1 [high] At a resume, a fresh session cannot tell "the claim you made", so a resumed prep finds its own claim and stops
Where: Design, Decision 5; `experiments/ref-skill-bullet.txt` ("Yours are the branch this worktree is on, its PR and the claim you made. … a `preparing` claim you did not make is a prep under way"); AC4
Evidence: Decision 5 says that "a claim you did not make" is "read from what you did, not from `.cache/tracker-claim-<id>`". A session that resumes a prep has done nothing yet. Nothing the bullet runs says who made a claim. `./zn status c0270` prints only `preparing`, with no stamp and no maker, and `./zn status` prints `<id> preparing <title>`. The dispatcher starts z-design on a `preparing` issue only when `f.ownClaim` is set (`dispatch.luau:353`). It sets that when the chain's history holds a z-design step (`dispatcher.luau:1641-1646`), and the step's whole instruction is "Read `z-nile/skills/z-design/SKILL.md` and follow it for <id>." (`dispatch.luau:148`). So every `./zn dispatch retry` of a mid-prep z-design step is a fresh session resuming a claim it did not make in its own context. Retries happen after `questions.md`, after a ruling under `## Open to the human`, and after a quota, failed or lost step. The bullet sends that session to `questions.md` and stop. A person's new terminal session on a prep (after `/clear`, the next day) is in the same position. This breaks resumes that work today, and the design's "No behaviour change" does not hold for them.
Critique: there are two tempting fixes. One is "the claim is yours when you are in the `prep-<slug>` worktree". But c0264's two sessions were each in their own prep worktree (`prep-c0264-readme-intent`, `prep-readme-states-znile-intent`), so each would have called the claim its own, which is the incident again. The other is "read `.cache/tracker-claim-<id>`". E5 shows that file held the pre-claim value, and it is one checkout's memory, not the prep's.
Consequences: the claim is only evidence at one moment. Before the claim, any `preparing` claim belongs to someone else, and the tool already refuses a second claim (`status.canClaim` allows `preparing → preparing` only through a raft, `status.luau:101-103`; `canMove` has no such edge). At a resume, nothing records who made the claim. On its own it proves nothing, and it becomes evidence only together with another prep that the branch, PR or worktree searches find. One blind spot is left: a claim given back and retaken by a session on another machine that has pushed nothing. zz-rules Whose claim already forbids giving back a claim you did not make, so the design can accept it, but it must say so.
Recommendation: rewrite Decision 5 and the bullet in three parts.
(1) Before the claim, any `preparing` claim is another prep's; `./zn status <id> preparing` refuses it anyway.
(2) At a resume, under the dispatcher, the claim is the chain's: the dispatcher starts z-design on a `preparing` issue only after its own chain's z-design step. In a terminal, it is yours when this prep claimed it, as step 1 requires. In either case, a `preparing` claim counts as another prep only alongside another branch, PR or worktree, or when this prep never claimed.
(3) State the blind spot above as accepted, citing Whose claim.
Add docs.spec needles for (1) and (2), for example "at a resume, the claim alone" and "the dispatcher starts", and Rules and mutants rows. The mutants: "a `preparing` claim you did not make" kept unconditional at a resume, and the dispatcher sentence removed. Rerun E3.
Lesson: z-design/instructions-runnable-where-they-run

### F2 [high] The prep's own prototype branches and worktrees, and other issues' PRs that only mention the id, count as "a prep under way"
Where: Design, Decision 5 and Existing work ("Items mid-prep … follow the bullet at their next resume"); the bullet's "Any other branch with an open PR or none, another worktree, … is a prep under way"
Evidence: I ran the bullet's searches for c0283 (`preparing-heron-mellow-quiet`; its prep is `prep-c0283-check-paths`, PR #388 open):
- **Branches:** `git branch -r --list '*c0283*' '*design-approval-gate*'` lists `origin/prep-c0283-check-paths`, `origin/proto-c0283` and `origin/proto-c0283-publish`. Neither proto branch has a PR (`gh pr list --head proto-c0283` is empty).
- **Worktrees:** `git worktree list | grep -e c0283 -e design-approval-gate` lists `.worktree/proto-c0283`, `.worktree/proto-c0283-mut` (detached HEAD) and `.worktree/proto-c0283-publish`, besides its own.
- **PRs:** `gh pr list --repo philipbergen/z-nile --state all --search c0283` lists #388 and also #396, which is OPEN, head `prep-restack-drops-unfinished-layer-commits`, titled "tracker: prep c0300 Restack drops unfinished layer commits". That is c0300's prep, which only names c0283 in its text.

Under the bullet, a resume of c0283 finds two branches, three worktrees and one open PR that are "a prep under way". All of them are its own prototypes (c0283's prep document names `proto-c0283` and `proto-c0283-publish`) or another issue's prep. Of the 45 items `preparing` on `origin/main`, five have an `origin/proto-<id>` with no PR: c0144, c0162, c0163, c0283 and c0325. Under the dispatcher each would stop at `questions.md`, and F3 keeps it stopped. Existing work says these items "follow the bullet" but never ran it on them.
Critique: the tempting fix is to exempt names starting `proto-`. But prototypes are not always named that way (`proto-c0265-env`, `proto-check-paths-home`, and the detached `proto-c0283-mut` has no branch). The PR side needs its own rule as well.
Consequences: "yours" now has to include work this prep made besides its branch. That needs a source the session can read, which is the issue document, where z-design already records prototypes in Unknowns & experiments. A prototype the document does not name falls back to asking, which is the safe side. A PR that only mentions the id can name the live prep (as #256's closing comments did), so it should still be read, just not counted.
Recommendation: Decision 5 and the bullet should say that yours are also the prototype branches and worktrees this prep made: those its issue document names, and worktrees checked out at them, detached ones included. A PR counts only when its head is a branch the branch search found, or its title names this issue as its prep (`prep <id>`). A PR that only mentions the id is read and not counted. Any other hit is read, and it counts as another prep when it holds a design of this issue that is not yours. Rewrite Existing work from a run of the bullet's searches on every item `preparing` on `origin/main`, recording what each resume finds under the new rule (none for c0283's prototypes or #396). Add docs.spec needles and rows for both sentences. The mutants: your own prototypes not counted as yours, and every PR the search lists counted.
Lesson: z-design/new-rules-migrate-existing-work

### F3 [medium] Under the dispatcher, the answer to the bullet's question never reaches the retried step, and "join it" is not something a dispatched step can do
Where: the bullet's "Under `ZN_DISPATCH=1`, write what you found and the choice (join it or stop) to `$ZN_DISPATCH_DIR/questions.md` and stop"; Decision 6
Evidence: `./zn dispatch retry` moves `questions.md` to `answered-<n>.md` (`dispatcher.luau:2020-2026`) and reruns the step with the same prompt (`dispatch.luau:148-161`), which does not name that file. No skill names it either (`grep -rn answered- z-nile/skills/*/SKILL.md` finds nothing). The retried step is a resume: it runs the searches again, finds the same hit and asks again. "Join it" cannot be done headless, because the dispatcher always runs z-design in `.worktree/prep-<slug>` (`dispatcher.luau:794`), never on the other prep's branch. Today the only answers that let the chain through are acts on the other prep: closing its PR, or deleting its branch and worktree. For an F2 false positive, that means deleting the prep's own prototype.
Critique: the tempting fix is "the session reads the answer". But the question offers an option, join, that the dispatched step cannot carry out.
Consequences: the session needs a reading rule for earlier answers, and a ruling can go stale (it names a branch whose head has moved, or a new hit has appeared). A stale ruling asks again.
Recommendation: the headless sentence names the options that work: (a) the user ends the other prep (closes its PR, deletes its branch, gives its claim back) and retries; (b) the user keeps the other prep, runs `./zn dispatch stop <id>` and carries on with it in a terminal (this is "join it" under the dispatcher); (c) the user rules that a hit is not a prep of this issue. A retried step first reads `$ZN_DISPATCH_DIR/answered-*.md` and follows a ruling that names exactly the hits it finds now; otherwise it asks again. Add needles for `answered-` and the three options, and a row for each.
Lesson: z-design/dispatched-refusals-name-their-way-through
