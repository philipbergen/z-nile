## Problem
Scope decision (user, 2026-11-08): "yes go ahead and split the whole thing up and tie them togetjer in a raft so we can run through these a layer at a time in a PR" (c0265's design was split into c0265, c0274, c0275, c0276 and c0277, one raft, delivered a layer at a time in depends_on order; this issue carries the user's worktree rule).

User (2026-11-08): "Rename the old worktree and create a copy to continue the work and call it R123-1 R123-2 and so on. That way a process that wakes up knows it has been discarded and can clear and pick up something new. I assume that there is a point in trying to recover the work and not just discarding it"

On one host a taker (c0274) and the old owner can share one worktree, so a returning old session could carry on in the taker's checkout and never learn it lost the claim. What should happen:
- **A takeover discards the old worker's worktree**, so a process that wakes up there knows it has been discarded and can clear its context and pick up something new.
- **A numbered copy continues the work** (`R123-1`, `R123-2` and so on).
- **The work is recovered, not just discarded**: what the old worker left (pushed commits, unpushed commits, uncommitted files) is kept and shown to the taker.

Decided with the user (2026-11-11), idea interview Q1: "Until the raft merges" (the renamed old worktree is kept until the raft's last layer merges or the raft is aborted, then removed with its branch by `./zn done` or z-stop; `./zn reap` lists any left over).

Decided with the user (2026-11-11), idea interview Q2: "Same as rafts" (a design draft follows the raft rules: a prep branch is pushed after every commit, so a taker on any host gets the draft, and a discarded prep worktree is kept until the prep's PR merges or the prep is stopped, then removed; `./zn reap` lists leftovers).

## Idea
On one host, a taker (c0274) and the old owner can share one worktree. A returning old session could then carry on in the taker's checkout and never learn that it lost the claim. c0277 is one layer of the heartbeat raft (c0265, c0274, c0275, c0276, c0277), delivered a layer at a time in depends_on order.

- **A takeover discards the old worker's worktree** by renaming it. A process that wakes up there finds its paths gone, knows it has been discarded, and can clear its context and pick up something new.
- **A numbered copy continues the work:** `R123-1`, `R123-2` and so on.
- **The work is recovered, not just discarded.** What the old worker left (pushed commits, unpushed commits and uncommitted files) is kept and shown to the taker.
- **The old worktree is kept until its work ends** (Q1, Q2). A raft's renamed worktree stays until the raft's last layer merges or the raft is aborted, and `./zn done` or z-stop then removes it with its branch. A design draft's (a taken-over `preparing` claim) stays until the prep's PR merges or the prep is stopped, and is then removed. `./zn reap` lists any left over.

Not here: taking over the claim and telling an old owner through its beat (c0274); the automatic beats a discarded worktree must stop (c0275); and several hosts (c0254, where every commit is pushed, so a taker on another host continues from the pushed branch through c0274's takeover).
