# c0287: c0117 specs wait on wall clock

## Problem
c0117/control-cli.spec.luau proves an orphan's death with absentAfter(grandchild-orphan, 5): a file must be gone within 5 s of wall clock, the same load-sensitive shape c0279 found in dispatch-cli.spec.luau (where it failed under load, 4 of 8 runs red at load 47 to 97). control-cli is the slowest spec in the gate (905 s). Found during c0279's prep, outside its scope (the user scoped c0279 to every load-sensitive case in dispatch-cli). What should happen: control-cli proves a process's death by the process (c0279's deadWithin, kill -0 polled on a bounded wall clock), never by a file's absence after a fixed sleep, and stays green on a loaded machine.

Evidence from another session (2026-11-08, landing c0273 at load 58 to 99): control-cli.spec's late-push case (line 587) failed when its 10 s poll for `late.lua` on the fixture origin ran out. The other fixed-delay waits in c0117's specs outside dispatch-cli: control-cli `absentAfter` at line 648 and polls at lines 415 and 582, and `trackerstate` line 354. Each is in scope here: every wait in c0117's specs outside dispatch-cli (c0279's file) proves its event by the event itself on a bounded wall clock, and stays green on a loaded machine.

## Non-goals
- No change to the dispatcher (`z-nile/engine/`), the supervisor, the publish broker or any `stall_seconds` default: the cases and their fakes are what wait wrongly, not the code they test. The prototype changes no engine file.
- `z-nile/tests/acceptance/c0117/dispatch-cli.spec.luau` is c0279's (done) and stays as it is; its `deadWithin` keeps its own copy (c0279's AC3 pins that file whole). The fake modes c0279 pins (`stop`, `cap`, `push`, `tool` and the first line) are untouched (AC1 runs c0279's `pins.sh`).
- Specs outside c0117's folder that share the fake (`tests/tooling/dispatch-guards.spec.luau`, `tests/tooling/repo-fence.spec.luau`) keep their own limits (dispatch-guards' root `stall_seconds = 3`): they only have to stay green (E7).
- The dispatcher's own `unlock` closes its lock holder's stdin and does not wait for the holder to exit, so a run that starts the instant another ends could, in principle, find the lock still held. That is engine behaviour shared by every dispatcher spec (a holder needs one scheduling slice; the next `./zn` needs a whole bash and lune start), not a wait in these specs, and no load run here or in c0279's 8 shows it.
- A dispatched step's variables reach the specs' fixtures (trackerstate's broker cases are red in a dispatched step on `main`, E1b): d0355's (and d0298's runner change). AC2 runs without them and says so.

## Solution
(captured idea: control-cli proves a process's death by the process, c0279's `deadWithin`, `kill -0` polled on a bounded wall clock, never by a file's absence after a fixed sleep; every wait in c0117's specs outside dispatch-cli proves its event by the event itself on a bounded wall clock, and stays green on a loaded machine.) The census (E0) finds nine waits in `control-cli.spec.luau` and `trackerstate.spec.luau`, and none in c0117's other six specs. Every one becomes a wait on its event through one helper, `within(seconds, event)`, bounded on the wall clock (`os.time`) by a limit only a missing event reaches (**(prep)**: 60 s, and 600 s for the concurrent runs); the fakes behind them hold on markers, never on sleeps; and the stall limits become ones only a real stall reaches, as c0279 did for dispatch-cli:
1. **Stall limits** **(prep)**. The root `stall_seconds = 3` becomes 120 (`tool_seconds = 240`, c0279's values for dispatch-cli); the two issues whose cases test a stall keep short limits of 10 s, the limit the user chose for c0279's stall cases ("Widen them to 10 s", c0279, 2026-11-08): c0095 `tool_seconds = 10`, `stall_seconds = 10` (was 8 and 8), c0097 `tool_seconds = 30`, `stall_seconds = 10` (was 24 and 10).
2. **The orphan's death by its pid**, and the locks the next runs need by the locks themselves.
3. **The late push held on a marker** the spec writes once the step's run has returned, then waited for on the origin.
4. **The authors' keepalive** until the nested review's CLI has started, so the silence the case tests is the review's, not the nested `./zn`'s start.
5. **The two pollers** (the concurrent runs, the two brokers) bounded on the wall clock.

Why this holds under any load: no case waits for a clock to prove anything. Every wait ends the moment its event happens (the pid dead, the lock free, the commit on origin, the runs done) and fails only after a bound on the wall clock (`os.time`, so the polling commands' own cost is inside it) that only a missing event reaches: 60 s against events of a few seconds (E5: 2.06 s for the supervisor's grace, the slowest), 600 s for two runs that take about 21 s at load 12 (E9; 240 s summed before). The fakes hold on markers (the pid file, `late-go`, `started.zz-review`) and their holds are an hour (`sleep 3600`) or bounded polls that load only lengthens. The stall limits: 120 s is a silence no slow fake reaches; the 10 s limits of the stall cases now cover only the silence the case is about (the review's), which the review's beats break every second; the keepalive covers the nested start, which load stretches, by construction (E3: a 12 s start, over the 10 s limit, passes).

`dead` reads a pid that the system reused for another process as alive: a rare false red after 60 s, never a false green; the grandchild's hour-long sleep and the immediate kill make a reuse inside the case's seconds implausible (c0279's reasoning).

### Scalability
The waits end with their events, so the file does not get slower: the orphan case no longer waits out `absentAfter`'s 5 s (it ends about 2 s after the kill, when the supervisor lets `run.lock` go); c0095's quiet sub-case waits 2 s longer (its reviewer stalls at 10 s, not 8). control-cli took 214 s in the prototype's plain run, started at load 42 (E2), against 827 s recorded in `tests/slow.tsv` (a loaded gate) and 905 s in the capture. AC2's run (every fake 3 s slower, each review's start 12 s slower) took 365 s against E2's 214 s (E3): about 150 s more per check run, the check's alone. A broken implementation costs up to 60 s per wait (600 s for the concurrent case), once, where it used to cost 3 to 10 s.
### Maintainability
One rule for the next case: wait through `within(seconds, event)` on the event itself, with a bound only a missing event reaches; a fake that must outlive a wait holds an hour or on a marker; a case that tests a stall says so with its own short limit in its issue's `.models.toml` (c0095, c0097), and the root's limit is one no slow step reaches. `FAKE_DELAY` (c0279) and `FAKE_START_DELAY` are the knobs that prove load insensitivity, and only a check sets them.
### Usability
A broken lifeline fails at the orphan case's `dead` line (`expected true, got false`, 60 s later); a stuck lock at its `free` line; a late push that never lands at the `within` line; an author that loses the review's beats as `expected "ok", got "stalled"` (M7).
