# c0435: Slim design for easy tickets

## Problem
- Every ticket pays the same fixed drafting work before its risk is known (every design section, acceptance specs, experiments and a size record), and that minimum is high even for a small change: on 2026-11-21 the design of d0328, a three-line fix, cost about 195,000 tokens, 16 minutes and two review rounds on a cheap model.
- Design review is about 43% and design about 25% of agent time, and the cost comes from what the design skill demands, not from the model.
- A ticket is marked easy (`risk: easy`) only once its draft is complete (`z-nile/skills/z-design/SKILL.md:59`), so no ticket is known to be easy when its design starts and the drafting work cannot scale with its risk.
- What already scales with risk comes after the draft: the review round caps for design and for code (2, 5 or 9, `[rounds]`, `z-nile/skills/zz-rules/SKILL.md:185`), and the light path, where `./zn ready <issue>` makes an easy ticket's single raft with no rafting step, the separate code review is skipped and the final review is its only code review (`z-nile/skills/z-ready/SKILL.md:8`, `z-nile/skills/zz-execute/SKILL.md:62`, `z-nile/skills/zz-review/SKILL.md:137`).
- An easy ticket on the light path therefore still gets two reviews, a separate design review of its full draft and then the final review that covers its code.

## Non-goals
- It does not change the design path for a ticket marked normal or risky.
- It does not change which model runs any activity.
- It does not change the rules for what counts as easy, normal or risky, only when the mark is set.
- It does not change the light path's conditions, its skipped code review or any round cap.
- It does not add a review to any ticket.
- It does not remove the ticket, the full gate, the final review or the owner's approval for any ticket.

## Solution
- The designer sets a ticket's `risk:` as the first step of design, before writing any of it, and the owner can still overrule it by editing the ticket.
- A ticket marked easy at that point gets a short note as its design: the problem, the change, the files, the one test that proves it, and what could go wrong.
- An easy ticket's test is written with the code, still before the code it tests, with no separate acceptance script beforehand.
- An easy ticket's design has no experiments section and no size record.
- An easy ticket with a short note has no separate design review, and takes the existing light path unchanged in its conditions (`z-nile/skills/z-ready/SKILL.md:8`).
- The light path's final review is that ticket's one review: it adds the short note to what it already checks (the trace and the code), so it replaces the design review and adds no review.
- That final review keeps its model (`final-review`) and the code round cap for an easy ticket (2).
- An easy ticket that does not meet the light path's conditions (an unlanded dependency, an `Overrides:` entry, or membership of a raft with other tickets) takes the full design path.
- The final review of an easy ticket may raise its level, which sends the ticket through the full design path, design review included, before it can merge.
- Every ticket keeps the full gate before merge, the final review on the best model, and the owner's approval of every guess.
- Approved by the owner (2026-11-22): after the lead walked through the proposal (a short design note, the test written with the code, no experiments section or size record, one review covering design and code, and a review that may raise the level and send the ticket through the full path), offering "approve", "approve but keep two reviews" or "reject", the owner answered: "I like easy path". The lead reads that as approval of the proposal as walked through, with the one combined review.

**The easy path, step by step, as main does it.**
1. The designer sets `risk: easy` FIRST (z-design, Risk; z-idea proposes it at capture), then writes, under `## Design`, one `### Short note` (level 3 or deeper) with five labelled lines: `Problem:`, `Change:`, `Files:` (backticked paths), `Test:` (one backticked path ending in `.spec.luau`: the engine refuses a command, though z-design's paragraph still says "the one spec or command", see Known differences) and `Risk:`. The ticket's `## Acceptance criteria` holds one `AC1` line and its `check.sh` one `check "AC1: ..."` line that names the note's `Test:` path. That is the whole design: no experiments, no `size:`, no acceptance folder, no design review.
2. The test is written with the code, still red first: the first commit holds the failing spec under the project's `tests/`, the second the code.
3. `./zn status <id> prepared`, `./zn ready <issue>`, the build, `./zn finish`, as for any light ticket.
4. ONE review, `final-review-NNN.md`, on the activity `final-review` (`./zn model final-review --for <R-id>`; since f0421's ruling of 2026-11-24 another model than the builder's is preferred, not required, though the walk's own sentence still says "Run it on a model other than the one that built it", see Known differences). In its Light path item it also judges the short note and the design beside the code.
5. The final review may say the ticket is not easy by a line `Level: normal` or `Level: risky`. The walk then answers `release` naming `./zn stop <R-id>`, `done` refuses, and `./zn stop` publishes the raise (risk raised, `design: draft`, no raft, the raft emptied); the ticket goes through z-design in full, z-raft and `ready`. Only a final review's line does this (`lightgit.raisedOf` reads the top layer's `final-review-NNN.md` files). A `Level:` line in a design review makes the raft not light, so it lapses to the code review (next paragraph), and in a normal or risky ticket's design review it raises nothing: `light.reason` is not asked about those tickets.

**When a light raft stops being light after it is built (design-review-001 F2).** A raft readied on the light path whose built diff passes the 150-line bound, or touches a protected file or a promoted spec, is no longer light. The walk then asks for "code review round 1" (`work.luau:472-485`), `done` asks for a `review-NNN.md` (`main.luau:5557`), and the final review is no longer told to judge the short note (`work.luau:350-352`, zz-review Final mode item 4 applies only while the raft "is still light"). `ready`'s demand for a design review reads the raft's own `light: true` line (`readyfacts.luau:134`, `workread.luau:612`), so it stays met. So such a ticket gets a code review and a final review, keeps five lines as its whole design, and no review is told to read them: Solution bullet 8 ("takes the full design path") does not hold for it, and bullet 6 only while it stays light. The owner rules (Open to the human, F2); a fix in the walk or `done` is an engine change outside this closing.

**The short note (current).** `easy.note(doc, check)` (pure, `z-nile/engine/easy.luau`) returns `(found, problem)`. Found when a heading titled `Short note` at level 3 or deeper sits under `## Design` (outside fenced code). The problem names the first of: a part given twice; a missing or empty part (Problem, Change, Files, Test, Risk); a `Files:` or `Test:` line with no backticked token; a `Test:` token that does not end in `.spec.luau`; when `check` holds a `check "` line, no check line whose command names that path as a word (`easy.runsPath`: after a space, quote, `=` or `/`, before a space, quote or the end, not in a trailing comment; any command counts, `test -f <path>` as well as `tests/run`); no `AC1` line under `## Acceptance criteria` (`easy.hasAc1`). Parts may carry a list marker and `**` emphasis.

**Raising (current), and what remembers it (design-review-001 F6).** `easy.raised(reviews)` reads candidate `Level:` lines (a line that is `Level:` once heading marks, quote marks, list markers, backticks and `**` are stripped; fenced code skipped; the first word after the colon is the value). The highest of `normal` and `risky` wins; `easy` raises nothing; any other value is a problem on which `ready`, the walk, `done` and `stop` refuse. Which line is read where: `ready` and `lightready` read the ticket's folder (design and final reviews); `lightgit.reason` reads the design reviews on origin/main and the raft's final reviews; `lightgit.raisedOf` (the walk's `release` and `stop`) reads the top layer's final reviews only. What remembers a raise: a design review's `Level:` line stays in the ticket's folder on main, so a later `risk: easy` is still refused. A final review's line lives on the layer branch; after `./zn stop` main holds only the raised `risk:` and `design: draft`, and setting `risk: easy` and `design: ready` again restores the approved contract exactly (a non-normal `risk:` is the only frontmatter the contract hashes, so the old approval holds again) and lets `./zn ready <issue>` make a new light raft whose folder has no `Level:` line. So "raising is one way" holds for a design review's line, and for a final review's only by the skill's "never lower it". The owner rules (Open to the human, F6); remembering it on main is an engine change outside this closing.

### Scalability

### Usability
An easy ticket's design is five lines; the refusal says which part is missing, or that the review says it is not easy and what to do next.
