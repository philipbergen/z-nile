### F2 [high] `./zn stop` on a raft of several tickets takes the raised ticket out but leaves its built layer in the stack the rest of the raft lands
Where: Design item 10 ("`./zn stop <R-id>` ... takes every raised ticket out of the raft in one published move ... the others stay in the raft, an emptied raft is given back as today"); `main.luau:7265-7290`; `lightgit.raisedOf` (`lightgit.luau:176-200`, which reads `layergit.layersOf(raft.id)`)
Evidence: today the raise path runs only for `#ids == 1`. It moves the raft and its one ticket to the backlog, so no built layer outlives it. Item 10 makes `stop` loop over the raised tickets and keep the others "in the raft". It does not say whether that raft stays active or goes back to the backlog, or what happens to the raised ticket's layer branch. Take raft R with layers `R/1/c0010`, `R/2/c0011` and `R/3/c0012`, stacked in that order, and a final review that raises c0011. After the stop, c0011 is `design: draft` with an empty `raft:`. If R stays active, `layergit.layersOf(R)` still lists `R/2/c0011`, and `R/3/c0012` is built on it. `git diff origin/main...HEAD` then still contains c0011's code. `done` would land code for a ticket that is back in design, or `trace --check` would fail on source files no claim covers. Nothing says which. The prototype does not hold this code, and no row covers it.
Critique: this is a consequence of round 1 F4's decision ("how `stop` takes one ticket out of a raft of several"). The fix answered the front-matter edits, not the built work, so an executor cannot deliver it without asking (item 8, Executability). The tempting fix is "restack R without the raised layer". That is stack surgery. It breaks every later layer that touches the same lines, and a member that `depends_on` the raised ticket cannot stay.
Consequences: whichever rule is chosen decides what happens to the remaining members' built code. Here is the rule that holds without surgery. `stop` gives the whole raft back to the backlog, as today. Its members other than the raised tickets stay listed and `prepared`. The raised tickets leave with the raise published. The branches stay as `z-stop` already leaves them (named, untouched). The raft is readied and built again. That costs a rebuild of the unraised members. A member that `depends_on` a raised ticket then fails `ready` until the raised one lands, which is today's dependency rule.
Recommendation: decide the rule in item 10 in one sentence, for example the give-back above, and record it as a guess in `guesses.md`, because it decides what happens to built work. Fix the walk text (F1) to say what follows. Add the stop case to the Test plan (`tests/tooling/light-path.spec`, IO: a three-layer fixture raft raising its middle ticket; after `stop` the raft is in the backlog with two members, the raised ticket has `risk: risky`, `design: draft` and `raft:` empty, and no layer branch is deleted). Add a Rules and mutants row, "stop keeps the raft active with the raised layer in its stack".
