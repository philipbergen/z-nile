### F1 [high] Giving a raft of several tickets back and building it again re-reads the old final review and the old layers, so the rebuilt raft stops for good or lands the raised ticket's code
Where: Design item 10 ("`./zn stop <R-id>` gives the **whole raft back to the backlog** ... no layer branch is deleted or restacked (they stay named and untouched, as `z-stop` leaves them); the raft is readied and built again"); `guesses.md` G2; the walk text in `experiments/prototype.patch` ("the other members stay prepared. Then ask again"); `workread.luau:409-437`; `easy.reviewTexts` (`easy.luau:187`); `layergit.layersOf` (`layergit.luau:226-246`); `z-nile/skills/z-stop/SKILL.md` step 4
Evidence: take raft R with layers `R/1/c0010`, `R/2/c0011` and `R/3/c0012`, and a `final-review-001.md` on the stack tip that says `Level: c0011 risky`. `stop` publishes the raise and gives R back with members c0010 and c0012. The branches stay. z-stop step 4 offers two ways to ready R again. The first is to keep the branches and run `start` on them. Then `layergit.layersOf(R)` matches layers by raft id, not by membership, so it still lists `R/2/c0011` (and a new `R/2/c0012` takes the same position, so `byPos` keeps whichever ref sorts last). The raft folder at the tip still holds `final-review-001.md`. `workread` passes every `final-review-NNN.md` in `sides` to the raise reader. With item 10's `raisedBy(reviews, { "c0010", "c0012" })`, the line `Level: c0011 risky` is "no ticket c0011 in the raft", a `levelProblem`. The walk then answers `none` with "fix the review's Level line" forever, on a review file that is never edited. The second way is to start fresh: the human closes the PR and deletes the branches. No walk answer, guess or Implementation step tells anyone to do that, and the walk text only says "Then ask again". The old `trace.md` and the final-review round count (`finalRound`, capped by `codeMax`) carry over on kept branches too, so a rebuilt raft can start its one review already at its maximum.
Critique: this rule came from round 2's own Recommendation ("the branches stay as `z-stop` already leaves them. The raft is readied and built again"), which did not follow the re-ready step through. Today's single-ticket path never meets the problem, because the raft it gives back is emptied and never built again. The tempting fix is to filter `Level:` lines to the current members. That hides the stale line, but the old c0011 layer and the stale trace and round count stay in the stack.
Consequences: any rule that keeps the raft id has to say which old final reviews, trace and layer branches count after a re-ready. That means a review "epoch" for the readers, plus a refusal or a deletion of old layer branches, and the tooling may not delete branches (z-stop: "Never close, delete or remove them yourself"). A rule that empties the raft avoids all of that. Every member leaves, as on today's single-ticket path. The unraised members are `prepared` with `raft:` empty, so `./zn work next` answers `rafting` for them, and z-raft groups them into a new raft with a new id: no old branches, reviews, trace or positions. The cost is the same rebuild plus one z-raft pass. A member that `depends_on` a raised ticket waits under today's dependency rule.
Recommendation: in item 10, `stop` on a raise empties the raft in one published move, for one ticket or several. The raft gets `issues: []` and goes back to the backlog. Each raised ticket gets the raised `risk:`, `design: draft` and `raft:` empty. Each other member stays `prepared` with `raft:` empty. Rewrite G2 and the walk's release text to match ("the other members leave the raft and are rafted again"). In the Test plan's `light-path.spec` IO case, assert that after `stop` the raft lists no issue, c0010 and c0012 are `prepared` with `raft:` empty, c0011 is risky, `draft` and has `raft:` empty, and no branch is deleted. Add a pure case to `walk.spec`: `work.decide` with those two sized, unrafted prepared tickets answers `rafting`. Add a Rules row with the mutant "stop keeps the unraised members in the old raft".

### F3 [medium] A `Level:` line at or below a ticket's own risk counts as a raise, and the brief tells the reviewer to write one
Where: Design item 10 ("Per ticket the highest of normal and risky wins; easy raises nothing"); `easy.raisedBy` in `experiments/prototype.patch`; `work.finalBrief` in the prototype ("if the ticket needs more than it has (an easy ticket that is not easy, a ticket that needs a design review), say so with a line Level: normal or Level: risky")
Evidence: once the raise is read for every raft (round 2 F1's change), a raft's members can be `normal` already. Take normal ticket c0011, whose designer skipped the now-optional review, and a final reviewer who follows the brief for "a ticket that needs a design review" and writes `Level: c0011 normal`. `raisedBy` returns `{ c0011 = "normal" }`, because it never compares against the ticket's own `risk:`. The walk answers `release`, `stop` gives the whole raft back (F1) and sends c0011 to `design: draft`. Under item 10's own rule, a ticket at `normal` needs no design review, so c0011 comes back unchanged. The result is a full rebuild for a raise that raised nothing. A ticket that needs a design review needs `risky` (z-design step 5 as revised), and the brief's "Level: normal" wording points the reviewer the wrong way.
Critique: on the light path the ticket was always easy, so any `normal` or `risky` was a real raise. That stopped being true when the raise was extended to every raft. The tempting fix is to drop "a ticket that needs a design review" from the brief. That still leaves `raisedBy` counting a same-level line, and a reviewer can still write one.
Consequences: the reader needs each member's current level, which `workread` already holds (`levels`, `workread.luau:404`). A line at or below the current level raises nothing. It is better not to count it as a problem either, because a reviewer confirming a level is harmless.
Recommendation: make `easy.raisedBy` take `levels: { [string]: string }` (ticket to current risk) instead of `ids`, keep a raise only when its rank is above the ticket's level, and keep the `#ids == 1` bare form. Reword the brief: "a ticket that needs a design review: `Level: <ticket> risky`; an easy ticket that is not easy: `Level: <ticket> normal` or `risky`". Add AC8 cases: `Level: c0011 normal` with c0011 at normal gives `{}`, and `Level: c0011 risky` with c0011 at normal gives `{ c0011 = "risky" }`. Add a Rules row with the mutant "a raise to the ticket's own level counted", and run it on the prototype.
