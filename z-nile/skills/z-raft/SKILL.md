# Rafting

A raft is what `z-ready` hands out and `z-run` delivers as one PR. `z-design` designs one issue at a time and stops at a prepared issue with no raft; this skill groups prepared issues into rafts. Read the zz-rules skill first (Sync and claim, **Rafting**).

**The lock.** An issue's raft is its own `raft:` line. This skill writes it in its worktree, and the rafting PR carries it, so two PRs that raft the same issue edit the same line and the second cannot merge cleanly. The published claim (`preparing-…`) stamps the raft only: a prepared issue is never restamped.

## 1. Plan
Take `./zn lost` first: lost work comes before anything not yet started, and a lost rafting claim is taken over by `./zn status <R-id> preparing` (its members following; continue from the branch the old rafting worktree pushed, else start fresh). Work in a rafting worktree: `cd "$(./zn worktree raft-<slug>)"` (any short slug, for example `raft-backlog`), then `./zn sync`.
- **No argument**: run `./zn raft-plan`. It prints the plan as commands, one per line, and `skip <id>: <why>` for an issue it can't place. The policy is fixed and needs no judgement:
  - one type per raft and one project per raft; a raft that is not prepared in the backlog (new, being prepared, active or done) is never pulled in, and a `single: true` raft never takes another issue;
  - defects fold into one open prepared defect raft (the lowest id that still fits), and chores fold the same way;
  - features group only when they share a surface: a Touched path of one overlaps a Touched path of another (or of an open feature raft's member), taken as connected groups; an issue with no Touched modules shares nothing;
  - a raft that would pass about 1,500 changed lines (the sum of its members' `size:`) starts another; an issue estimated over that is a raft by itself and is never split;
  - an open raft with a member that has no `size:` takes nothing, because the cap can't be kept: the plan prints `skip <R-id>: <member> has no size:, …` and opens another raft instead (with an argument you may still `fold` into it);
  - a `solo: true` issue is always a raft by itself (it records a user decision, such as sequenced rafts), and nothing is ever folded into a raft that holds one;
  - an issue with no `size:` is skipped and named: send it back to z-design.
- **With arguments, it does what was asked**: which issues and rafts are grouped is the caller's choice, overriding the fold and surface rules and the cap. The tracker's rules always hold, because the commands enforce them: one type, backlog only, one project, and solo issues alone. So `z-raft c0001 c0002` is `./zn new raft <type> <slug> c0001 c0002 --project <name>`, `z-raft R0010 c0003` is `./zn fold R0010 c0003`, and `z-raft recombine R0010 R0020 [<issue>…]` is `./zn recombine R0010 R0020 [<issue>…]`.

## 2. Claim and group
Keep your claim alive while you work: start `./zn run --time 3600 -- ./zn beat <id> --watch` in the background (it beats every 5 minutes and stops by itself; start it again when it reports its bound), or run `./zn beat <id>` yourself at least every 15 minutes.
Run the plan's commands in order (`./zn raft-plan --apply` runs them all), or the ones the arguments name:
- `./zn new raft <type> <slug> <ids…> [--single] --project <name>` makes a raft of prepared issues: it publishes the raft with a fresh `preparing-…` stamp, leaves the issues' status as it is, and writes their `raft:` here.
- `./zn fold <R-id> <ids…>` claims a prepared raft (its status only, published) and writes the issues' `raft:` and the raft's `issues:` here.
- `./zn unfold <R-id> <ids…>` takes members out of a raft (claiming it as `fold` does): their `raft:` is emptied and they leave `issues:`. It is how a member goes back to z-design, during a rafting or after it landed. When `<R-id>` is retired or missing (audit's `dead-raft` error), `unfold` claims nothing and only clears the members' `raft:`; it refuses a member in `active/` (give it back first with `./zn stop <id>`).
- `./zn recombine <keeper> <other> [<ids>…]` moves the listed members of the other raft, or all of them, into the keeper. It claims both rafts with one stamp, and retires the other only when it ends up empty: its folder moves to `<root>/rafts/retired/<R-id>` in this branch, with `retired: <keeper>`, so its design reviews, refutes and `harvest.md` stay where `lessons` and `metrics` read them.
Each command commits its own edits in this worktree and remembers its claim in `.cache/tracker-claim-<R-id>`. `new raft`, `fold` and `recombine` also record in the raft's `grouped:` line a hash of each prepared member's design as it was when grouped (its document without `status:` and `raft:`, its `check.sh`, its `acceptance/` specs, and its design reviews, refutes and `harvest.md`; not `spend.md`), so a member redesigned while the rafting is open is noticed (§5).

## 3. Write each raft's design
In each raft document this branch changes (`R<NNNN>.md`):
- **Theme**: why these issues belong together: the member titles and the surface they share (their common Touched paths), and the user decisions their designs quote.
- **Whole-raft design**: the interfaces between the members: for each module two members both touch, the public types and signatures each one adds or changes there, copied from their designs, and whether they agree.
- **Order & dependencies**: the members in `depends_on` order, then by id, and any order the interfaces force.
- **Risks & rollback**, and the raft's `check.sh` RCn checks after the generated RC0 lines (RC0 runs the raft's project's gate, `--project <project>`) (a raft check finds its members with `./zn find <id>` or `members`, never a hard-coded path).
Then set the raft's `design: ready`. z-raft sets a raft's `design:`; z-design sets an issue's.

## 4. Coherence review (a raft of several issues)
A raft that groups two or more issues gets one short, raft-only coherence review before it is prepared, always for features that share a surface, whenever this rafting changed its record (a raft that only retired an orphan, kept the rest of a partial recombine, or unfolded members needs none). Start a clean reviewer with the zz-review skill in **raft mode** on the **raft role** `.models.toml` names (as z-design step 5.2 says: `./zn dispatch zz-review <R-id> --mode raft`). That role names no author: the owner's table gives the review the same Opus as the raft, and naming the author would drop it. It writes `design-review-NNN.md` in the raft's folder. A dispatched reviewer writes `review.md` in its state folder instead, and the dispatcher places it there. Answer every finding once, in the raft document or in `design-refute-NNN.md`, and harvest it into the raft's `harvest.md` (z-design step 5.3), then `./zn lessons --uncovered <R-id>` and `./zn lessons --check`. A finding whose fix needs a member's own design to change takes that member out of the raft instead (`./zn unfold <R-id> <id>`) and sends it back to z-design; this skill never edits a member's design. One challenge of this record, then the human (z-design, The design review, step 4, in the raft document): anything you refute, or any high you could not close, goes under `## Open to the human`. There is no second reviewer. A later fold that changes the grouped record is a new record and needs its own one review; an `unfold` alone needs none. The reviewer quotes the record it read (`Grouped: [...]`), a review file is never edited after it is written, and the carried move and `ready` refuse a raft of several issues whose reviews quote no record covering the current one.
As soon as a subagent reviewer returns, `./zn spend <R-id> --phase design-review --note design-review-NNN` (it shares the row among the members this branch gives the raft; a review started with `./zn dispatch zz-review` records its own row). Mark your own answering span with `./zn spend <R-id> --phase design-review --begin` when you start and `./zn spend <R-id> --phase design-review` when you stop. Do not pass `--time` or `--tokens`.

## 5. Prepare and land
For each raft, `./zn status <R-id> prepared`: it is carried by the PR (it publishes nothing), takes each member that is prepared on `origin/main` as it is, and refuses a member that another raft holds there (unless that raft carries this raft's own claim, as recombine's other raft does), a raft with no Theme or Order, and a raft of several issues whose coherence reviews quote no `Grouped:` record covering its current one (§4: a raft whose record this rafting didn't change needs no new review). A recombined other raft that keeps members is prepared the same way. Then commit (`tracker: raft <R-ids> <summary>`), and follow Landing a change (zz-rules skill): `./zn sync`, the gate, push, PR, merge. Ask the user before merging a rafting PR they didn't ask for. Under the dispatcher, don't ask and don't merge: once the PR is open and your spend is recorded, end the step; the dispatcher passes the gate (the user started it).

**Redesigned members.** A member whose document changed on `origin/main` since this raft grouped it (a design claimed and landed inside the rafting window) makes the carried move refuse ("<id> (<recorded> → <now>): changed on origin/main since <R-id> grouped it"), and `./zn ready` refuses the landed raft the same way: its coherence review read the old design. Drop it with `./zn unfold <R-id> <id>`; to keep it, fold it again (a new record) and rerun the coherence review (§4): the engine refuses the raft until a review quotes the new record.

**Conflicts.** A conflict on an issue's `raft:` line when you sync means another rafting PR landed first with that issue: take `main`'s line, drop the issue from your raft's `issues:` and Theme, and prepare again, or release and plan again. A conflict on `raft:` that is really an edit to `depends_on:` beside it is the same false alarm: take both edits. A member that another terminal re-claimed for design while you were rafting (its `status:` is someone else's `preparing-…`) makes `./zn status <R-id> prepared` refuse with "claimed by another terminal" (if it is your own claim and this checkout's claim file was cleared, the message says so: `./zn status <id> new`, then claim it again); a conflict on a member's `status:` line means the same: take `main`'s line, drop the member from your raft (its `raft:` back to empty, out of `issues:`), and prepare again. Never take your own side of a `status:` conflict.

**Giving up.** `./zn release <R-id>` gives a claim back in one published commit: every raft and issue that carries its `preparing-…` stamp goes back to `prepared` (its design is ready on `origin/main`) or `new`. It releases only the claim this checkout remembers (`--force` for one the user says is abandoned), and gives back the issue claims this checkout made for the raft's members too. Then remove the worktree. A raft made by `new raft` that is released stays on `origin/main` at `new` with no member (an orphan): `./zn audit` warns about it, and a later rafting PR retires it with `./zn recombine <any raft of its project and type> <orphan>`, which moves it to `rafts/retired/`.

## 6. Report
The rafts and their members, the plan's skips, the coherence findings and what changed because of them, and the next step: `/z-ready <R-id>`.

## Headless (under `./zn dispatch`)
When `ZN_DISPATCH=1` is set you run headless and unattended, with the agent's own GitHub credential, and nobody answers a question. Never decide what you would ask the user, and never guess a ruling. A scope change or a missing credential: write the questions to `$ZN_DISPATCH_DIR/questions.md`, one `## Q<n>` heading per question with the options and what each costs, and stop. The dispatcher stops the chain there until the user answers and runs `./zn dispatch retry <id>`. A refutation or an unclosed high is written under `## Open to the human` in the item's document and you stop without ruling on it: the dispatcher stops the chain on that unruled list until the user rules in the document and runs `./zn dispatch retry <id>`. `./zn land`, `status`, `spend` and every other `./zn` command that publishes tracker state are allowed: the dispatcher pushes that tracker state to `main` for you. Never `git push` to `main` and never merge a pull request: the dispatcher merges at its gates, your credential can't, and `z-nile/bin/git` and `gh` refuse. Record your spend as this skill says. Do not pass `--time` or `--tokens`: the dispatcher records the session's wall, CPU, tokens and model. Start every review with `./zn dispatch zz-review <id> --mode <design|raft|code|stream>` (zz-rules skill, Dispatch), and give that one command a two-hour timeout (in Claude Code, the Bash tool's `timeout` of 7200000 ms): a review takes minutes, and the dispatcher keeps it alive by its heartbeat.

## Lessons
Search with `./zn lessons <terms>` when you are about to do something a lesson might cover.

A new lesson (from a review finding, a correction by the user, or a step that failed in a way a rule would have prevented) is handled by your role, not by the skill you are reading:
- **A subagent** (a stream, a reviewer, any agent working from a brief) never writes a lesson file or runs `./zn new`. It puts the candidate (the rule, why, and the question that catches it) in its review or final report.
- **The lead of a prep or raft** writes it in `<skills folder>/<the skill the lesson improves>/lessons/<slug>.md` (the real folder: `z-nile/skills/` for z-nile's skills, `<project>/skills/` for a project's, never through a `.claude/skills/` link, which git can't add through; format: zz-rules skill, Items), and records where it came from in its own `harvest.md`: review findings through the harvest (z-design step 5.3, zz-execute Phase 4 step 5), and anything else (a subagent's candidate, a correction by the user, a failed step) as `- note <what happened>: <lesson ids>`. It adds a note to an existing lesson when one fits, writes a new file only when none does, and commits both with its work.
- **Any other lead** (in the main checkout, a capture, or a metrics report) captures it: `./zn new issue c lesson-<slug> --project z-nile --desc "<skill>: <the rule>, because <what happened>; check: <the question>"` (z-ticket skill). The prep that takes the issue writes the file.
