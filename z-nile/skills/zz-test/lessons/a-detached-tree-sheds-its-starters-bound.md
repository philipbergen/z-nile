---
title: A detached tree sheds its starter's bound at its root
when: both
tags: [detached, reaper, bounded]
check: Which of this detached tree's processes, between its root and the point that sets its own bound, still carry the starter's deadline, share its process group or hold its resource limits (`ulimit -t` set by `./zn run` and the jail: a hard limit cannot be raised from inside), and which credentials and wrapped tools (a dispatched step's bot token and git wrapper) does it inherit that its own work cannot use, and what do the reaper and the starter's `./zn run` do to each once the starter ends?
---
A process started to outlive its starter inherits the starter's whole environment, its process group, its resource limits, and its credentials and wrapped tools. `./zn run` kills its command's whole group when the command ends or its deadline passes, and the reaper stops anything past the deadline it carries. A detached helper that keeps either dies with its starter.

**Why:** d0383's watch net (R0138, 2026-11-19) was detached with `( … ) &`. Code review 002 F1 showed its outer wrapper kept the kicker's `ZN_BOUNDED_DEADLINE`, so the reaper's deadline rule would stop the whole net; fixing that showed the net also shared the kicker's process group, so a kick made under `./zn run` lost its net when that command ended. The fix starts the net in its own group (`setpgrp`) and clears the inherited deadline at the top of its script. Review 003 F1 found a third inheritance: `./zn run --time T` sets `ulimit -t T` (soft and hard), the net kept it, and its own `./zn run` cannot raise a hard limit. The kick now refuses to start a net under a hard CPU limit below the floor (lib/cpulimit) and raises a left-over soft limit in the script.

**How to apply:** Start the detached tree in its own process group and unset the starter's bound before anything else runs; give it its own `./zn run` bound inside. Add a case where the starter is a `./zn run` that ends, normally and by its deadline, while the detached work is held on a marker. Test the script's effect one level down, without `ps`: a stub `zn` in the kicked folder records the variable and the limits it sees. What cannot be observed here is the reaper's view (macOS `ps -E` shows no environment for system bash and perl, so the `unset` matters for a perl whose environment it shows): say so. A limit a detached tree cannot shed is refused at its start, not worked around. See zz-test/detached-helpers-meet-the-reaper and z-design/a-detached-process-does-not-live-in-its-starters-folder.

A detached tree also inherits its starter's credentials and wrapped tools: decide each by what the detached work must do, not by what the starter was allowed to do. The net pushes to main, which needs the user's credential, so a kick made inside a dispatched step (bot token, git wrapper that passes tracker state only) starts no net, and the work starts from the process that holds the authority it needs (the dispatcher after its own merge). Stripping the step's variables instead would hand an unattended agent's descendant the user's keychain.
